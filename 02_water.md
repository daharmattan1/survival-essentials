# Water: Find It, Treat It, Store It

Use this guide if your tap water is off, you are under a boil-water notice, you are outdoors with a questionable source, or you are planning ahead. If local officials give different advice, follow theirs.

## Start here

- **Boil:** rolling boil 1 minute. Boil 3 minutes if you are above 5,000 ft or not sure of your elevation [1][2][6][7]. See [Boiling](#boiling).
- **No stove:** plain unscented bleach, 8 drops of 6% bleach per gallon of clear water, then wait 30 minutes [2]. Cold water and Giardia need more. See [Bleach](#bleach).
- **Cloudy:** [clear it first](#step-zero-cloudy-water).
- **Never drink** fuel- or chemical-smelling water, floodwater, or water under a "do not drink" notice. Boiling does not fix them [1][5][6][10]. See [Never drink](#never-drink).
- **Also:** [Quick-reference card](#quick-reference-card) (everything on one screen) and [Dehydration](#dehydration-what-it-looks-like-what-to-do).

---

## Three dangers

1. **Not drinking enough.** See [How much water you need](#how-much-water-you-need).
2. **Germs.** Vomiting and diarrhea dehydrate you further. Germs include bacteria, viruses and protozoa (single-celled parasites). Giardia, a protozoan, can take 1 to 2 weeks to make you sick [21]. Cryptosporidium ("Crypto") is another and causes watery diarrhea [22].
3. **Chemicals.** No home method removes fuel or toxic chemicals [1][5].

---

## The water decision, in order

Go in order. Steps 1 and 2 can end the decision. Steps 3 to 5 build your treatment. Step 6 applies on every path. Looks and smell cannot tell you whether water has germs. Treat all surface water as contaminated [21].

1. **STOP check: is it unsafe?** Fuel or chemical smell, an oily sheen, scum like pea soup or green paint, floodwater, or a "do not drink" notice. If yes, find another source. Boiling and chemicals will not fix it [1][5][6][10][11]. See [Never drink](#never-drink).
2. **Is it sealed bottled water, or tap water your utility has said is fine?** Drink it. Sealed bottled water is the most reliable choice [4]. Tap water under a boil-water notice is not ready: go on to step 3 and treat it [6].
3. **DO FIRST if it is cloudy: clear it.** See [Step zero](#step-zero-cloudy-water) [1][2][18].
4. **CHOOSE ONE method.**
   - Boil it if you can. Boiling is the broadest single method [7]. See [Boiling](#boiling).
   - Cannot boil? This guide's ranking, not an agency rule: filter it, then add a disinfectant. Next, a disinfectant alone. Last, sunlight. CDC says to filter before you disinfect [7][18][1]. See [Filters](#filters), [Bleach](#bleach), [Sun and UV light](#sun-and-uv-light).
5. **ADD-ON if Crypto is possible** (livestock upstream, sewage, a camp nearby). Use boiling or a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller. Bleach, iodine and chlorine tablets do not reliably kill Crypto, so switch if step 4 gave you one of them alone [1][18][21][22].
6. **FINISH on every path: store it.** Cover it, keep it in a clean container, and drink on a schedule [4][16]. See [Storing water](#storing-water).

No way to treat water at all? See [If you cannot treat water](#if-you-cannot-treat-water).

## How much water you need

- **Store at least 1 gallon (about 4 liters) per person per day.** That covers drinking and sanitation. Keep at least 3 days; 2 weeks is better [3][4].
- A normally active person needs to drink at least 2 quarts a day. In extreme heat you can sweat up to 3.5 quarts an hour [14][21].
- Needs can double in very hot weather. Children, nursing mothers, pregnant people, sick people and pets need more [3][4].
- **Do not ration water you have.** Drink what you need today and look for more tomorrow [14]. Ration sweat instead: rest, work in the shade, cut effort [21].
- Working in heat: drink 1 cup (8 oz) every 15 to 20 minutes. Do not drink more than 48 oz (1.5 quarts) an hour. Too much plain water can drop your blood sodium and become a medical emergency [16].

## Where to get water

### At home, best first

- **Sealed bottled water** [4].
- **Water heater tank.** It holds roughly 30 to 80 gallons. A tankless heater holds none. Use only the faucet-side tank, never the separate heating-loop (radiator or boiler) water [15][5]. Steps are in [Draining a water heater](#draining-a-water-heater).
- **Pipes.** Shut the main valve, open the highest faucet, draw from the lowest [14][5].
- **Melted ice** made from uncontaminated water. During a boil notice, do not use ice from fridge or freezer dispensers [5][6][9].
- **Liquid from canned fruit and vegetables.** Water-packed is better than syrup [5][15]. Do not use liquid from damaged cans. An undamaged all-metal can that sat in floodwater can be used only after it is cleaned and sanitized; throw out food or drink in screw-cap, snap-lid, pull-top or crimped-cap containers that touched floodwater [24].
- **Toilet tank, last resort only.** Never the bowl. Only if the water is clear and the tank has no colored or chemical cleaner. Treat it before drinking [5][14]. Some agencies advise against it entirely [15].

### Outdoors, best first

1. A spring, at the point where it comes out of the ground.
2. Moving water, such as a stream.
3. Still water such as a pond. Avoid scum and anything near livestock, camps or roads [5][21].

Treat all of it. Only very high mountain streams and springs in uninhabited areas might be exceptions [5][21].

- **Snow and ice.** Do not eat them; that costs body heat. Melt them with outside heat and treat the result. They are no cleaner than the source [21].
- **Rain.** Catch it, then treat it before drinking [5]. Runoff from leaves, tarps or roofs picks up dirt. The Army advises letting rain wash leaves for about 15 minutes before collecting [21]. Letting the first rinse run off a tarp or roof follows the same idea (general practice).

## Never drink

- Anything that smells of fuel or chemicals, has an oily sheen, or is under a "do not drink" notice. Boiling does not help [1][5][6].
- Scum like pea soup or green paint (algal bloom). Boiling can raise its toxin level [11].
- Floodwater. It can hold sewage, livestock waste, chemicals and coal ash [10]. Treatment does not make it drinkable [15].
- Pool or spa water. Use it for flushing and washing only [5][9].
- Water from a radiator, boiler or water bed [1][5][15].
- Seawater. It is about 4% salt, and drinking it dehydrates you further [21][23].

## Step zero: cloudy water

Dirt blocks UV and sunlight, soaks up chlorine, and clogs filters [8][18].

1. Let it sit still for about an hour [18].
2. Pour or siphon off the clear top, leaving the sludge behind.
3. Pour that through a coffee filter, paper towel or tightly woven cloth [1][2][18].

This removes particles only. Treat it afterward. If it is still tea-colored, count it as cloudy and double the bleach dose (this guide's rule).

---

## Boiling

Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa, including Crypto and Giardia [7][2].

1. Clear the water if it is cloudy.
2. Heat it to a rolling boil: large bubbles that keep coming when you stir.
3. Start the clock. Boil for **1 minute**. Boil **3 minutes** if you are above **5,000 ft (about 1,500 m)** [2], or if you are not sure of your elevation (this guide's rule).
4. Let it cool on its own. Store it in a clean, covered container [1][2].

CDC uses 6,500 ft (2,000 m) for the 3-minute boil [1][6][7] and EPA uses 5,000 ft [2]. This guide uses the lower number.

**What goes wrong**

- The fire dies or the bubbles stop. Restart the clock.
- You pour boiled water into a dirty container [1][18].
- You skip boiling because you own a filter. During a boil notice, boil even if you filter [6].
- Fuel is short. A field shortcut: bring water to its first simmer, take it off the heat, and keep it covered for 30 minutes. This is less certain than a rolling boil. Use it only when you must [18][8].

**Taste.** Pour boiled water between clean containers, or add a pinch of salt per quart [2].

**Boiling does NOT handle**

- Heavy metals, salts, fuel and most chemicals [1][2].
- Nitrate. Boiling makes it worse: water evaporates, nitrate stays [12].
- Algal bloom toxins. Boiling can raise their level [11].

## Bleach

Household bleach works on bacteria and viruses in clear water [18][19]. Its weak spot is Crypto, which tolerates chlorine and can live more than 7 days [22].

**Which bleach.** Plain, unscented household bleach only. No scented, color-safe, or added-cleaner bleach [2][4]. Check the label for 6% or 8.25%. Use bleach stored at room temperature for less than a year; old bleach loses strength [2].

**Dose for clear water (drops from a clean dropper, such as a medicine eyedropper)** [2]:

| Water | 6% bleach | 8.25% bleach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 quart or 1 liter | 2 drops | 2 drops |
| 1 gallon | 8 drops | 6 drops |
| 2 gallons | 16 drops | 12 drops |
| 4 gallons | 1/3 teaspoon | 1/4 teaspoon |
| 8 gallons | 2/3 teaspoon | 1/2 teaspoon |

If you cannot tell the strength but the label says 5 to 9%, CDC uses 8 drops per gallon [1].

**Cloudy, colored or very cold: double the dose** [1][2].

**Steps**

1. Clear the water first if it is cloudy.
2. Add the drops. Stir or shake.
3. Wait **30 minutes** for general disinfection [2].
4. Giardia, a common parasite, needs a long wait: about 45 to 60 minutes in mild water, and about 3 hours near 41 F (5 C) [18]. If the water is cold and Giardia is possible, boil or filter instead (this guide's rule).
5. Smell it. It should have a faint chlorine smell.
6. No smell? Add the same dose again and wait 15 more minutes [2][9].
7. Still no smell? Use another bleach or another method.

Some older sheets say 16 drops per gallon. That is the doubled dose. Your bleach label and local health department come first [2].

**Bleach does NOT handle**

- Crypto [1][22].
- Chemicals, fuel or toxins.
- Water you did not clear first [8].

## Tablets and drops: iodine and chlorine dioxide

Follow the label; strength and wait time vary [1][2].

**Chlorine tablets and iodine do not kill Crypto** [1][7]. Check the label: "chlorine" tablets are not the same product as chlorine dioxide tablets, and only the label's dose and wait apply.

**Chlorine dioxide tablets**

- If Crypto is possible, boil or use a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller instead.
- If tablets are all you have, use them exactly as the label directs, with a long wait: one expert guideline says 2 to 4 hours, another source says "several hours" [1][18][8].
- The evidence is mixed. Two health agencies call it only "somewhat effective" or of "low to moderate effectiveness" [7][11].

**Iodine cautions**

- Do not use it if you are pregnant, have thyroid problems, or are allergic to iodine. The Army also flags shellfish allergy [1][7][21].
- Do not use it for more than a few weeks [1][7].
- Tincture of iodine (2%, the liquid form): 5 drops per quart or liter, 10 if the water is cloudy. Wait at least 30 minutes [2]. The Army says 60 minutes if the water is cold and cloudy [21].

**Taste.** A small pinch of vitamin C (ascorbic acid) added after the full wait removes the taste. Never add it to water you plan to store, because it cancels the disinfectant that protects it [18].

## Filters

A micron is a unit of filter hole size. The smaller the number, the smaller the germ caught. Read the label for **"absolute"**, not "nominal." Absolute means every hole is that size or smaller. Nominal means only most of them are [7][1][8].

- **Absolute 1 micron or smaller (or NSF 53/58 cyst-rated, a label for tested parasite removal):** removes parasites such as Crypto and Giardia. Not bacteria reliably. No viruses [7][11].
- **Absolute 0.3 micron or smaller:** removes bacteria and parasites. Not viruses [1][7].
- **Viruses:** only an ultrafilter (pores finer than 0.01 micron) or reverse osmosis (RO, a fine-membrane system) removes them [18][7].

**Use.** Near people, animals or sewage, viruses are a live question: filter first, then add a disinfectant. Remote water with little human or animal activity mainly carries Giardia and bacteria [1][8][18]. If a flood reached your tap supply or well, follow your utility's or health department's notice [10][15].

**What goes wrong.** Filters clog, so carry a backup. A cracked or frozen filter may let germs through. Clean it the way the maker says [18][21].

**Homemade filters** (sand, gravel, cloth, charcoal) clear particles and cut some germs, but do not make water ready to drink. Treat the result [18][21].

**Filters do NOT handle** chemicals or toxins. Activated carbon helps with some chemicals but does not kill germs [18].

## Sun and UV light

**Handheld UV pens** work on small amounts of clear water. Follow the device's instructions exactly. The water must be clear. UV does nothing about chemicals. It also leaves nothing in the water to protect it afterward [7][8][18].

**Sun (SODIS, solar disinfection)** is a fallback, not a plan [18][19].

1. Use a clean, clear plastic bottle and relatively clear water. Strain cloudy water first.
2. Fill the bottle three-quarters full. Shake it 20 times. Fill it the rest of the way and cap it.
3. Lay it on its side in strong sun for 6 hours. If the sky is cloudy, leave it 2 full days [19][1][8].

It fails with cloudy water, no sun, and chemicals.

---

## Distilling water

Distilling boils water and catches the steam [3].

1. Fill a pot about halfway.
2. Tie a cup to the lid handle so it hangs right side up inside the pot when the lid is upside down. It must not touch the water.
3. Boil for about 20 minutes.
4. The water that drips off the lid into the cup is distilled. It is cleaner than what went in [3].

Distilling does not make fuel- or chemical-contaminated water drinkable. Those chemicals boil off with the steam and end up in your cup [5].

**Seawater variant.** Boil it and hold a cloth over the container to catch the steam, then wring the cloth out [21].

## What each method handles

| Method | Bacteria | Viruses | Giardia | Crypto | Chemicals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rolling boil | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Bleach, clear water | Yes | Yes | Slow (a) | No | No |
| Iodine | Yes | Yes | Slow (a) | No | No |
| Chlorine dioxide | Yes | Yes | Yes | Unsure (b) | No |
| Filter, absolute 1 micron | Not reliably | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| Filter, absolute 0.3 micron | Yes | No | Yes | Yes | No |
| RO or ultrafilter | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Salt only (c) |
| UV pen, clear water | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No |
| Sun (SODIS) | Yes | Yes | Not stated (d) | Do not rely (d) | No |
| Distillation | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Some (e) |

- (a) Slow: needs a long wait, longer in cold water. Bleach waits are in [Bleach](#bleach) [18].
- (b) Slow and uncertain. See [Tablets and drops](#tablets-and-drops-iodine-and-chlorine-dioxide) [1][18][8][7][11].
- (c) RO removes salt. If other chemicals are possible, pick a different source.
- (d) Sun: no Giardia figure given here. Do not count on it for Crypto.
- (e) Distillation removes salt and heavy metals. It does NOT remove fuel, solvents or other chemicals that boil off with the steam [5].

Table sources: [1][3][7][8][11][18]. No single treatment covers everything.

## If you cannot treat water

This is a least-bad ladder. It is judgment, not an agency rule. Stop at the first rung that gets you clean enough water.

1. Pick the best source: the clearest, moving, away from livestock, sewage, roads and anything that smells of chemicals [5][21].
2. Settle and strain it ([Step zero](#step-zero-cloudy-water)). This cuts some germs only [18].
3. Use any partial treatment you have. Some beats none.
4. **Last resort:** drink untreated water only when dehydration is the more immediate threat and nothing else is left. Know the cost. Vomiting and diarrhea can start sooner than the 1 to 2 weeks Giardia takes, and they dehydrate you further [21]. If you get sick, sip rehydration drink (see [Dehydration](#dehydration-what-it-looks-like-what-to-do)) and seek care.

## Worked scenario A: day 2 of a boil-water notice at home

Four people. Tap runs; power and stove work; the notice is still on. These steps follow CDC's boil-water guidance [6], except the lines marked general practice and the 5,000 ft boil threshold (this guide's lower number).

- **Water budget.** At least 1 gallon per person, so 4 gallons a day [3][4]. Boil in batches (rolling boil 1 minute; 3 minutes if you are above 5,000 ft or not sure of your elevation). Cool in clean, covered containers. Boil even if you own a filter pitcher.
- **Use boiled or bottled water** for drinking, preparing and cooking food (including washing produce), brushing teeth, and pets' bowls [6].
- **Baby formula.** Breastfeeding is best, then ready-to-use formula [6]. If you must use powder, mix it with bottled water or water that was boiled and cooled (general practice).
- **Ice.** No ice from fridge or freezer dispensers [6]. Throw out ice made since the notice began (general practice).
- **Dishes.** Use disposable dishes, or a dishwasher with a sanitize cycle or a final rinse of at least 150 F (66 C). Otherwise wash by hand, then soak 1 minute in 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per gallon of water. Air-dry [6]. That is CDC's boil-notice dish soak. For general post-emergency sanitizing, CDC and USDA use a stronger 1 tablespoon per gallon (see [09_urban_survival.md](09_urban_survival.md)).
- **Bathing** is fine if nobody swallows the water. Sponge-bathe babies. Laundry is fine.
- **Day-2 trap.** Deciding the notice is over because the water "looks clear." Only local officials lift a notice.
- If the notice changes to "Do not drink," boiling will not help. Use bottled water only.

## Worked scenario B: a cloudy creek and only unscented bleach

You have no stove, no filter, and no fuel. You have 2 gallons of brown creek water, a bottle of 6% unscented bleach, and a dropper.

1. Fill a clean, covered container from moving water, away from scum, shoreline mud and animals [21].
2. Clear it ([Step zero](#step-zero-cloudy-water)) [18][1]. Still tea-colored? Treat it as colored (this guide's rule).
3. Dose. Clear water: 16 drops of 6% bleach for 2 gallons (12 drops if your bleach is 8.25%). Cloudy or colored: double it. That is **32 drops** (24 for 8.25%) [2].
4. Stir. Wait 30 minutes [2]. Giardia needs longer, and cold water needs boiling or filtering instead (see [Bleach](#bleach)). If you cannot boil or filter, wait the long Giardia time [18].
5. Smell it. A faint chlorine smell is the check. No smell: add the same dose again and wait 15 more minutes [2].
6. Bleach will not reliably stop Crypto. If livestock graze upstream or people are camped nearby, boil if you can find fuel, or use a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller [1][18][21][22].
7. Keep it in a clean, covered container [16].

## Draining a water heater

Use this when the power is out. The water is hot and may carry sediment [15].

1. **Cut power first.** Switch off the breaker. For gas, set the heater to pilot unless its label says otherwise [15].
2. **Close the cold-water inlet** valve on top, so mains water cannot flow in [15].
3. **Let air in.** Open a hot faucet in the house, or the relief valve [15][13].
4. **Open the drain** at the bottom into a container. A garden hose helps. The water can be very hot, so wear gloves and glasses [15][14].
5. **Never turn the power or gas back on while the tank is empty.** Wait until power is back and the tank has refilled [14].
6. Treat the water if there is any chance the mains were contaminated [15][9]. Treat it as a short-term source only, because metals can dissolve at heat [18].

## Storing water

- **Containers.** Use food-grade plastic. Otherwise choose a tight lid, unbreakable material (not glass) and a narrow neck. Never reuse containers that held bleach, pesticides or toxic chemicals. Avoid milk jugs; they are hard to clean and turn brittle [4][14][13].
- **Sanitize before filling.** Wash with soap, rinse, add 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart, shake, wait at least 30 seconds, pour out, and air-dry [4].
- **What to fill with.** Public-supply tap water needs nothing added [14][13]. Do not store raw well or surface water. Treat it first [14].
- **Label and place.** Write "drinking water" and the date. Store at 50 to 70 F, out of the sun, away from gasoline and pesticides [4].
- **Replace** home-stored water every 6 months. Keep bottled water sealed and follow its date. If a screw-cap, snap-lid or pull-top container of water may have touched floodwater, throw the water out and use other water [24]. Treatment does not purify water contaminated with fuel or toxic chemicals [13], and floodwater can carry them [10]. Clean and re-sanitize any container that touched floodwater before reuse [13].

## Getting water when there is none

These are low-yield. Do not sweat out more than you collect [21].

- **Dew.** Tie a cloth around your lower legs, walk through wet grass at dawn, wring it into a container. Yield: "up to a quart or more." Treat it [21].
- **Plants.** Seal a clear plastic bag over a leafy branch of a non-poisonous plant. Weight a corner, collect before sundown, reuse the same limb 3 to 5 days [21].
- **Below-ground still.** Dig a hole about 3 feet across and 24 inches deep. Put a container in the middle and cover with plastic held down with rocks. The Army warns that this is "time and energy intensive and may not be worth the return" [21].

## Dehydration: what it looks like, what to do

In the field, watch the signs [17].

- **Early:** strong thirst, dry mouth, less urine, dark urine, tiredness.
- **Getting worse:** dizziness, headache, fast heartbeat, very little urine.
- **Severe (an emergency):** confusion, fainting, no urine, rapid heartbeat and breathing, shock. Get help now.
- **Babies and small children:** no tears, no wet diaper for 3 hours or more, sunken eyes, unusual sleepiness.
- **Older adults** may not feel thirsty [17].

When you work or sweat, drink on a schedule. By the time you feel thirsty, you are already behind [16]. Mild cases may need only more water. Severe cases need medical care and often intravenous fluids [17].

**Rehydration drink for vomiting or diarrhea:** 1 liter of treated or boiled water, 6 teaspoons of sugar, and 1/2 teaspoon of salt. Sip small amounts often. Get care immediately if you cannot keep fluids down, or you are confused, drowsy, cramping or dizzy [20]. See [`06_first_aid.md`](06_first_aid.md).

Do not drink urine, alcohol or seawater [16][21][23].

## Quick-reference card

- **Store:** 1 gallon per person per day. At least 3 days; 2 weeks is better. More for heat, illness, pregnancy. Replace home-stored water every 6 months [3][4].
- **Never drink:** fuel or chemical smell, pool, spa, radiator or boiler water, floodwater, scum or bloom [5][10][11][15].
- **Clear first:** settle about 1 hour, strain through a coffee filter or tight cloth [18][1].
- **Boil:** rolling boil 1 minute. Boil 3 minutes if you are above 5,000 ft or not sure of your elevation. Cool, cover [1][2][6].
- **Bleach** (plain, unscented, under 1 year old), clear water [2]:
  - Per gallon: 8 drops of 6%, or 6 drops of 8.25%. Per quart or liter: 2 drops.
  - Cloudy, colored or very cold: double the dose [1][2].
  - Wait 30 minutes [2]. Giardia: about 45 to 60 minutes in mild water, about 3 hours near 41 F (5 C) [18].
  - Cold water and Giardia possible: boil or filter instead.
  - Faint chlorine smell means it worked. No smell: repeat the dose and wait 15 more minutes [2][9].
- **Filter:** look for "absolute." 1 micron = parasites. 0.3 micron = bacteria and parasites. Viruses need a disinfectant added [7][1].
- **Crypto:** boil or use a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller. Bleach and iodine do not reliably kill it [1][18].
- **Sun:** clear bottle, clear water, 6 hours sun or 2 days cloud [1][8].
- **Heat work:** 1 cup every 15 to 20 minutes. Never over 48 oz an hour [16].
- **Rehydration drink:** 1 liter treated water, 6 teaspoons sugar, 1/2 teaspoon salt [20].
- **Get help now:** confusion, fainting, no urine, cannot keep fluids down [17][20].
