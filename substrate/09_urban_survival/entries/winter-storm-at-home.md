# Winter Storm at Home With No Heat

**This is general first-aid and safety information from government and Red Cross pages. It is not medical advice. Sleepiness or confusion in a cold person means call 911.**

Cold kills indoors, too. A cold house can cause hypothermia, and the common mistake that follows is burning something inside to get warm, which can kill by carbon monoxide (CO). This entry covers the no-heat home. For the survey version, see [`09_urban_survival.md`](../../../09_urban_survival.md#cold). For the outage overview, see [Power Outage Bug-In](power-outage-bug-in.md). For field treatment of the cold-injured outdoors, see [Thermal Injury: Hypothermia and Hyperthermia](../../06_first_aid/entries/thermal-injury-hypo-hyperthermia.md) and [`06_first_aid.md`](../../../06_first_aid.md#cold-hypothermia-and-frostbite).

> ⚠ **HYPOTHERMIA AND CARBON MONOXIDE: the two ways a cold house kills.**
>
> Hypothermia is a body temperature below 95 F, and it needs medical attention immediately [3][4]. **Nothing burns inside a tent, vehicle, garage, snow shelter or home, except a vented fireplace or stove in working order.** No gas oven or stove for heat. Generators run outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows, doors and vents. A vent or cracked window is not a fix [10][12]. See [Generator and Heater CO Prevention](generator-and-heater-co-prevention.md).

---

## Recognize

### Hypothermia

Shivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech, drowsiness [1][3]. Slowed breathing or heartbeat and pale, cold skin can also show [8]. In babies, look for bright red, cold skin and very low energy [3]. Being indoors does not protect you: see [`03_fire.md`](../../../03_fire.md#when-you-need-this-guide-and-what-goes-wrong).

### Who is most at risk

- Older adults, especially without enough food, clothing or heating; babies in cold bedrooms [3].
- People who are chronically ill, take certain medicines, drink alcohol or use drugs [3][8].
- Chronic conditions and some medicines weaken the body's response to cold in older adults. For older adults, a home at 68 to 70 F or warmer is the aim, and even 60 to 65 F can lead to hypothermia [9].

### Frostbite

Redness or pain may be the first sign, then numbness. The skin can turn white or grayish-yellow, and feel firm or waxy [3][4]. The person may not feel it. Fingers, toes, ears and nose go first [4].

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

**Call 911 right away for any of these:**

- Sleepiness, confusion, slurred speech or fumbling hands in a cold person [1][3].
- A body temperature below 95 F [3][4].
- A glassy stare, a stiff body with rigid muscles, or slow or no breathing [6]. Shivering that has stopped in someone who is still cold is also a warning (general practice).
- Frostbite with blisters, black skin, or any sign of hypothermia [7].
- A CO alarm sounds, or anyone has headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain or confusion while fuel is burning [12]. Everyone goes outside first, then calls 911.

**Leave for a warming location when:** you cannot stay warm, someone shows hypothermia signs after 911 care is arranged, or a household member's powered medical device cannot run (this entry's rule; see [When to leave](../../../09_urban_survival.md#when-to-leave)). Go to a community location with power, and check with local officials about open heating locations [10].

## Respond

### A. Hold the heat (no power, no heating)

1. **Stay in one small room** (general practice). Close off unneeded rooms to avoid wasting heat [4][5].
2. Stuff towels or rags in the cracks under doors. Close blinds and curtains to keep in some heat [4][5].
3. **Layer up** with loose, lightweight, warm clothing, a hat, mittens and blankets or sleeping bags [4]. Wear a hat indoors. Keep dry; wet clothing chills the body quickly [2].
4. Base layers of wool, silk or polypropylene hold more heat than cotton [2].
5. Eat and drink. Food gives the body fuel to make heat. Use non-alcoholic, non-caffeinated drinks. Warm, sweet drinks or broth are better choices than alcohol or caffeine [2][5].
6. **No alcohol or caffeine to "warm up."** They make the body lose heat faster [2].
7. Check on older relatives and neighbors once your own household is settled [4].
8. If you have a gas furnace, check that its vent is not blocked by a snowdrift as soon as it is safe to go out [5].

### B. The CO rule at home

1. Burn nothing indoors except a vented fireplace or stove built for it and kept in working order [10][12].
2. Never use a gas range or oven to heat a home [10][12].
3. Generators outdoors only, at least 20 ft from windows, doors and vents [1][10][12].
4. Have battery or battery-backup CO alarms on every level and outside sleeping areas [13].
5. Electric space heater (only if you have power): plug it into a wall outlet, keep it at least 3 ft from anything that can burn, and never leave it on while sleeping [13].
6. Details and the full list: [Generator and Heater CO Prevention](generator-and-heater-co-prevention.md).

### C. Hypothermia: the settled steps

Call 911 whenever you suspect hypothermia [8]. While you wait or if help is delayed:

1. **Get the person out of wind and rain.** Move them gently, with little exertion [8].
2. **Wet clothes come off only once you are somewhere warm and sheltered.** Replace them with dry, loose clothing. In the open, put dry layers and a waterproof layer over them instead. Cover the head and neck [3][6]. The Wilderness Medical Society guideline says wet clothes should not be removed until the person is in a warm environment [17]. See also [Warming a cold person](../../../03_fire.md#warming-a-cold-person).
3. **Body heat and dry blankets first.** Warm the center of the body: chest, neck, head and groin, skin to skin under dry blankets [3][4].
4. **Heat packs:** put them on the chest, armpits and back [17], wrapped in thin, dry cloth so they do not touch bare skin [6]. The Red Cross allows heating pads or hot-water bottles wrapped the same way [6]. See also the [shelter section](../../../04_shelter.md#someone-is-too-cold-hypothermia-and-frostbite). MedlinePlus says not to use direct heat such as a heating pad [8]. Use body heat and dry blankets first (this entry's rule, the safer choice).
5. **A warm, sweet drink, only if the person is alert, shivering and can swallow normally.** Red Cross and MedlinePlus say alert and able to swallow [6][8]; the Wilderness Medical Society guideline names an alert person with effective shivering [17]. No alcohol or caffeine [2][6]. If they are confused, drowsy or not shivering, give nothing by mouth.
6. Stay with the person. Rewarm slowly. Rapid rewarming can cause dangerous heart rhythms [6].
7. Call 911 first. If they stop responding and are not breathing normally, be ready to give CPR as you were trained [6], and keep warming them (general practice).

### D. Frostbite

1. Treat hypothermia first. Get out of the cold; remove wet clothing and tight jewelry [7].
2. **Rewarm only if the part cannot refreeze.** If it could refreeze, wait. Refrozen tissue is damaged worse [7].
3. Soak the part in warm, not hot, water, about 99 to 104 F [7], for 15 to 30 minutes [16]. MedlinePlus gives 104 to 108 F; this entry takes the cooler Red Cross range (this entry's rule, the safer choice). Test the water with your own hand, because numb skin cannot feel a burn (general practice).
4. Wrap loosely in a dry, sterile dressing, with gauze between fingers or toes [7].
5. Call 911 for blisters, black skin or signs of hypothermia [7].
6. Do not rub or massage frostbitten skin [3][15][16]. Do not use a heating pad, heat lamp, or the heat of a stove, fireplace or radiator to warm it [15]. MedlinePlus also lists a campfire and a hair dryer as direct dry heat to avoid [16]. Do not break blisters [7][16].
7. Do not walk on frostbitten feet or toes unless absolutely necessary [15].

### E. Frozen pipes

1. **Prevent:** let taps drip, and open the cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks so warm room air reaches the pipes [2][11].
2. **Thaw:** keep the faucet open, and use warm air from an electric hair dryer [2][11]. Never thaw with a torch or open flame [2][11]. The Red Cross also says to avoid kerosene or propane heaters and charcoal stoves [11].
3. Check the other faucets; if one pipe freezes, others may too. If you cannot find or reach the frozen section, or cannot thaw it, call a licensed plumber [11].
4. Ready.gov points readers to learn pipe protection ahead of time [1]. Do it before the storm.

### F. When to leave

See the triggers above. Follow local officials on travel (general practice). Stay inside during severe cold if you can heat a room safely, especially when wind chill is -50 F or below [4]. If someone shows hypothermia signs and you cannot travel safely, call 911.

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT burn anything indoors for heat** except a vented fireplace or stove in working order [10][12].
- **Do NOT use a gas stove or oven for heat** [10][12].
- **Do NOT run a generator in a garage or near a window, door or vent** [1][12].
- **Do NOT use alcohol or caffeine to warm up** [2].
- **Do NOT rub frostbitten skin, or warm it with a fire, stove, radiator, heat lamp or heating pad** [3][15][16].
- **Do NOT rewarm frostbite if it can refreeze** [7].
- **Do NOT warm a hypothermic person fast** with hot water or a heat lamp [8][6].
- **Do NOT give anything by mouth to a confused, drowsy or non-shivering person** [6][8].
- **Do NOT call a drowsy, confused person "just tired."** Call 911 [1][3].
- **Do NOT thaw a pipe with a torch** [2][11].

## Med Continuity

This entry gives no dosing. Cold can chill refrigerated or temperature-sensitive medicines in a freezing house; ask a pharmacist before using one that has frozen. Insulin that has frozen must not be used [14] (see the survey guide's [Medicines and power-dependent devices](../../../09_urban_survival.md#medicines-and-power-dependent-devices)). A powered medical device that cannot run means going where there is power. See [Chronic Medication Continuity](../../06_first_aid/entries/chronic-med-continuity.md).

## Failure modes

| What goes wrong | Fix |
|---|---|
| Whole house kept "as warm as possible" with a gas oven | One room, layers, blankets; oven stays off [10] |
| Generator "just in the garage" because of snow | Outdoors, 20 ft away, under an open cover [12] |
| Hot drink of alcohol to warm up | Warm, sweet, non-alcoholic drink only if alert and shivering [2][6] |
| Drowsy relative left to sleep | Call 911 [1][3] |
| Frostbitten feet warmed, then walked on | Rewarm only if it cannot refreeze; avoid walking [7][15] |
| Pipe thawed with a torch | Hair dryer, faucet open [2][11] |
