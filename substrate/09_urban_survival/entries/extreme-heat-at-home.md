# Extreme Heat at Home With No Power or Air Conditioning

**This is general first-aid and safety information from government and Red Cross pages. It is not medical advice. Heat stroke is an emergency: call 911.**

Extreme heat is a period of high heat and humidity with temperatures above 90 F for at least two to three days [5]. Heat illness can start before then. When a heat wave takes out the power, the fan and the air conditioner stop, and the house keeps warming [7]. This entry covers the signs, the first aid, and how to cool a person and a home. For the short version, see the survey guide ([Heat](../../../09_urban_survival.md#heat)). For the outage overview, see [Power Outage Bug-In](power-outage-bug-in.md). For body-temperature first aid in the field, see [Thermal Injury: Hypothermia and Hyperthermia](../../06_first_aid/entries/thermal-injury-hypo-hyperthermia.md).

> ⚠ **HEAT STROKE: body temperature above 103 F, or the signs below. It can kill within minutes.**
>
> Heat stroke can push body temperature to 106 F or higher within 10 to 15 minutes [1]. **Call 911.** Cool the person quickly while you wait. **Give nothing by mouth** [6] (this entry's rule, following NWS).

---

## Recognize

### Who is at most risk

- Older adults, children and infants, and people with chronic conditions [5][2].
- People with asthma, heart disease or pregnancy, and outdoor workers and exercisers [2].
- People who take certain prescription medicines that affect how the body controls temperature or sweating [3]. Do not change a medicine on your own. Ask a pharmacist or prescriber.
- People who drink alcohol, and people not used to heat [8].
- Anyone without air conditioning [3].
- People who live alone, who should have someone check on them [2][4].

### Heat exhaustion

Headache, nausea, dizziness, weakness, irritability, thirst, heavy sweating, and a raised body temperature [1]. The skin is cool, moist and pale [10]. Heat cramps are painful muscle cramps and spasms, usually in the legs and abdomen [6].

### Heat stroke

Confusion, altered thinking, slurred speech; hot, dry skin or heavy sweating; seizures or fainting; a body temperature above 103 F [1][5][6]. A rapid, strong pulse is also a sign [5]. Do not wait for a thermometer to act on the signs (general practice).

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

**Call 911 now for any of these:**

- Confusion, slurred speech, seizure, or fainting in the heat [1][6].
- Body temperature above 103 F [5][6].
- Hot, dry skin, or heavy sweating together with confusion [1].
- Heat exhaustion where the person vomits, cannot take fluids, faints, does not improve, or whose responsiveness changes [10].

**Leave the house, or get the person to air conditioning, when:**

- Anyone shows heat illness signs and the home cannot be cooled (this entry's rule; Ready.gov says to find a cooling center if your home has no air conditioning [5]).
- A household member depends on a powered device and the power is out (see [`09_urban_survival.md`](../../../09_urban_survival.md#when-to-leave)).
- You cannot reach a cool room below 90 F (this entry's rule, the safer choice).

Call a provider if heat exhaustion symptoms get worse or last more than an hour [5][6].

---

## Respond

### A. Heat stroke (call 911 first)

1. **Call 911.** If you are alone, use speaker and keep working (general practice).
2. Move the person to shade or the coolest room. Remove outer clothing [1][5].
3. **Cool at once.** Best is cold-water immersion up to the neck, for 15 minutes or until they are no longer confused [9]. If you cannot, use ice packs, ice sheets, cooling towels, a cold shower, or misting and fanning, alone or together [9]. Put cold wet cloths or ice packs on the head, neck, armpits and groin [5].
4. Keep cooling for 20 minutes, or until the person improves or EMS arrives [9]. Keep their face above water (general practice).
5. **Give nothing by mouth** [6]. (NWS says "Do NOT give fluids." MedlinePlus allows sips only for an alert person who is not vomiting [8]. This entry takes the stricter NWS rule.)
6. Stay with them. If they stop responding or stop breathing normally, follow the CPR steps you were taught and tell the 911 dispatcher (general practice).

### B. Heat exhaustion

1. Move to a cooler place. Loosen or remove clothing [1][10].
2. Cool them with cool compresses, cold wet cloths or cool water [1][10].
3. If they are awake and can swallow, give small, slow sips of a cool sports drink or water [1][10]. Do not let them gulp.
4. Watch them. Call 911 if they vomit, cannot take fluids, faint, get worse, or their responsiveness changes [10]. Get medical help if it lasts more than an hour [5][6].

### C. Cooling the home with no power

1. **Block the sun.** Cover windows that get morning and afternoon sun with shades or drapes. Outdoor awnings and louvers do more than indoor shades [7].
2. **Windows:** if the air outside is hotter than the air inside, keep them closed. Open them when it is cooler outside, and mind home security [7].
3. **The fan rule: do not rely on a fan.** Fans help only when the room is below 90 F. Above 90 F a fan can raise body temperature [2]. Ready.gov also says not to rely on a fan as your main cooling device [5], and EPA says not to use a fan in a closed room [7].
4. **Cool the person, not just the room:** take cool showers or baths, wear loose, lightweight, light-colored clothing [4][5][11].
5. **Drink plenty of fluids**, even if you do not feel thirsty [4]. Light yellow or clear urine means you are drinking enough [2]. If a doctor limits your fluids or you take water pills, ask how much to drink in hot weather [3].
6. **Cut indoor heat:** cook less; use cold food first. Use battery lights, not candles [7].
7. **Go to a cool place.** Without air conditioning, use a cooling center, library or mall. Dial 2-1-1, or call the local health department or emergency management office, for locations [2][5][7][12].
8. **Fuel and power:** never run a generator indoors, in a garage or shed. Outdoors only, at least 20 ft from the house [7]. See [Generator and Heater CO Prevention](generator-and-heater-co-prevention.md).

### D. Check on people

1. Check on older adults and neighbors who live alone [2][4][5]. CDC advises checking on older adults in your care at least twice a day [3]. Ask someone to check on you, too [4].
2. **Never leave people or pets in a closed car** [5].
3. If the power stays out, food and refrigerated medicine have their own clocks: see [Food Safety During Outages and Floods](food-safety-during-outages.md).

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT rely on a fan above 90 F.** It can raise body temperature [2].
- **Do NOT give anything by mouth to a person with heat stroke** [6].
- **Do NOT give salt tablets** [8], or fever medicines such as aspirin or acetaminophen, to a person with heat stroke [8]. For cramps, CDC also says to avoid salt tablets [1].
- **Do NOT wait for the person to "sleep it off"** if they are confused, faint or have a seizure [1][6].
- **Do NOT leave a child, older adult or pet in a parked car**, even for a moment [5].
- **Do NOT open windows when the air outside is hotter than the air inside** [7].
- **Do NOT use the stove or oven** for cooking if the house is already hot (general practice).
- **Do NOT stay in a house you cannot cool** when someone is at high risk [5].

---

## Med Continuity

This entry gives no dosing. Some medicines affect how the body handles heat [3][8]. If you take one, ask a pharmacist or prescriber now, not during the heat wave. Insulin and some other medicines have storage limits that a hot house can pass: see [Chronic Medication Continuity](../../06_first_aid/entries/chronic-med-continuity.md) and the survey guide's [Medicines and power-dependent devices](../../../09_urban_survival.md#medicines-and-power-dependent-devices). For a powered device that cannot run, go where there is power.

## Failure modes

| What goes wrong | Fix |
|---|---|
| Waiting for a thermometer while a confused person gets worse | Act on the signs and call 911 [6] |
| Running a fan at 95 F and assuming it helps | Cool showers, shade, or leave for air conditioning [2][5] |
| Windows opened at midday on a hot day | Open only when it is cooler outside [7] |
| Giving a heat stroke patient water or a salty drink | Nothing by mouth; cool the body [6] |
| Nobody checks on the neighbor who lives alone | Check at least twice a day; use the cooling center [3][5] |
| Generator brought indoors for a fan | Outdoors only, 20 ft away [7] |
