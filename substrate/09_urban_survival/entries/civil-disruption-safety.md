## Recognize

### Escalation Signs (Early)
- Large crowds gathering in public spaces (parks, main streets, government buildings).
- Chanting, shouting, or signs of organized group activity.
- Increased police/security presence, barricades, or road closures.
- Social media or news reports of planned protests or demonstrations.
- Vandalism, broken windows, or graffiti at specific locations.

### Escalation Signs (Active Unrest)
- Crowds moving rapidly or erratically; pushing, shoving, or aggression.
- People wearing masks, helmets, or protective gear suggesting confrontation.
- Fire, smoke, looting, or property destruction visible.
- Police in riot gear, tear gas canisters, or water cannons in use.
- Sounds of breaking glass, sirens, or gunfire.
- Crowds blocking roads or preventing vehicle movement.

### Personal Risk Indicators
- You are in or near the crowd when behavior shifts from peaceful to violent.
- Escape routes are blocked or uncertain.
- You are identified (by appearance, clothing, or action) as part of a "opposing" group.
- Nightfall or low visibility (harder to navigate, higher risk of accidental injury).

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

**Evacuate or shelter in place immediately if ANY of these occur:**

1. **Active violence:** gunfire, explosions, people attacking one another with weapons.
2. **Tear gas, pepper spray, or crowd-control munitions** used by law enforcement or rioters — breathing becomes difficult, vision compromised; leave the area.
3. **You are trapped or separated** from your exit path — building cordoned off, streets blocked, or you cannot see a safe route.
4. **A crowd surge or crush** — people are packed tightly and falling, or the crowd is pressing dangerously.
5. **Fire or smoke** spreading in buildings or vehicles.
6. **You are targeted or at risk** of being detained, assaulted, or caught in crossfire (wrong place at wrong time).
7. **Night has fallen** and visibility is poor; navigation and risk of injury increase sharply.
8. **You have vulnerable people with you** (children, elderly, people with medical conditions) who cannot move quickly or tolerate prolonged stress.

**Action:** If you see ANY red flag, depart the area calmly (don't run, which triggers panic); move perpendicular to the crowd's flow if possible; seek shelter in a nearby building or vehicle; contact authorities or someone outside the area if safe to do so.

## Respond

### Phase 1: Awareness & Early Avoidance (Before Escalation)

1. **Stay informed.** Monitor local news, emergency alerts (FEMA app, local radio, text alerts from your city), and social media to identify planned or active disruption in your area.
2. **Avoid the area.** If you know a protest or disruption is happening:
   - Alter your route; don't drive or walk near the gathering.
   - Stay home if in the immediate area.
   - If at work or school, follow shelter-in-place protocols or evacuate early before crowds form.
3. **Blend in (gray-man approach).** If you must be near an area with activity:
   - Wear neutral, everyday clothing (no political symbols, logos, or distinctive apparel).
   - Avoid drawing attention by stopping, filming, or engaging with crowds.
   - Keep your phone on silent; don't record or live-stream.
   - Move through quickly and calmly; don't loiter.

### Phase 2: If You Encounter an Escalating Crowd

1. **Assess your surroundings.** Look for exit routes (alleys, side streets, buildings to enter).
2. **Do NOT engage.** Do not shout back, wave signs, mock, or interact with the crowd — this marks you as a potential target or friction point.
3. **Move away calmly.** Walk (don't run) perpendicular to the crowd's direction; running triggers panic and pursuit. If you're in the crowd, move slowly toward the edge.
4. **If blocked, find shelter.** Enter a nearby building (store, café, public building), a vehicle, or a secured area.
5. **Stay with a group if possible.** Individuals are more vulnerable; being with others (friends, family, strangers seeking shelter) provides both safety and reduced visibility.

### Phase 3: Shelter-in-Place Decision

**Choose shelter-in-place if:**
- You are already home or in a safe building.
- Evacuation routes are blocked or dangerous.
- Disruption is outside your immediate area and moving away.
- You have vulnerable people (children, elderly, medical conditions) with you.

**Actions:**
1. Lock doors and windows.
2. Close curtains/blinds; avoid standing near windows.
3. Stay away from the street side of the building.
4. Monitor news/emergency alerts for updated information.
5. Have water, food, and first-aid supplies accessible.
6. Keep your phone charged and ringer on (for emergency alerts).
7. Do NOT venture outside to observe, even briefly — the situation can change rapidly.

### Phase 4: If You Must Evacuate

1. **Decide on a destination early.** Before leaving, know where you're going (friend's/family's home, emergency shelter, out of town).
2. **Use a vehicle if available and safe to drive.** Avoid main roads; use side streets. Do NOT drive into crowds or blocked areas.
3. **On foot evacuation:** move away from crowds, use back streets and alleys, avoid main gathering areas.
4. **Take essentials:** wallet, ID, phone charger, a small bag with water/snacks, medications, and change of clothes if time allows.
5. **Use landmarks, not GPS.** GPS may route you toward congested areas; rely on memory or printed maps.
6. **Stay in contact.** Call or text someone outside the area to confirm your status and destination (don't tie up emergency lines with non-urgent calls).
7. **If law enforcement is present,** comply with instructions (police may order evacuation); do not resist or question — compliance keeps you safer.

### Phase 5: If You Are in a Vehicle

1. **Do NOT stop to observe.** Keep driving; turn around if road is blocked ahead.
2. **Keep doors locked and windows up** while stationary.
3. **If surrounded by a crowd,** drive slowly and carefully (do NOT accelerate or ram); if safe, continue moving; if unsafe, park in a protected area (garage, well-lit lot) and shelter in place.
4. **Have gas, water, and a phone.** You may be stuck in traffic or blocked from reaching your destination.

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT record the crowd, police, or violence.** Recording makes you identifiable and can escalate situations. If you witness something critical (crime, injury), report it to authorities *after* you leave the area.
- **Do NOT confront, antagonize, or argue** with protesters, rioters, or police. Confrontation makes you a target.
- **Do NOT stay to "see what happens."** Curiosity in a dangerous situation gets people killed. Leave early.
- **Do NOT assume police or authority figures are uniformed.** Undercover agents may be present; comply with identified, uniformed law enforcement.
- **Do NOT split up from your group.** Individuals are more vulnerable; stay together.
- **Do NOT go back for belongings** if you've evacuated. Your life is worth more than your stuff.
- **Do NOT walk alone at night** during or immediately after disruption. Risk of random violence increases sharply after dark.
- **Do NOT carry weapons** unless you are trained and legally entitled (this varies by jurisdiction). A weapon you don't know how to use or can't defend is a liability.
- **Do NOT assume social media reports are accurate.** Rumor spreads faster than truth; rely on official emergency alerts and established news sources.

## Medical Continuity

If you have ongoing medical needs (prescriptions, insulin, dialysis, etc.):

1. **Before evacuation, take your medications and medical devices** with you.
2. **Wear a medical alert bracelet** (diabetes, allergies, cardiac conditions, etc.) so responders know your condition.
3. **If you cannot access a pharmacy during disruption,** contact a telehealth provider or hospital emergency department for guidance on rationing or alternatives.
4. **Inform someone outside the area** of your medical needs so they can advocate if you become separated or need help.
