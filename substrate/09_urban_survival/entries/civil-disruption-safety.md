> **Not medical or security advice.** This is a short entry. It includes only what Ready.gov and FEMA pages say about alerts, shelter in place, evacuation, and planning. It does not cover crowd tactics, self-defense, or weapons, because no official page opened for this review supports them. When officials give instructions, those instructions override this entry. Call 911 for an emergency.

## Recognize

- Federal, state, and local officials use alerts, local media, and local emergency management messages to tell people to shelter in place or evacuate. Sign up for alerts from your local emergency management agency and keep your phone able to receive emergency alerts (Ready.gov Evacuation; Ready.gov Get Tech Ready).
- FEMA lists civil unrest among the situations for which evacuation and shelter-in-place are used as protective actions (FEMA Planning Considerations). Your local officials decide which one applies.

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

- **An official evacuation order or advice to evacuate:** follow it. "Always follow the instructions of local officials," and leave early enough to avoid being trapped (Ready.gov Evacuation).
- **An official shelter-in-place instruction:** stay where you are and follow it (see Respond).
- **Anyone is hurt or in immediate danger:** call 911.

---

## Respond

### Before anything happens

1. **Know more than one way out.** Be familiar with alternate routes and choose destinations in different directions so you have options (Ready.gov Evacuation).
2. **Build a go-bag or emergency supply kit** with the essentials: water, food, flashlight, first aid kit, extra batteries, prescription medications, copies of important documents, cash, and a phone charger with a backup battery (Ready.gov Build A Kit; Ready.gov Evacuation).
3. **Make a family communication plan.** Decide how you will contact one another and reconnect if separated, and pick a familiar family meeting place (Ready.gov Make A Plan).
4. **Sign up for local alerts.** Have an emergency charging option for your phone. Text rather than call, because mobile networks can be overwhelmed (Ready.gov Get Tech Ready).

### During a disturbance

1. **Stay informed and follow the instructions of local authorities.** Pay attention to local media for official news and instructions as they become available (Ready.gov Shelter).
2. **If officials tell you to shelter in place, stay where you are and avoid uncertainty outside** (Ready.gov Shelter). Staying home with your kit, water, and phone charger is the plan, not going out to look.
3. **If officials tell you to evacuate, go.** Use your planned route, take your go-bag, and leave early (Ready.gov Evacuation).
4. **Check on family, older adults, and neighbors** (Ready.gov lists this under extreme heat; no opened page gives civil-disruption-specific neighbor guidance).

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT ignore an evacuation order.**
- **Do NOT depend on one exit or one route.** Have a second.
- **Do NOT wait until a disturbance starts to assemble a kit or a communication plan.**
- **Do NOT change, stop, or ration a prescription dose without a clinician.** If you will be away from home or cannot get a refill, call your doctor or pharmacist.

---

## Med Continuity

1. Keep prescription medications in your kit. CDC recommends at least a week's supply of all prescription medications and a cooler for refrigerated ones (CDC Building an Emergency Kit).
2. Write your medical needs, including prescriptions and equipment, into your family plan (Ready.gov Make A Plan). Organize and protect your prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs, and vitamins (Ready.gov Build A Kit).
3. If you run short, call your doctor or pharmacist. See the `chronic-med-continuity` entry. Do not ration doses on your own.
