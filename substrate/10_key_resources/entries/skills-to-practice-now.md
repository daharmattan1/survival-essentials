## Goal & When To Use

**Knowledge alone = failure under pressure.** Reading "how to start a fire" is not the same as lighting one in the rain with wet wood. Stress + unfamiliarity = panic + mistakes. This entry describes the **five core skills worth drilling to muscle memory NOW, before you need them**:

1. **Fire-starting** — using multiple methods in adverse conditions
2. **Water purification** — treating water safely with at least two methods
3. **Shelter-building** — rigging a tarp or debris shelter in 30 minutes
4. **First-aid response** — wound care, CPR, treating common emergencies
5. **Navigation** — moving to a destination without GPS

**The practice spine:** calm conditions + repetition + feedback = competence that survives stress. Practice is the ROI difference between "I read a manual" and "I will survive."

## Steps

### 1. Fire-Starting (Practice Until You Can Do It in Rain)

**Why now:** Fire = warmth + water treatment + morale. A single failed attempt in an emergency burns time and calories you can't spare.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Condition | Goal | Frequency |
|---------|-----------|------|-----------|
| **1–3** | Ideal (dry materials, calm) | Build 3 different fire lays (teepee, log cabin, star) | 1× per week, 3 weeks |
| **4–6** | Adverse (wet materials, wind) | Start fire with damp wood using tinder bundle | 1× per week, 3 weeks |
| **7–10** | Extreme (rain, cold hands, limited materials) | Start fire with collected field materials in 15 min | 2× per week, 2 weeks |

**Specific drills:**

1. **Tinder → Kindling → Fuel progression:** Gather dry tinder (char cloth, dryer lint, birch bark). Start with it, graduate to twigs (pencil-thin), then branches (wrist-thick), then logs. Repeat until you can transition smoothly without the fire dying.

2. **Multiple fire-starting methods:** Practice with each:
   - Lighter (backup: waterproof in Ziploc)
   - Ferrocerium rod + tinder (practice striking until sparks land consistently on tinder)
   - Waterproof matches + tinder
   - Bow drill (if you want mastery; time-intensive but zero-gear fallback)

3. **Wet-weather fire:** Practice in light rain. Collect materials from beneath trees (protected wood). Build a lean-to fire structure. Use split wood (exposes dry interior) not whole branches.

4. **Cold-hands test:** Wear gloves or dunk hands in cold water, then start a fire. You'll discover coordination issues and fix them in practice, not in an emergency.

**Success criteria:** You can build a self-sustaining fire in rain within 15 minutes using found materials. You've built at least 10 fires across different conditions.

### 2. Water Purification (Treat Water Consistently & Safely)

**Why now:** Contaminated water kills faster than hunger. You need confidence in your method, not just theoretical knowledge.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Method | Source | Test |
|---------|--------|--------|------|
| **1–2** | Boiling | Tap water | Boil for 1 min, let cool, drink. No illness = success. |
| **3–4** | Filter + boiling | Local stream (collect a jug) | Filter through cloth, then boil. Test with taste and wait 24h for illness. |
| **5–6** | Chemical (iodine or chlorine) | Local stream | Treat per instructions, wait contact time, drink. Note taste. |
| **7–8** | Combination (filter → boil → cool) | Murky local source | Full 3-step process, drink. Reflective: did you catch any mistakes? |

**Specific drills:**

1. **Boiling:** Boil water for exactly 1 minute (3 min if above 6,500 ft). Use a timer. Confirm the water reaches a rolling boil (not just hot). Let cool and drink it. Repeat 5 times. You'll own the timing and confidence.

2. **Filtration:** Build a simple sand-gravel-charcoal filter using a bottle with holes. Run cloudy stream water through it 2–3 times. Observe clarity. Understand its limitations (removes particles, not biological pathogens alone — you must boil after).

3. **Chemical treatment:** Use iodine tablets or liquid chlorine dioxide per instructions. Understand contact time (iodine = 30 min cold, 20 min warm). Taste the result (iodine has a flavor; some people find it unpleasant). Know it does NOT reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*.

4. **Integration test:** Fill a jug from a muddy source. Treat it using your chosen method end-to-end. Drink it. Wait 24 hours. If you're fine, you've validated the process.

**Success criteria:** You've treated and consumed water using at least 3 different methods. You understand which pathogens each method kills. You can explain (aloud, to someone) why you chose your method for a given source.

### 3. Shelter-Building (Rig a Tarp in 30 Minutes)

**Why now:** Shelter = protection from elements. In cold rain or heat, a shelter is the difference between survivable and deadly. Muscle memory = speed under stress.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Structure | Time Goal | Condition |
|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **1–2** | A-frame tarp lean-to | 20 min setup | Backyard, clear weather |
| **3–4** | Ground-to-tree ridge tarp | 25 min setup | Backyard, wind |
| **5–6** | Debris shelter (leaves/branches) | 30 min setup | Backyard or local woods |
| **7–8** | Tarp shelter with water runoff | 35 min setup | Rain (test actual rain conditions) |

**Specific drills:**

1. **Tarp A-frame:** Lay a 10×10 tarp. Rig one edge to a rope between two trees at head-height. Stake or weight the other edge on ground. Pitch angle should shed water. Practice until you can do it in 15 minutes. Work on knot speed (you'll tie dozens).

2. **Ridge-line tarp:** String a rope between two trees at ~6 feet high. Drape tarp over the line, stake corners to ground. Adjust for weather (steep angle = rain shedding; flat = more interior space). Should take 20–25 min.

3. **Debris shelter:** Gather branches, lean them against a ridgepole at 45°. Layer leaves/pine needles/bark for insulation. Leave an opening facing downwind. Sleep in it once (even just for an hour in daylight, fully clothed) to understand comfort and adjustments.

4. **Knot practice:** Your shelter depends on knots. Practice the bowline (creates a fixed loop), clove hitch (wraps around trees), and taut-line hitch (tensioning). Tie each 50 times. You should be able to tie them in the dark or with cold hands.

**Success criteria:** You can rig a weather-resistant tarp shelter in 30 minutes. You've actually slept (or sheltered) in at least one shelter you built. You can explain the trade-offs (quick vs. weather-proof vs. insulated).

### 4. First-Aid Response (Treat a Wound, Perform CPR, Handle an Injury)

**Why now:** When EMS is unavailable, your first-aid response may be the difference between life and death. Hands-on practice removes hesitation.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Skill | Practice Method |
|---------|-------|-----------------|
| **1** | CPR compressions | CPR dummy (Red Cross class recommended, ~$100) |
| **2–3** | Wound closure & bandaging | Practice bandaging on family members (minor scrapes) |
| **4–5** | Tourniquet application | Windlass tourniquets on a leg model or own arm (practice, not deployed) |
| **6** | Splinting | Create splints for arm fractures using cardboard + tape; test on family |
| **7** | Recovery position | Practice rolling an unconscious person into recovery position |
| **8** | Shock management | Review and practice steps (keep warm, elevate legs, calm talking) |

**Specific drills:**

1. **CPR hands-on (Red Cross class):** Take a 4-hour Red Cross or AHA CPR/AED course. You'll practice on a dummy. The practice is mandatory; reading about it is insufficient. Cost ~$60–$100. Recertify every 2 years.

2. **Wound care:** Next time you have a minor cut, treat it yourself using your first-aid kit. Observe: Do you know when to clean with water? When to apply pressure? How long to hold? What to cover it with? Practice on minor wounds builds confidence for major ones.

3. **Tourniquet application:** Buy a windlass tourniquet (CAT or SOFTT-W, ~$15). Practice applying it to your own leg above the knee, using one hand. Know where to place it (2–3 finger widths above the wound, never on a joint). Practice 10 times. Your goal: 10-second deployment if needed. Don't actually tighten to pain, but practice placement and technique.

4. **Splinting:** Fracture someone's arm (for practice). Use a cardboard tube or rolled magazine. Secure with tape or cloth strips. Check: Can the patient move fingers (yes)? Is the splint stable? Can you do it in low light? Practice 3 times.

5. **Shock position:** In your living room, practice rolling someone into the recovery position (side-lying, head tilted back for airway, top leg bent for stability). Know when to use it (unconscious but breathing; NOT if spinal injury suspected). Practice 5 times so you're confident if needed.

**Success criteria:** You've taken a certified CPR course (certificate in hand, valid). You've treated minor wounds using your kit. You've practiced applying a tourniquet and splint. You understand the RED FLAGS that mean "call EMS if possible" vs. field-treatable wounds.

### 5. Navigation (Move to a Destination Without GPS)

**Why now:** When electronics fail, you navigate with map and compass or by sun/stars. Uncertainty + wrong directions = wasted energy and panic.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Tool | Route | Time |
|---------|------|-------|------|
| **1** | Map + compass | Local 2-mile loop (marked trails) | 45 min |
| **2** | Map + compass | 3-mile route with 1 bearing change (mark on map beforehand) | 90 min |
| **3** | Map only (no compass) | Route using landmarks + terrain | 90 min |
| **4** | Sun/stars navigation | Daytime azimuth to distant landmark | 30 min |
| **5** | Night navigation | Star navigation to identify cardinal directions | 30 min (safe backyard) |

**Specific drills:**

1. **Map reading:** Study a topographic map of a familiar area. Identify: contour lines (elevation), trails, water features, landmarks. Point to your location on the map. Point to a destination. Imagine a route. Now walk that route (on actual trails) and verify the map matches reality. Do this 3 times in different areas.

2. **Compass basics:** Hold the compass flat. Align the orienting lines with map grid lines (north-south). Rotate the housing until the orienting lines align with the map's north-south axis. The direction-of-travel line now points to your destination. Walk that bearing. Practice 5 times on short (200-meter) bearing walks.

3. **Terrain association:** Don't follow a compass bearing blindly. Look for terrain features (hilltops, valleys, rivers) on the map. Match them to the landscape you're walking through. This confirms you're on course and builds confidence.

4. **Daylight position fixes:** Stop at 3 points on your hike. Identify 2–3 distant landmarks (hilltops, water towers, road intersections). Use your compass and map to back-bear to your position (the intersection of the back-bearings is your location). You'll learn how to "find yourself" if unsure.

5. **Sun/star navigation:** North Star (Polaris) is directly north in Northern Hemisphere (use it 24/7 if visible). Sun rises east, sets west. At solar noon, the sun points south. Practice finding your position (lat/long) using celestial navigation once; understand it as a fallback if map is lost.

**Success criteria:** You can navigate a 3-mile route using map and compass without getting lost. You understand back-bearing (confirming your position). You can identify at least 3 stars (Polaris, Sirius, Betelgeuse) and use them to find north at night.

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| **You practice once, then forget until emergency** | Schedule drills quarterly. Use your calendar: "Quarterly fire-starting drill" on Mar 21, Jun 21, Sep 21, Dec 21. Treat it like a dentist appointment. |
| **You practice in ideal conditions but panic in adverse weather** | Deliberately practice in rain, wind, cold. Don't wait for emergencies to experience unfavorable conditions. Weather practice is more valuable than fair-weather reps. |
| **Muscle memory didn't stick (you forget how to tie the knot)** | Review monthly. Tie a bowline once a week for 5 minutes. Skills decay if unused. Maintenance reps are faster than rebuilding from scratch. |
| **You're injured or out of shape; some drills feel impossible** | Adapt the drill to your body. Can't walk 3 miles? Start with 1 mile. Can't tighten a tourniquet one-handed? Practice two-handed until you're strong, then transition. Competence > perfect execution. |
| **You practice but don't reflect (miss what went wrong)** | Keep a log: date, skill, conditions, what went well, what was hard, what to improve. Review monthly. Reflection cements learning; mindless reps don't. |
