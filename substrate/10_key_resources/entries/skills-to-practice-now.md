## Goal & When To Use

**Knowing a skill is not the same as being able to do it under stress.** Reading "how to start a fire" is not lighting one in the rain with wet wood. This entry describes **five core skills worth drilling now, in calm conditions, before you need them**:

1. **Fire-starting** with more than one method, in poor conditions
2. **Water treatment** with at least two methods
3. **Shelter-building**: rigging a tarp or debris shelter
4. **First aid**: bleeding, CPR, splinting, positioning
5. **Navigation** without GPS

**Practice rule:** calm conditions, repetition, then feedback. Log each session (date, skill, conditions, what went wrong). This entry is a training plan, not the procedure itself. For the actual steps, use the chapter guides: [`03_fire.md`](../../../03_fire.md), [`02_water.md`](../../../02_water.md), [`04_shelter.md`](../../../04_shelter.md), [`06_first_aid.md`](../../../06_first_aid.md), [`07_navigation.md`](../../../07_navigation.md).

**Safety rules for every drill:** practise on yourself or a willing, uninjured volunteer. Never injure anyone, or yourself, to make a drill realistic. Nothing in these drills involves drinking test water, eating wild plants or mushrooms, or using real medicine.

## Steps

### 1. Fire-Starting (practise until you can do it in rain)

**Why now:** A failed attempt in an emergency costs time, warmth and energy.

**Before any fire drill:** check for burn bans and fire restrictions, use a cleared spot or a ring, and keep water and a shovel beside the fire. Put it fully out when you finish. See the burn-ban section of [`03_fire.md`](../../../03_fire.md).

**Practice plan:**

| Stage | Condition | Goal |
|-------|-----------|------|
| **1** | Dry materials, calm | Build three different fire lays (teepee, log cabin, star) |
| **2** | Damp wood, wind | Start a fire with a tinder bundle and split wood |
| **3** | Light rain, gloved or cold hands, limited materials | Start a fire from materials you gather yourself |

Repeat each stage until it works every time. Time yourself and log the time.

**Specific drills:**

1. **Tinder, kindling, fuel:** Start with fine tinder, move to pencil-thin twigs, then wrist-thick branches, then logs. Repeat until the fire does not die at the hand-off.
2. **Several ignition methods:** Practise with a lighter, a ferrocerium rod and tinder, and waterproof matches. Bow drill is optional and slow; learn it only after the others are easy.
3. **Wet-weather fire:** Collect wood from under trees. Split it to reach the dry interior.
4. **Cold-hands test:** Wear thick gloves or mittens and start the fire. Do not soak your hands in cold water; that risks cold injury.

**Done when:** you can start a self-sustaining fire in light rain from gathered materials, using at least two ignition methods.

### 2. Water Treatment (practise the steps, not the test)

**Why now:** You need your method to be automatic. The rules for treating water are in [`02_water.md`](../../../02_water.md); this section is only how to rehearse them.

**Never test a method by drinking untreated or stream water.** Feeling fine the next day proves nothing. Giardia can take 1 to 2 weeks to make you sick (see `02_water.md`), and CDC says chlorine bleach and iodine do not work well against parasites such as Cryptosporidium. Drink only water you have treated by the rules in `02_water.md`. In a drill, use tap water, or water you will pour out.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Method | Practice water | What you are rehearsing |
|---------|--------|----------------|-------------------------|
| **1** | Boiling | Tap water | Timing, rolling boil, cooling and storing |
| **2** | Clearing cloudy water, then boiling | Tap water stirred with a spoonful of clean dirt (pour it out when done) | Cloth pre-filter, settling, then the boil |
| **3** | Bleach or tablets | Tap water | Reading the label, measuring, setting a timer for the full wait |
| **4** | Filter | Tap water | Assembling, cleaning and backflushing as the maker says |
| **5** | Full sequence | Tap water | Clear, treat, store, in the right order, without looking |

**Specific drills:**

1. **Boiling:** Use a timer. Rolling boil for 1 minute; 3 minutes if you are above 5,000 ft or not sure of your elevation. That is the rule in `02_water.md`. Agencies differ (CDC says 3 minutes above 6,500 ft), and this guide takes the safer choice.
2. **Chemical treatment:** Use the product label for dose and wait time, not numbers from memory. Iodine has cautions (pregnancy, thyroid problems, iodine allergy) and a limit on how long it can be used; see `02_water.md`.
3. **Filters:** Learn the label. "Absolute 1 micron or smaller" removes parasites; it does not remove viruses. See `02_water.md`.
4. **Crypto rule:** If Crypto is possible, boil or use a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller. Chlorine and iodine alone are not enough.

**Done when:** you can run boiling, one chemical method and one filter from memory, and you can say aloud which germs each one does not reliably stop.

### 3. Shelter-Building (rig a tarp)

**Why now:** In cold rain or heat, shelter decides how long you last. Speed under stress comes from repetition. Designs and hazards are in [`04_shelter.md`](../../../04_shelter.md).

**Practice plan:**

| Stage | Structure | Condition |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| **1** | A-frame tarp pitch | Backyard, clear weather |
| **2** | Ridgeline tarp between two trees | Backyard, wind |
| **3** | Debris shelter (branches, leaves) | Backyard or local woods, only where allowed |
| **4** | Tarp with water runoff | Light rain |

Time each pitch and try to beat your last time, but do not trade a taut, water-shedding pitch for speed.

**Specific drills:**

1. **A-frame:** Tie one edge to a rope strung between two trees at about head height. Stake the other edge. The pitch should shed water.
2. **Ridgeline:** String a line between two trees. Drape the tarp over it and stake the corners. Steeper sheds rain; flatter gives more room.
3. **Debris shelter:** Lean branches against a ridgepole, then layer leaves or needles on top for insulation. Spend an hour in it in daylight, fully dressed, to learn what needs fixing.
4. **Knots:** Practise the bowline, clove hitch and taut-line hitch until you can tie them with cold hands or in the dark.

**Done when:** you can rig a weather-resistant tarp shelter alone and have actually sheltered in one you built.

### 4. First Aid (bleeding, CPR, splinting, positioning)

**Why now:** Hands-on practice removes hesitation. Reading about CPR or a tourniquet is not enough. This is practice, not medical advice; the steps are in [`06_first_aid.md`](../../../06_first_aid.md).

**Practice plan:**

| Skill | How to practise |
|-------|-----------------|
| **CPR and AED** | Take a hands-on course (Red Cross or American Heart Association). Ask the provider for the length, price and how long the certificate lasts. |
| **Bleeding and tourniquet** | Take a Stop the Bleed or similar hands-on course. Between classes, rehearse placement and routing with a training tourniquet. |
| **Bandaging** | Practise wrapping a pressure bandage on a willing volunteer who has no injury. |
| **Splinting** | Splint a pretend injury on an uninjured volunteer. |
| **Recovery position** | Practise on a willing volunteer (see below). |
| **Shock positioning** | Review and rehearse the steps (see below). |

**Specific drills:**

1. **CPR:** Do it in a course, on a manikin. A class gives you feedback on depth and rate that a book cannot.
2. **Pressure and tourniquet:** For life-threatening arm or leg bleeding: put a tourniquet on now; do not keep trying pressure alone (06_first_aid.md). Place the tourniquet at least 2 to 3 inches above the wound, between the wound and the heart, never over a joint. Tighten it fully, secure the rod, and write down the time (Stop the Bleed, DHS; matches `06_first_aid.md`). Do not loosen it (the Red Cross tourniquet guidance cited in `06_first_aid.md` says so). For other bleeding, press hard and add cloth if it soaks through. Rehearse with a training tourniquet, ideally with an instructor. Do not tighten a real tourniquet on a person for practice.
3. **Splinting:** Pretend a volunteer has a broken forearm (they are not injured). Splint the joint above and the joint below, pad it, tie it snugly but not tight, then check the part beyond the splint (colour, warmth, feeling). Cardboard or a rolled magazine works, with tape or cloth strips. Practise three times, including in poor light.
4. **Recovery position:** For a person who is unresponsive but breathing and has no suspected spine injury: roll them toward you onto their side, top leg bent at hip and knee, head tilted back to keep the airway open. Practise on a willing volunteer until it is smooth. If a spine injury is possible, do not move them (`06_first_aid.md`).
5. **Shock positioning:** This is a different skill from the recovery position. Per `06_first_aid.md`: lay the person flat, raise the legs about 12 inches unless there is a head, neck, back or leg injury, keep them warm, and give nothing by mouth. Rehearse the steps aloud.

**Done when:** you hold a current CPR/AED certificate, you have completed a hands-on bleeding-control class, and you can run the tourniquet, splint and positioning steps without notes.

### 5. Navigation (move without GPS)

**Why now:** Map and compass skill is learned on the ground, not from a page. The method is in [`07_navigation.md`](../../../07_navigation.md); read its compass-and-declination section before the first drill.

**Practice plan:**

| Session | Tool | Route |
|---------|------|-------|
| **1** | Map and compass | Short marked-trail loop near home |
| **2** | Map and compass | Longer route with one planned bearing change |
| **3** | Map only | Route by landmarks and terrain |
| **4** | Sun shadow | Daytime direction check against your compass |
| **5** | Polaris | Clear night in a safe place; find north |

Tell someone your route and return time before you go.

**Specific drills:**

1. **Map reading:** Study a topographic map of a familiar area: contour lines, trails, water, landmarks. Walk the route and check that the map matches the ground.
2. **Compass and declination:** Follow the steps in `07_navigation.md`, including writing the declination on your map and applying it with the right sign. Walk short bearings and check where you end up.
3. **Terrain association:** Match hills, valleys and streams on the map to what you see. Do not follow a bearing blindly.
4. **Back-bearing:** Take bearings on two or three distant landmarks and plot them to find your position (resection). `07_navigation.md` gives the steps.
5. **Sun and Polaris:** Practise the shadow-stick method in `07_navigation.md`. Polaris is the one star that marks north, and only in the Northern Hemisphere; other stars move. Check any sun or star direction against your compass.

**Done when:** you can walk a planned route using map and compass, correct for declination, and confirm your position with a back-bearing.

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| **You practise once, then forget** | Put quarterly drills on your calendar. Skills fade without use. |
| **You practise only in good weather** | Add rain, wind and cold gradually, and always with a safe way out. |
| **Knots and routines do not stick** | Repeat short drills weekly. Quick maintenance reps beat relearning. |
| **A drill is too hard for your body today** | Scale it down: a shorter walk, a two-handed tourniquet drill with the trainer. Competence matters more than perfect form. |
| **You practise but do not reflect** | Keep the log and review it monthly. |
| **A drill would involve injury, illness, or risky food or drink** | Stop. Replace it with a rehearsal on a manikin, a trainer, or tap water. |
