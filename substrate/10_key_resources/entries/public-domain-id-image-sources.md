## Goal & When To Use

This entry points to places where **real reference photos** of look-alike pairs can be found, and how to check whether you may copy them into this repo. During the prep phase (calm, with power, with internet), you:

1. Find reference photos of the pairs in this repo's mushroom and plant entries (morel vs. false morel, wild onion vs. death camas, and so on).
2. Check the license of each image.
3. Download and archive only images whose license permits it, or print them for your own field reference.
4. Log them in this repo's [`_media/SOURCES.md`](../../../_media/SOURCES.md).

**The critical rule:** Images are **supporting evidence, never sufficient for identification.** A photo alone can kill. Use photos only alongside the species entry's positive-ID checklist, the whole organism in hand, and, for any species with a deadly look-alike, an in-person expert. Never use a photo to decide that a wild plant or mushroom may be eaten.

## Steps

### 1. Search the Sources (and know what each one is)

None of these is an identification authority. They are places to find images, and each image has its own license.

**Biodiversity Heritage Library (BHL)** — digitized historical books and illustrations.
- URL: https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/
- Search by binomial (e.g., "Morchella esculenta") or common name.
- **Not all BHL items are public domain.** BHL lists three statuses: public domain, no known copyright, and in copyright (digitized with the rights holder's permission, usually under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0). For U.S. publications it gives the public-domain cutoff as the current year minus 95 (published before 1931, as of 2026). Check the **Copyright Status** field under "Show Info" in the book viewer [BHL page above].
- Old plates can be outdated or wrong in nomenclature. Look for several views (whole organism, cross-section) and confirm against a current reference.

**USDA PLANTS Database**
- URL: https://plants.usda.gov/ (redirects to plants.sc.egov.usda.gov)
- Useful for leaf shape, growth habit and range.
- **Not every PLANTS image is public domain.** The PLANTS image search has a "Copyright Status" filter with separate choices for "Non-Copyrighted Images" and "Copyrighted Images". Use the non-copyrighted filter if you want files for this repo, and give credit anyway. Copyrighted images need the rights holder's permission.

**USDA Weed Images** (National Agricultural Library)
- URL: https://agdatacommons.nal.usda.gov/articles/Weed_Images_The_Source_for_Images_of_Weeds_and_Weed_Management_in_Agriculture/24661503
- Photos of weeds in many growth stages, some of which are also plants people try to eat. The record page says "U.S. Public Domain" but only points to the images, which are hosted at weedimages.org (https://www.weedimages.org/). Check the license on weedimages.org for each image before copying (checked 2026-10-21).

**Wikimedia Commons**
- URL: https://commons.wikimedia.org/
- Community-contributed. Licenses vary by file and are shown on each file page. Species labels are sometimes wrong. Treat as a lead, not a source of truth.

**iNaturalist**
- URL: https://www.inaturalist.org/
- Community-contributed observations. Photos carry the uploader's license (many are all-rights-reserved). Use the license filter to find CC0 photos. A community identification is not a verified one.

**Mushroom Observer**
- URL: https://mushroomobserver.org/
- Mushroom Observer says every image is released under a Creative Commons license or into the public domain, but each uploader picks the license. CC-BY-SA is the default, and CC0 is one option among several, including non-commercial ones [Mushroom Observer page above]. Check each image. Do not assume CC0.

### 2. Check the License Before Downloading

**What this repo accepts for local files:** only **public domain or CC0**. This is the same rule as [`CONVENTIONS.md`](../../../CONVENTIONS.md) §8 and [`_media/SOURCES.md`](../../../_media/SOURCES.md).

**Public domain in the U.S.:**
- Works published in the U.S. before 1931. Works published in 1930 entered the public domain on January 1, 2026 [U.S. Copyright Office, link above]. The cutoff moves forward one year each January 1.
- Works made by U.S. federal employees as part of their jobs are generally public domain, but an agency's site can also host images from outside contributors (see the USDA PLANTS note above). Check each image.
- A file labelled "public domain" by an uploader is a claim, not proof. Look for the source and date.

**CC0:** no rights reserved. Fine to copy and archive.

**CC-BY and CC-BY-SA:** fine to look at online and to link to. Do **not** copy them into this repo, because the repo accepts only CC0 and public domain. If you want an attribution-licensed image for your own private field card, follow its attribution terms.

**Not usable:** all-rights-reserved images, and any image with no clear license. Use a different image.

**Where the license is shown:** on Wikimedia Commons at the bottom of the file page; on iNaturalist and Mushroom Observer in the photo details; on USDA PLANTS in the usage guidelines shown with each picture; on BHL in the "Copyright & Usage" section of the book viewer.

### 3. Download and Archive for Offline Access

**During prep (with internet):** For each image, save a note alongside it:

```
filename: <file name>
URL: <page where you found it>
License: <exact license as shown on the page>
Source: <site, book or record>
Attribution: <author or artist, if listed>
Downloaded: <date>
```

Store copies on more than one device (phone, laptop, USB stick) and print the ones you want.

### 4. Make a Look-Alike Reminder Card (optional)

A printed card is a reminder of what to check. It is not an identification tool and it does not tell you anything may be eaten.

1. Pick a pair from a species entry in this repo and keep the card text consistent with that entry.
2. Print the two photos side by side with the key differences underneath.
3. Laminate it, or keep it in a waterproof sleeve.
4. Write on the card: **"Reference only. A photo is never enough. Positive ID with the whole specimen and an expert before anything is eaten."**

Example text (cap descriptions from the regional morel entry; hollow, stem and cap-attachment traits from the sources named below):

```
MOREL (Morchella) vs. FALSE MOREL (Gyromitra)
REFERENCE ONLY. A card is never enough to identify a mushroom.

Morel (Morchella):
- Ridged, pitted cap: regular pits separated by ridges, honeycomb-like
- Cut lengthwise: hollow inside, cap and stem one continuous hollow
- Cap joined to the stem along its length (most species)

False morel (Gyromitra):
- Cap wrinkled, lobed, brain-like
- Cut lengthwise: stem filled with cottony fibers; not one continuous hollow
- Cap attached to the stem only at the top
- POISONOUS. Do not eat.

Other mushrooms also resemble morels (see the species entry).
If anything differs, or you are unsure: do not eat it.
```

Sources for these traits: the regional morel entry (pitted or honeycombed cap with ridges for true morels; wrinkled, brain-like, lobed cap for false morels), Missouri Department of Conservation (true morels are hollow; false morels are not) and Tom Volk's page (UW; cap fused along its length in Morchella and attached only at the top in Gyromitra; stalks of Gyromitra filled). See also the full entry [`morel-morchella.md`](../../../regional/mid-atlantic-appalachian/mushrooms/entries/morel-morchella.md) and its positive-ID safety gate.

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Failure | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| **Image shows only one angle (top view)** | Find another source showing underside, cross-section, and whole organism. A single angle is not enough, and even several photos are not enough to identify. |
| **License is unclear or missing** | Do not use it. Find an image with a clear public-domain or CC0 license. |
| **Image is labelled public domain, but you cannot find its source** | Treat it as unlicensed. |
| **A Commons or iNaturalist photo may not be the species you searched** | Species labels there are sometimes wrong. Compare with a published field guide and the species entry, and do not rely on it. |
| **A printed card gets wet or faded** | Keep a color copy at home. The card is only a prompt; the species entry holds the real checks. |
| **No internet during prep** | Use library Wi-Fi or a friend's connection. Do the downloads before you need them. |
| **AI-generated images appear in search results** | They exist, and they often show invented traits. Verify the source page and the license. If an image comes from a non-official site, do not use it. |
