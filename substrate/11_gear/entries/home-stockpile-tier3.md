# Home Stockpile Tier 3 — Shelter-in-Place, 2-Week Supply

## What & Why

Tier 3 is your bug-in larder: 14 days of supplies to shelter in place if infrastructure fails (power, water, transport). Scenarios: extended winter outage, pandemic isolation, natural disaster aftermath. You're not going anywhere, so weight is irrelevant; redundancy and volume matter. If one system breaks (water goes out, power dies, route to store closes), you have backups and stores.

Two-week supply targets 1 gallon water per person per day (drinking + cooking) and ~2,000 calories per person per day (canned goods, dry staples, protein). Tier 3 also replaces consumables: batteries in bulk, candles, fuel for cooking/heat, sanitation supplies. The discipline: rotate stock, test all systems seasonally, keep consumables and backup tools out of sight (not a disaster supplies advertisement).

## Selection Criteria

**Water storage**
- Look for: food-grade containers, dark color (algae inhibition), sealed lids, stackable or modular
- Cheap versions fail: thin plastic cracks under weight, lids unseal, colored containers degrade
- Test before filling: can you move a full 5-gallon jug? (45 lbs; consider rotation strategy—don't stack 10 deep)

**Food staples (non-perishable base)**
- Canned goods: low-sodium where possible (salt + no refrigeration = stomach issues). Proteins (tuna, beans, chicken), vegetables, fruits, soups
- Dry goods: rice, pasta, oats, flour (rotate annually; freezer helps extend life; whole grains go rancid, use within 6 months)
- High-calorie backup: peanut butter, nuts, powdered milk, cooking oil (calories-dense, lasts 2+ years sealed)

**Power sources (redundant)**
- Batteries: 50+ AA, 50+ AAA, 20+ D. Buy alkaline in bulk (Costco). Test headlamps/radios work with them
- Generator (if budget allows): propane or dual-fuel. Requires fuel rotation and monthly run (fuel stabilizer helps); noisy, use outside only (carbon monoxide risk indoors)
- Solar charger or hand-crank power bank: no fuel, regenerative, quiet, slower

**Heat source (emergency backup)**
- Propane camping stove or portable heater (outdoor use only; never indoors—carbon monoxide)
- Fireplace/wood stove (if your home has one; store 2–3 cords dry firewood, rotate seasonally)
- Blankets and layers: wool blankets (40+ lbs insulation, works damp), sleeping bags, cold-weather clothing for all household members

**Light (redundant, extended duration)**
- Flashlights: 4–6 units (different sizes for different rooms/uses)
- Headlamps: 2–3 spares (hands-free for repairs, first-aid)
- Candles: 20+ tea lights or votive candles (long burn time, low cost); matches/lighter to light them
- Glow sticks: instant light, no fire risk (for kids, emergencies)

**Sanitation (critical, often overlooked)**
- Toilet paper: 24+ rolls (48 = 1 month single person). Hand sanitizer and wet wipes (if sewer/water fails, no flush)
- Trash bags (heavy-duty, 100+ count): essential for waste management if pickup stops
- Soap, laundry detergent, bleach: keep water running, or improvise hand-washing with containers + bleach for sanitizing surfaces
- Feminine products, diapers, adult incontinence: 3–4 week supply for anyone who needs them

**Medical (extended stockpile)**
- Over-the-counter medications: pain (ibuprofen 500 count), cold/cough, anti-diarrheal (Imodium 50 count), antihistamine, antacid, laxative
- First-aid supplies: bandages (100+ count), sterile gauze (100 pads), medical tape (4 rolls), elasticated bandages, antibiotic ointment (20+ tubes)
- Personal prescriptions: 30-day supply minimum (work with doctor for emergency refills)
- Wound care: antiseptic solution, tweezers, scissors, thermometer

**Tools (backup/redundancy for repairs)**
- Multitool: second one (first one lives in Tier 2 or on you)
- Fixed-blade knife: backup
- Hammer, nails, screws: repairs
- Duct tape (3–4 rolls), electrical tape, zip ties: quick fixes
- Flashlight with alkaline batteries: hand-crank won't work if injured or in shock

## Multi-Use

- **Water storage**: emergency drinking (if taps go out), cooking, cleaning/sanitation, gardening (rotation), flushing toilet (bucket method if needed)
- **Canned goods**: nutrition, morale (comfort foods during stress), trade goods (beans can swap for information with neighbors), broth/liquid for cooking
- **Dry staples (rice, pasta)**: calories, combine with canned goods for complete meals, shelf-stable for years, lightweight storage
- **Batteries**: power all flashlights/radios/clocks, trade currency (valuable if supply breaks), backup for comms
- **Propane**: cooking (stove), heat (heater—outdoor only), light (if appliances work)
- **Candles**: light, heat (minimal but measurable in small space), morale/ritual (psychological benefit during stress)
- **Blankets/sleeping bags**: insulation, emergency shelter (if house damaged), travel if evacuation forced
- **Bleach**: water sanitation (2–3 drops per gallon), surface disinfection, wound cleaning (dilute), laundry
- **Duct tape**: seal windows (emergency weatherization), patch holes, secure tarp, repairs
- **First-aid supplies**: treat minor injuries (avoiding ER if possible), prevent infection (antibiotics), manage pain/sickness

## Maintenance / Failure Modes

**Water storage**
- Failure: algae growth (green tint), bacteria if contaminated (rare with clean starting water). Fix: drain and refill every 6 months; add unscented bleach (1/8 tsp per gallon) to inhibit growth
- Prevention: store in cool, dark location; stack on level surface (not stacked too high—weight risk); keep rotation calendar on label

**Canned goods**
- Failure: rust on can exterior (inspect at purchase), swelling (botulism—discard). Fix: transfer to new can if surface rust is minor; discard if bulging
- Prevention: store on shelves (away from concrete floor—moisture); rotate oldest first (FIFO); keep cool/dry

**Dry staples (rice, pasta, flour)**
- Failure: insect infestation (moths, weevils) if stored in paper or thin bags. Fix: freeze for 48 hours before storing (kills eggs), or use food-grade buckets with oxygen absorbers
- Prevention: transfer to sealed containers (Rubbermaid, buckets); food-grade is critical (can leeches chemicals); rotate every 6–12 months

**Batteries**
- Failure: depleted, corroded. Fix: test quarterly; replace corroded batteries with fresh stock
- Prevention: store upright, away from moisture; buy and rotate annually; keep in cool location

**Generator**
- Failure: won't start (stale fuel, old oil, mechanical). Fix: change oil every 50 hours, run monthly for 10 minutes, use fuel stabilizer (Sta-Bil)
- Prevention: store fuel separately in approved container (gasoline vaporizes, propane bottles are sealed); keep manual on hand

**Propane heater/stove**
- Failure: won't ignite (thermocouple fouled), tank empty, slow flame. Fix: clean burner, check tank valve, refill tank
- Prevention: never use indoors (carbon monoxide kills silently; must be outdoor or vented); keep away from children; test monthly

**Medications/first-aid**
- Failure: expired. Fix: rotate with pharmacy (discard old, buy new) every 6–12 months
- Prevention: keep calendar on kit; check expiration during quarterly maintenance; store in cool, dry place

**Bleach**
- Failure: loses potency over ~1 year (evaporates, degrades). Fix: buy new bottle every 12 months
- Prevention: store sealed, in dark container (UV degrades it); label date of purchase
