# EDC Tier 1 Kit — Everyday Carry

## What & Why

Tier 1 is what lives on your body *right now*. If an emergency starts in the next hour—vehicle breakdown, weather event, medical issue—these items prevent panic and buy time. The philosophy: carry things that work *and* that you can use competently. Dead weight that requires YouTube instruction is worse than nothing.

EDC kit focus is weight discipline (under 2 lbs total) and redundancy: two ways to make fire, two ways to see in dark, two cutting tools. At backpacker scales, multi-use items—a knife that can baton and prep food, a ferro rod that signals and starts fire—are survival economics.

## Selection Criteria

**Cutting tool (fixed or folding knife)**
- Look for: 3.5–4.5 inch blade, full tang or solid construction, can handle batoning (striking the spine to split wood)
- Cheap versions fail: plastic handles crack, blade edges roll immediately, can't handle impact
- Test before carry: your knife should be capable of splitting pine kindling

**Fire starting (redundant methods)**
- Lighters: BIC or Zippo. Cheap disposables fail in humidity/cold. Test by running under water—it should relight
- Ferro rod: 1/8–3/16 inch diameter, mil-spec preferred. Should produce shower of sparks even when wet
- Backup: waterproof matches or char cloth. Matches go in waterproof container (film canister works)

**Light source (redundant)**
- LED headlamp: hands-free is critical. 100–200 lumens is sufficient. Look for AAA battery (common, replaceable)
- Backup: compact flashlight or AA lenser-style light
- Batteries: carry spares. Alkaline only if carry weight matters; rechargeables if you rotate gear monthly

**Water treatment**
- Sawyer Mini straw or similar: 0.1 µm absolute, fits most bottles, lasts 100k gallons
- Backup: purification tablets (chlorine dioxide, not iodine—faster, fewer taste complaints)
- Container: lightweight collapsible bladder or metal bottle (boiling backup)

**Medical (minimal, high-frequency)**
- Tourniquet or flat headwrap (wounds to extremities)
- Sterile gauze pads (4–6 count)
- Antibiotic ointment and medical tape
- Ibuprofen, antihistamine (OTC basics)

## Multi-Use

- **Knife**: cuts kindling, food prep, first-aid (cutting bandages), repairs
- **Ferro rod**: fire starting, emergency signaling (sparks visible at distance)
- **Lighter**: fire, light (hold to tinder), signal/visibility
- **Headlamp**: hands-free light, can warm hands/face in cold, read maps/signals after dark
- **Water bottle**: boils water for purification/cooking, stores filtered water, can serve as windbreak for fire lay
- **Tourniquet**: secures tarp/paracord during shelter build, restraint, handles/straps repair
- **Gauze/tape**: wounds, blister prevention, gear repair (athletic tape also wraps sprains)

## Maintenance / Failure Modes

**Knife**
- Failure: edge rolls after impact or use on hard materials. Fix: sharpen on stones or bring a compact sharpener (credit-card style, 0.5 oz)
- Prevention: don't use knife as pry bar (that's pliers work); use batoning for impact, not hacking

**Ferro rod**
- Failure: rare; they're nearly indestructible. Scrape striker end to clear salt buildup if corrosion occurs
- Prevention: keep in dry pocket; saltwater/spray can oxidize striker

**Lighter**
- Failure: fuel evaporates in heat or very cold. Fix: carry two lighters, store in insulated pocket
- Prevention: test monthly; BIC lighters work down to ~0°F with practice

**Battery-powered gear (headlamp, flashlight)**
- Failure: batteries leak, corrode contacts, or die. Fix: carry spares separately; swap batteries quarterly
- Prevention: use alkaline only (rechargeable leak-prone); remove batteries during extended storage (3+ months)

**Water filter**
- Failure: clogged membrane (clean by backflushing with clean water via syringe). Rare: membrane rupture means replace
- Prevention: pre-filter through cloth if water is visibly turbid; don't force water through dirty membrane
