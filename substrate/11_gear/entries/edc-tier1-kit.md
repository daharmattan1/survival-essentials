# EDC Tier 1 Kit — Everyday Carry

> Gear-selection advice below is general practice unless a bracketed number points to a source in `## Sources`. First-aid items here are general information, not medical advice. For the steps, use [06_first_aid.md](../../../06_first_aid.md). The chapter this entry supports is [11_essential_gear.md](../../../11_essential_gear.md).

## What & Why

Tier 1 is what lives on your body or in your everyday bag. If an emergency starts in the next hour (vehicle breakdown, weather, an injury), these items buy time. The rule: carry only what you have practiced with. A tool you have never used, or a tourniquet nobody has trained you on, does little when you are scared and in a hurry.

Carry two ways to make light and two ways to make fire (general practice). The National Park Service's Ten Essentials list for the outdoors is the closest sourced model for a carry kit: navigation, sun protection, extra clothing, illumination, first-aid supplies, fire, repair kit and tools, nutrition, hydration, and emergency shelter [3]. This entry covers the on-body subset. Shelter, food and bulk water belong to [bug-out-tier2-kit](bug-out-tier2-kit.md) and [home-stockpile-tier3](home-stockpile-tier3.md).

## Selection Criteria

**Cutting tool (knife or multi-tool)**
- NPS lists "duct tape, knife, multi-tool, and scissors" under repair kit and tools [3]. Pick one tool you can open and use with cold hands.
- Cheap versions fail: handles that crack, edges that chip on first real use, locks that do not hold (general practice).
- Test before you carry it by using it to prepare kindling. Fire technique is in [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md).

**Fire (two methods)**
- NPS: "waterproof matches or a lighter, plus fire starters" [3]. Ready.gov lists matches in a waterproof container for the home kit [4].
- Carry a lighter plus a backup (matches in a waterproof container, or a ferro rod). Technique and the order of preference for each method are in [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md).
- Fire is for warmth and signaling outdoors. Nothing burns inside a tent, vehicle, garage or house except a vented fireplace or stove in working order. See [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md#fire-indoors-in-tents-and-in-vehicles-the-carbon-monoxide-rule).

**Light (two sources)**
- NPS: a flashlight and a headlamp [3]. A headlamp frees both hands.
- Carry spare batteries of the type your light uses [4]. Check them on a schedule (see Maintenance).

**Water treatment**
- A bottle you can drink from, plus a filter or a treatment option. NPS lists water and the supplies to treat it [3].
- Read the label for the filter's pore size and what it does not remove. [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md) says a filter rated at 1 micron absolute or smaller handles *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*, most filters do not remove viruses, and chlorine or iodine tablets do not reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*. Follow the maker's instructions and replace the filter when it slows or fails. This entry gives no filter lifetime because none is sourced.
- Amounts of water for the home and the bag are in [home-stockpile-tier3](home-stockpile-tier3.md) and [bug-out-tier2-kit](bug-out-tier2-kit.md).

**Medical (small, high-use)**
- **Tourniquet: only a purpose-made one counts.** A commercial windlass tourniquet is the item. A headwrap, belt, cord or scarf is not a substitute for the kit item. [06_first_aid.md](../../../06_first_aid.md#severe-bleeding) treats an improvised band as a last resort only.
- **Take a Stop the Bleed or first aid course.** Stop the Bleed teaches three techniques: direct pressure, packing a wound, and applying a tourniquet. Courses are held in person around the world, and an online version exists [2].
- Sterile gauze pads, adhesive tape, non-latex gloves and bandages follow the Red Cross kit list [6].
- Wound care is washing with mild soap and clean water [5]. Do not use household bleach on a wound. Details are in [06_first_aid.md](../../../06_first_aid.md).
- Over-the-counter medicines: only ones you already use. Follow the label dose. This entry gives no doses.
- Your own prescription medicines go with you if you will be away from home. An emergency can make a refill hard to get [4], so talk to your doctor or pharmacist about an emergency supply [8].

## Multi-Use

- **Knife or multi-tool:** kindling, food prep, cutting tape and bandages, repairs [3].
- **Lighter or matches:** fire, a flame to light tinder, a visible signal in the dark (general practice).
- **Headlamp:** hands-free light for repairs, first aid and walking at night [3].
- **Bottle (metal if you can):** holds treated water; a metal one can also be used to boil water (general practice; see [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md)).
- **Gauze and tape:** dressings, blister care, a taped repair on gear.
- **Tourniquet: one job only.** It controls life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg. Do not use it to tie, lash, repair or restrain. It stays in the kit, clean and ready.

## Maintenance / Failure Modes

**Tourniquet**
- Failure: you cannot find it, cannot put it on one-handed, or place it wrong. Fix: train. Placement is at least 2 to 3 inches from the wound, between the wound and the heart, never over a joint, and note the time [1].
- Prevention: keep it where you can reach it with either hand. Practice in a class, not for the first time on an injury. Replace it if the strap, buckle or windlass is worn or cracked (general practice).

**Knife / multi-tool**
- Failure: the edge dulls or chips, or the lock slips. Fix: sharpen or replace it. Do not use a knife as a pry bar (general practice).

**Fire tools**
- Failure: a lighter is empty, a match box is wet. Fix: carry a backup and check both when you check the kit (general practice). Method-by-method failure modes are in [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md).

**Battery-powered gear (headlamp, flashlight)**
- Failure: dead batteries, or corrosion on the contacts. Fix: replace the batteries and carry spares. Prevention: remove batteries from gear you will not use for a long time (general practice). CDC says to check emergency kits every 6 months [7].

**Water filter**
- Failure: it clogs or cracks. Fix: follow the maker's cleaning steps, and treat the water another way (boil, or see [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md)) if the filter fails or you doubt it. Keep a filter from freezing unless the maker says it can (general practice).
