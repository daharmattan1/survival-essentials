# Bug-Out Tier 2 Kit — 72-Hour Grab-and-Go

## What & Why

Tier 2 is your 72-hour evacuation bag. The scenario: you have 10 minutes to leave home. Weather, wildfire, flood, medical emergency—you need to survive and move for 3 days until help arrives or you reach a rally point. Tier 2 assumes you have your Tier 1 EDC and now add the infrastructure to extend that: shelter building (tarp + insulation), water storage (beyond what's in one bottle), food, radio (communications), and redundant fire/light.

At ~20–25 lbs loaded, it should be packable and carryable if you need to walk. The discipline: if it doesn't do two jobs, it stays home.

## Selection Criteria

**Water (storage + treatment)**
- Look for: 2–3 L carrying capacity (bottles or collapsible bladder); filter rated ≤1 µm (removes *Giardia*/*Crypto*); purification tablets as backup
- Cheap versions fail: thin-walled bottles crack under load, filters clog after 1,000 gal (not 100k), tablets are iodine (slow, tastes bad)
- Test: fill your bottles and carry for a mile. Can you grip/pour without spillage?

**Shelter (tarp + insulation)**
- Tarp: 9×12 ft minimum, ripstop nylon or canvas (not plastic sheeting—sun brittles it). Grommets or loop corners, not just seams
- Emergency blanket: aluminized Mylar (reflects ~90% body heat). Check they're not stuck together or crinkled past use
- Paracord: mil-spec 550, not cheap replacement; test it doesn't fray or snap under load

**Fire starting (redundant, extended)**
- Two full-size lighters (not cheap disposables)
- Ferro rod + waterproof striker
- Waterproof match kit (~30–40 count, UCO Stormproof or similar)
- Cotton balls + petroleum jelly (50+ count, cheap DIY tinder; store in plastic bag)

**Light (redundant, extended duration)**
- Headlamp rated for 20+ hours (check lumens × battery hours = total light output)
- Backup flashlight (AA or AAA model)
- Extra batteries: 4 AA + 4 AAA alkaline minimum

**Food (3 days, calorie-dense)**
- Meal bars or energy bars: 2,000–3,000 calories (6–9 bars)
- Beef jerky or SPAM: protein, shelf-stable, no prep
- Nuts, trail mix, or peanut butter packets: 200–300 cal/ounce
- Electrolyte powder: mixes with water, prevents cramps/dehydration

**Radio (information + signaling)**
- Hand-crank or battery-powered AM/FM radio (not smartphone—assumes no cell)
- Two-way radio (handheld ham or GMRS): requires license for ham; GMRS is license-light
- Whistle: 120 dB piezo (far cheaper signal than shouting)

**Navigation**
- Local area map (waterproof if possible; plastic sheet protects paper)
- Compass: basic orienteering model, ~$5
- GPS if you own one (not required; map+compass sufficient for local evacuation)

**First-aid (extended beyond Tier 1)**
- Larger first-aid kit or individual supplies: sterile gauze (20+ pads), medical tape (2 rolls), elasticated bandages (ankle/wrist support)
- Over-the-counter medications: ibuprofen (50 count), antihistamine, anti-diarrheal, antacid, any personal Rx (7-day supply)
- Tweezers and small scissors (wound cleaning)

## Multi-Use

- **Tarp**: rain shelter, ground cloth, water collection (corner tie-up during rain), insulation layer under sleeping area, emergency flag/signal (bright color)
- **Paracord**: lashing shelter, securing load to pack, fishing line (inner strands), tourniquet/restraint, repairs (torn fabric), fire lay structure
- **Emergency blanket**: shelter lining (heat reflection), insulation layer, signaling (crinkled, reflects light), water collection in a pinch
- **Meal bars**: quick calorie fix, morale boost, don't require fire/water, light weight per calorie
- **Jerky/SPAM**: protein, no refrigeration, can be eaten dry or reconstituted in hot water for broth
- **Radio**: weather alerts, rescue coordination, time check (sync to local time via broadcast), morale (news/music)
- **Headlamp + backup light**: hands-free work after dark, signal others (turn on/off pattern = SOS), map reading, first-aid procedures
- **Map + compass**: navigation, identifying landmarks, planning evacuation route, morale (staying oriented reduces panic)
- **Whistle**: audible signal (carries farther than voice), three-blast distress pattern, navigation (confirm you're heard)

## Maintenance / Failure Modes

**Tarp**
- Failure: tears on branches or sharp objects. Fix: repair kit (duct tape, fabric patch + seam sealer) or use emergency blanket as backup
- Prevention: inspect for snags before packing; pad sharp objects in backpack corners

**Paracord**
- Failure: fraying at ends. Fix: burn the cut ends with lighter (fuses nylon). Rare: rope abrades through on rough rock; rotate load or re-route
- Prevention: wrap ends with tape or burn them immediately after cutting

**Water filter**
- Failure: blocked by sediment or biofilm. Fix: backflush (squeeze clean water backward through filter) or soak in clean water overnight
- Prevention: pre-filter through cloth/coffee filter if water is visibly turbid; don't over-compress when squeezing

**Batteries**
- Failure: depleted (check headlamp works before bug-out), corrosion if stored damp. Fix: replace spares, clean contacts with pencil eraser
- Prevention: store batteries separately from gear (no contact with metal); replace every 12 months if not used

**Meal bars / jerky**
- Failure: melted/damaged if heat exposure. Fix: rotate annually; none. Prevention: keep in cool, dry pack section; check expiration dates

**Radio**
- Failure: battery dead (hand-crank models have spring; may weaken over years). Fix: crank and test monthly. For battery-powered, replace annually
- Prevention: store with batteries removed if unused for 3+ months
