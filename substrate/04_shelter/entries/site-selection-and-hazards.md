## Goal & When To Use

Before building any shelter, choose ground that won't actively hurt you. The Army manual's site checklist (ATP 3-50.21, para 6-9): enough materials to build the shelter you need; level and big enough to lie down with your gear; protection from sun, wind, rain and snow; **free of dead trees that may fall**; **free of avalanche, rockslide, mudslide and flash-flood areas and high-water marks**; not in a low-lying area that may be a cold sump (a hollow where cold air pools); and **suitable for signaling and recovery**.

In plain terms, a good site is:

- **Not in a hazard zone.** Not under dead trees or limbs, not in a flash-flood channel, not on a stream bank, not under a steep snow slope, not in the lowest hollow.
- **Protected from wind.** Natural screens such as fallen trees, rocks and large trees help (ATP 6-12).
- **Near water, but not on it.** Water nearby is useful, but the Army lists it as one factor among many, and NWS warns against camping on the banks of high, fast-running streams and in narrow valleys. **Treat all water before drinking it, however clean it looks.** See [`02_water.md`](../../../02_water.md).
- **Findable.** Rescuers need to reach you (see step 5).

The site is the first shelter problem. A good shelter on a bad site can still fail or hurt you.

## Steps

### 1. Avoid Flash-Flood Zones
- **Do not camp on the banks of high, fast-running streams, creeks or rivers, or in narrow valleys.** Distant rain can be channeled into gullies, narrow canyons, ravines and burn scars. Heavy rain can cause debris flows that destroy campsites and bury campers in minutes (NWS Blacksburg).
- **A dry channel is a flood channel.** Treat gullies, ravines and dry streambeds as places water will come (general practice, consistent with the NWS point above).
- **Look for high-water marks.** The Army manual lists high-water marks as a site hazard (ATP 6-9). Lines of debris on banks or trunks show where water has reached (general practice).
- **At even a slight rise in water level, go to higher ground immediately** (NWS Blacksburg). If camping in a low area, "get to higher ground immediately" (NWS flood page). Do not wait for water to reach you.
- **Six inches of fast-moving water can knock you off your feet** (NWS).

### 2. Scout for Hazard Trees and Overhead Hazards
- **Look up before settling.** A hazard tree has a structural defect that makes it likely to fail in whole or in part (USFS).
- **Signs of weak trees (USFS):** lacking needles, bark or limbs; conks (shelf-like fungus), broken tops, basal scars, numerous downed limbs, ants, or an abundance of woodpecker holes.
- **Check the site for things that could fall on the shelter:** loose rocks, dead limbs and similar (ATP 6-11).
- **Wind raises the risk.** If winds pick up while you are in the forest, head to a **clearing** out of reach of any potential falling trees (USFS). Do not move under another tree.

### 3. Avoid Cold Sinks
- **Avoid low-lying areas that may be a cold sump** (ATP 6-9). Cold air flows downhill and can pool in hollows and valley bottoms (general practice).
- **Choose ground a little above the lowest hollow** that still has a wind screen and is out of any flood channel (step 1). No source here gives distances or temperature differences, so none are claimed.

### 4. Assess Wind Exposure
- **Avoid ridge tops and open ground.** They are exposed to wind, and lightning injuries and deaths are more likely when you are exposed on higher ridges or out in fields (NWS Blacksburg).
- **Use natural screens:** fallen trees, rock and large trees reflect heat and block wind (ATP 6-12); large rocks on the leeward sides of hills and clumps of bushes are natural shelters (ATP 6-14).
- **Check the wind direction before building** and put the back of the shelter into the wind (ATP 6-16).
- **A tent or camper is not safe in high winds** (NWS Blacksburg).

### 5. Be Findable, Not Hidden
- **Stay put and stay visible.** NPS: if lost, stay put; changing location makes it harder for authorities to find you. Wear bright clothing and lay out your shelter or arrange rocks so it can be seen from the air (NPS Outdoor Emergency Plan). USFS: "stop, stay calm, stay put," especially at nightfall, if injured or near exhaustion.
- **Do not tuck the shelter out of sight.** The Army's checklist wants a site "suitable for signaling and recovery" (ATP 6-9). Choose ground rescuers can reach and aircraft can see. Signaling steps are in [`04_shelter.md`](../../../04_shelter.md) ("Be found from the shelter").
- **Tell someone your plan before you go** (USFS; NPS Yosemite: let someone know your itinerary).

### 6. Sun and Shade
- **In hot weather, favor shade.** Avoid the hottest part of the day, usually 1 to 5 PM; stay in shade when possible (NWS Blacksburg).
- **No sun-direction rule is given here.** Which slope gets morning or afternoon sun depends on where on Earth you are, and none of the sources open for this entry gives a directional rule.

### 7. Ground, Water Table and Bugs
- **Avoid waterlogged ground.** If you must stay in a marsh or any area with standing water, build the bed raised above the water, as in the Army's swamp bed (ATP 6-37), with allowance for high water.
- **Thick, brushy, low ground harbors more insects.** Inspect the site for venomous snakes, ticks, mites, scorpions and stinging ants (ATP 6-11).

### 8. Lightning
- **No outdoor place is safe in a thunderstorm.** NWS: "you are NOT safe outside." Only a **building with electricity and plumbing**, or a **metal-topped vehicle with the windows closed**, counts as a safe shelter (NWS). Tents, open-sided picnic shelters, dugouts and small buildings without plumbing or electricity do not. A tent "offers NO protection."
- **If you cannot reach a building or hard-topped vehicle, you can only lessen the risk** (NWS):
  - Avoid open fields, hilltops and ridge tops. Stay away from tall, isolated trees or other tall objects. In a forest, stay near a lower stand of trees.
  - Spread out if you are in a group.
  - Stay away from water, wet items such as ropes, and metal objects such as fences and poles.
  - Cave entrances are dangerous and small overhangs can let lightning arc across the gap. Going well into a cave "increases your safety somewhat," but caves can still be struck (NWS).
  - NPS Yosemite: stay away from peaks, ridges, caves, water and open areas; seek shelter in low forested areas, but avoid tall, solitary trees.
- **Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder** before resuming activity (NWS).
- **Low ground vs. flooding:** NWS says to camp in a valley, ravine or other low area if you are camping in the open, but ravines flood (step 1). This guide's judgment: low, flat ground above any drainage, not the channel itself.

### 9. Avalanche, Rockslide and Mudslide Ground
- **Never camp in an avalanche zone** (NPS Mount Rainier). The Army also lists avalanche, rockslide and mudslide areas as site hazards (ATP 6-9). Do not shelter at the foot of a steep snow slope (this guide's judgment). Snow-shelter siting is in `emergency-shelter-types.md`.

## Failure Modes & Fixes

### Flash Flood During the Night
- **Recognize it.** Rising water, rumbling, or heavy rain upstream (distant rain can be channeled to you, NWS Blacksburg).
- **Move immediately** to higher ground (NWS). Survival matters more than gear (general practice).
- **Fix.** Choose the next site above any high-water marks and out of channels, narrow valleys and ravines.

### Hazard Tree Falls
- **Recognize it.** A loud crack followed by a crash, or you wake under a tree with a fresh break.
- **Move away from the hazard.** If winds are up, head to a clearing out of reach of falling trees (USFS). A branch that falls and misses means the tree may be compromised (general practice).
- **Fix.** Do not re-shelter under a tree with fresh breaks, a lean, or the weak-tree signs listed above.

### Cold Spot in a Hollow
- **Recognize it.** Your shelter is colder than expected and the ground feels very cold (general practice).
- **Fix.** Move to a slightly higher spot out of the hollow at first light. If anyone shows hypothermia signs, see [`../../06_first_aid/entries/thermal-injury-hypo-hyperthermia.md`](../../06_first_aid/entries/thermal-injury-hypo-hyperthermia.md).

### Wind Damage
- **Recognize it.** Strong gusts shake or tear the shelter. NWS Blacksburg: a tent or camper is not safe in high winds.
- **Fix.** Rebuild in a more sheltered spot (behind natural screens, ATP 6-12) and face the back into the wind.

### Insects and Snakes from Low, Brushy Ground
- **Recognize it.** Biting insects increase sharply or you find snakes, ticks or stinging ants (ATP 6-11).
- **Fix.** Move to higher, more open ground, and inspect the new site before settling.

### Caught Outside in a Thunderstorm
- **Recognize it.** Thunder is audible; lightning is visible.
- **Fix.** Get to a building with plumbing and electricity or a hard-topped metal vehicle if one is reachable. If not, leave ridges, peaks, open fields and isolated tall trees, stay out of water, spread out, and wait 30 minutes after the last thunder (NWS; NPS Yosemite).
