## Goal & When To Use

Cloudy water is water with dirt, silt or other bits floating in it. Clearing it first is not treatment. It makes the real treatment work. Use this entry when:

- Water from a creek, pond, rain catchment or tank looks cloudy, muddy or colored.
- You plan to use bleach, UV or a filter. Cloudy water soaks up chlorine, blocks UV, and clogs filters [1][4][6].
- You plan to boil. Clearing improves the taste and look of water [6].

**Clearing does not make water drinkable.** The Wilderness Medical Society says clarification "does not disinfect if used alone" [6]. The Yellow Book says it reduces germs "but not enough to ensure potable water" [3]. Something from the next section must still follow. Survey guide: [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md#step-zero-cloudy-water). Where to find water in the first place: [water-sourcing-and-storage.md](water-sourcing-and-storage.md).

## ⚠ CONTAMINATION — what this method does and does NOT handle

> ⚠ **Settling and pre-filtering clear the water. They do NOT disinfect it and remove no chemicals. Always follow with boiling, a disinfectant, or a rated filter.**
>
> - **What it removes:** large particles such as silt, sand and dirt [3][6]. It may cut some germs, because many cling to particles, but not enough to count as treatment [3][6].
> - **Threats that REMAIN after clearing:** bacteria, viruses and parasites in the water, including *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*; and all chemicals, fuel, toxins and dissolved substances. Sedimentation does not remove suspended fine or dissolved material [6].
> - **What must follow (pick one):**
>   - **Boil:** rolling boil 1 minute; 3 minutes above 5,000 ft or if you are not sure of your elevation (EPA 5,000 ft; CDC 6,500 ft). Kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa including *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*. Does NOT remove chemicals.
>   - **Filter:** an absolute 1 micron or smaller filter removes parasites such as *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*; an absolute 0.3 micron or smaller filter removes bacteria and parasites. Neither removes viruses, so near people, animals or sewage add a disinfectant after filtering. A cloth or coffee filter is not a micron-rated filter.
>   - **Chemical:** plain unscented bleach kills bacteria and viruses in clear water. It does NOT reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*. Doses are in the next section.
>   - **UV pen:** only on water you have already cleared. See [uv-treatment.md](uv-treatment.md).
> - **The boundary:** no single method covers chemicals plus every germ. Clearing covers neither. If the water smells of fuel or chemicals, or is floodwater, stop: see [floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md](floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md).

## Steps

1. **Collect carefully.** Take water from moving or clear spots, away from scum, shoreline mud and animals. Use a clean container [2].
2. **Settle.** Set the container down and leave it still for about an hour, or until sediment has formed on the bottom [6]. Muddy water should stand for a while so silt and debris settle [5]. Large particles like silt and sand settle by gravity [3].
3. **Decant.** Pour or siphon off the clear water from the top into a second clean container. Leave the sludge on the bottom behind. Do not shake or tip the first container.
4. **Strain.** Pour that clear water through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter [1][2][6]. Use a tightly woven cloth, or fold it double (WMS lists a coffee filter, fine cloth, or double-thickness closely woven cloth) [6].
5. **Look again.** If it is still cloudy or tea-colored, repeat steps 2 to 4. Count tea-colored water as cloudy for dosing (this guide's rule, from [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md#step-zero-cloudy-water)).
6. **Now treat it.** Pick one method in the CONTAMINATION block above and follow its own entry in full.

### If you will use bleach

Doses below are copied from [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md#bleach). The method itself is in [chemical-treatment-chlorine-iodine.md](chemical-treatment-chlorine-iodine.md). Use plain, unscented household bleach, under a year old.

- **Clear water:** 8 drops of 6% bleach per gallon (6 drops of 8.25%); 2 drops per quart or liter.
- **Cloudy, colored or very cold: double the dose** [1][2]. For 6% bleach that is 16 drops per gallon. EPA and CDC both say to double the bleach if the water is cloudy, colored or very cold [1][2]. CDC adds "murky" to the list [1].
- Stir, then wait **30 minutes**. A faint chlorine smell afterward is the check. No smell: add the same dose again and wait 15 more minutes [2].
- Cold water and Giardia possible: boil or filter instead (this guide's rule). Cold water slows the chemical reaction [6].

CDC explains why: "Cloudy water contains substances that neutralize chemical disinfectants" [3]. Doubling the dose is what EPA and CDC advise [1][2]. Clearing first is still the better move, because the doubled dose does nothing for the other problems of cloudy water.

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

**Clear first. Then treat. Never stop at clear.**

- **Order:** settle, decant, strain, then boil, add bleach, or filter. Cloudy water can rapidly clog filters, so strain before the filter [6]. Carry a backup treatment in case a filter clogs (general practice).
- **Time:** about 1 hour of settling, or until sediment has formed [6].
- **Cloth:** clean. A dirty cloth, container or hands put germs back in. Wash cloths with soap and water and let them dry in the sun between uses (general practice).
- **Dose:** if bleach is your treatment and the water is cloudy, colored or very cold, use the doubled dose from the section above [1][2].
- **Crypto risk (livestock upstream, sewage, a camp nearby):** clearing does not help here. Boil, or use a filter rated absolute 1 micron or smaller [1].

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Failure | Cause | Recovery |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Water looks clear, so you drink it | Clear water can still carry germs [3][6] | Always follow with boiling, a disinfectant, or a rated filter. |
| Still cloudy after one pass | Fine particles that settle slowly | Settle longer and strain again. If it will not clear, double the bleach dose or boil [1][2]. |
| Sludge stirred back into the water | Poured too fast or tipped the container | Let it settle again. Pour slowly from the top. |
| Dirty cloth or container | Germs added after clearing | Use a clean cloth and container. Treat afterward. |
| Strained water used in a UV pen while still hazy | Particles shield germs from UV [4] | Clear it again or boil. |
| Filter clogs right away | Particles loaded the filter [6] | Pre-strain and settle first. Carry a backup. |
| Cloudy water has a fuel, chemical or sewage smell | Chemical or sewage contamination | Clearing does not help. Do not drink it. See [floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md](floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md). |
| Tempted to add alum, ash or baking powder to clump the dirt | The WMS describes these clumping agents, but this guide gives no amounts [6] | Skip them. Settle and strain instead (this entry's rule). |
