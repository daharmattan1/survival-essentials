## Goal & When To Use

A UV pen or lamp is a small, battery-powered light that you put in a container of water for a timed cycle. The light damages the genetic material of germs so they cannot make you sick. CDC says portable UV units can kill parasites, bacteria and viruses "in small amounts of clear water" [1]. Use one when:

- You have a working unit, its label, and batteries you trust.
- The water is already clear, or you can make it clear first (see [pretreatment-settling-and-prefiltering.md](pretreatment-settling-and-prefiltering.md)).
- You need a small amount of water for drinking. CDC's Yellow Book describes these units as treating about 1 to 2 liters (1 to 2 quarts) at a time [2].

This is a convenience tool, not a first choice. Boiling is still the broadest single method (see [boil-disinfection.md](boil-disinfection.md)). Survey guide: [02_water.md](../../../02_water.md#sun-and-uv-light).

**UV pens are not SODIS.** SODIS uses plain sunlight in a clear plastic bottle and is covered in [solar-sodis.md](solar-sodis.md). A pen or lamp makes its own UV light inside the water and runs on a timer. The two do not share times or rules. Do not use SODIS hours for a pen, and do not use a pen cycle for a sun bottle.

## ⚠ CONTAMINATION — what this method does and does NOT handle

> ⚠ **UV pen or lamp: inactivates germs in small amounts of CLEAR water only. Does NOT remove chemicals. Leaves no protection in the water afterward. A weak dose cannot be seen or tasted.**
>
> - **Threats handled (clear water, full labeled dose):** bacteria, viruses, and the parasites *Giardia* and *Cryptosporidium* [1][2][6]. Efficacy depends on dose and exposure time [2]. Independent test data is lacking for many individual units [5], so boil when in doubt.
> - **Threats that REMAIN:**
>   - **Chemicals, fuel, metals, toxins:** UV does not remove them [4]. Water that smells of fuel or chemicals cannot be made drinkable [3].
>   - **Germs shielded by particles:** cloudy water lets germs hide from the light [1][2][6].
>   - **Germs added after treatment:** UV leaves no lasting residual in the water, so it does not prevent recontamination during storage [2][6].
> - **Boil (for comparison):** rolling boil 1 minute; 3 minutes above 5,000 ft or if you are not sure of your elevation (EPA 5,000 ft; CDC 6,500 ft). Boiling kills bacteria, viruses and protozoa including *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*. It does not remove chemicals.
> - **Filter (for comparison):** an absolute 1 micron or smaller filter removes parasites such as *Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*. An absolute 0.3 micron or smaller filter removes bacteria and parasites. Neither removes viruses. UV does reach viruses, which is why a filter plus UV covers more than either alone.
> - **Chemical (for comparison):** bleach and iodine kill bacteria and viruses but do not reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*. UV does cover it.
> - **The boundary:** no single method covers chemicals plus every germ. UV covers the germs, in clear water, if the dose is delivered. It covers no chemicals.

## Steps

1. **Check the water first.** If you can see cloudiness, silt or floating bits, clear it. CDC: "Filter water before using UV light to disinfect it" [1][3]. Let it settle, pour off the clear top, and strain it through a clean cloth, paper towel or coffee filter. See [pretreatment-settling-and-prefiltering.md](pretreatment-settling-and-prefiltering.md).
2. **Still cloudy or colored? Do not use UV.** Switch to boiling. Particles "block germs from the light" [1].
3. **Check the device.** Read the label. Check that the batteries are fresh or charged. Look for cracks or fogging on the lamp window and for dirt on it (general practice).
4. **Keep the light off your eyes and skin.** The lamp is UV-C, and UV-C can injure skin and eyes after a few seconds of exposure [7]. The eye injury causes severe pain and a feeling of sand in the eyes [7]. Follow the label's instructions to protect skin and eyes [7]. Run the lamp only inside the water, and do not look at it lit or point it at anyone (general practice).
5. **Fill a clean container.** Use the container size and water amount that the label allows. The label is the rule for volume. CDC's Yellow Book describes portable units as delivering "a metered, timed dose" [2].
6. **Run the labeled cycle in full.** Follow the label for how long, how to stir or swirl, and how deep to put the lamp. The Minnesota Department of Health says correct power delivery, water agitation and contact time are all needed [5]. Do not cut the time short. This entry gives no times of its own, because they differ by device.
7. **If the cycle was interrupted or you doubt the dose, run a full labeled cycle again, or boil the water instead** (this entry's rule). Do not make up your own times. The Yellow Book gives no time for extra doses [2].
8. **Store it clean.** Pour into a clean, covered container, or drink from the treated container. Nothing in the water protects it afterward [2][6].
9. **After use.** Dry the device as the label directs and store it with spare batteries (general practice).

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

**Clear water, the full labeled dose, and charged batteries cannot be skipped or shortened.**

- **Clarity:** the water must be clear before the lamp goes in. Filter or settle first [1][3][5]. A cloudy sample is not treated water, even if the light came on.
- **Dose and time:** follow the device's label exactly, including stirring. Efficacy depends on dose and exposure time [2][5].
- **Batteries:** use fresh or charged batteries. You cannot see a weak dose, and CDC's Yellow Book lists as a limit that you "cannot know if devices are delivering required UV doses" [2]. If you doubt the batteries or the lamp, boil the water instead (this entry's rule).
- **Eyes and skin:** UV-C can injure skin and eyes after a few seconds [7]. Follow the label's protection instructions and keep the lit lamp in the water.
- **Chemical risk (fuel or chemical smell, oily sheen, floodwater, "do not drink" notice):** UV does nothing about it. Use bottled water or another source [3][4]. See [floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md](floodwater-and-chemical-contamination.md).
- **Crypto risk (livestock upstream, sewage, a camp nearby):** UV is listed as effective against *Cryptosporidium* at the full dose [2][6]. If you have any doubt about the device, boil.

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Failure | Cause | Recovery |
|---------|-------|----------|
| Water looks cloudy or has particles | Particles shield germs from the light [1][2][6] | Settle and strain. If still not clear, boil instead. |
| Light is dim, blinks or will not start | Weak batteries or a damaged unit | Replace or charge the batteries. If the unit is not working, boil. Do not guess at a dose (this entry's rule). |
| You cannot tell whether the dose was delivered | Dose cannot be seen or tasted [2] | Boil, or run a full labeled cycle again (this entry's rule). |
| Stopped the cycle early or lamp touched the container side or bottom | Interrupted or poor exposure (general practice; follow the device manual) | Start over with a full labeled cycle. |
| Water is dirty again after treatment | No lasting residual [2][6] | Treat it again; keep it in a clean, covered container. |
| Water had fuel, chemical, or sewage smell | Chemicals; UV does not remove them [3][4] | Do not drink it. Find another source. |
| Large or dirty batch of water | Pens are made for small amounts [1][2] | Use boiling or a filter plus a disinfectant. |
| Taste or look is odd, you assume UV "fixed" it | UV does not change taste or appearance [6] | Do not use taste or look as a test. Looks and smell do not show germs. |
