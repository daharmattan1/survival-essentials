## The Principle

**STOP** is a pause before you act. The Forest Service teaches it as **Stop, Think, Observe, Plan**. The FAA's survival course teaches the same letters as **Sit, Think, Observe, Plan**. The Army manual FM 21-76 does **not** use STOP; it uses the word SURVIVAL (Size up the situation, Undue haste makes waste, Remember where you are, Vanquish fear and panic, Improvise, Value living, Act like the natives, Live by your wits). The idea is the same in all three: slow down before you move.

For a person who is lost, the **Plan** step usually ends in **staying put and being found**. The Forest Service says: "stay calm, stay put," and calls panic your greatest enemy.

## Why It Holds

Acting in haste costs you. FM 21-76 says you may make a wrong move when you react quickly without thinking, and "if you act in haste, you may forget or lose some of your equipment" and "become disoriented so that you don't know which way to go." Its advice: "Don't move just for the sake of taking action."

Stress is part of it. The Army manual describes the body preparing to fight or flee: breathing rate increases and muscle tension rises. It also says a person cannot maintain that level of alertness indefinitely, and that fear can immobilize a person or, used well, make them cautious. A short deliberate pause is how you stop reacting and start choosing. FAA CAMI says to take a few moments to collect your thoughts, drink some water, attend to any injuries, and then make a plan.

## How To Apply

**Stop (or Sit).** As soon as you realize you may be lost or in trouble, stop. Stay calm. Do not keep walking "to see if it gets better."

**Think.** Recall how you got here and what landmarks you should have seen (Forest Service). Ask:
- Am I in immediate danger right now (fire, flood, traffic, falling objects)?
- Is anyone hurt? FM 21-76: "If injured, first aid has top priority no matter what climate you are in."
- How much daylight is left?

**Observe.** Inventory what you have (FAA CAMI says to inventory your survival equipment and attend to any injuries). Note the weather and the time. Use a compass if you have one, and do not wander (Forest Service).

**Plan.** Choose one or two small actions for the next hour. The plan for a person who is lost:

- **If you are not confident of your route, stay put.** If it is nightfall, you are injured, or you are near exhaustion, stay in place (Forest Service).
- **If you know the way and it is safe, you may retrace your steps** (NPS). Where the guidance differs, take the more cautious choice and stay.
- **Call 911 if you have service.** NPS lists what to tell them: GPS coordinates from your phone, the trail or area where you started, your last known location, a description of your surroundings and landmarks, the clothing you are wearing, the equipment you carry, and any medical conditions.
- **Stay put.** NPS: "Changing location will make it difficult for authorities to find you. Move only if there is an immediate threat to your life." For children, NPS teaches Hug-A-Tree: find a tree, hug it, and stay put.
- **Be found.** Blow a whistle three times, rest, and blow three times again. This saves energy compared to yelling. Flash a flashlight in the SOS pattern (three quick, three slow, three quick) sparingly, and flash a mirror at planes and helicopters (NPS graphic).
- **Shelter and conserve energy.** Find a place out of sun, rain, or snow. Put on extra layers. Wait patiently for help (NPS). Do not build a fire if fire risk is high or it is wildfire season (NPS).
- **Keep the group together.** NPS: set a pace that works for everyone so no one is left behind.

**Before you go (the part that makes being found work).** Fill out a trip plan and leave it with someone who is not going. Forest Service: tell them the trailhead you are parking at, your route, and when you expect to return. NPS also lists a map, whistle, mirror, bright clothing, and the 10 Essentials.

**Apply STOP whenever** you realize you are lost, when panic starts, or when the situation changes and you face a new decision. (Repeating it at those points is this guide's judgment.)

## Common Failure

- **Skipping STOP and running.** Acting in haste is how people lose gear and get more lost (FM 21-76).
- **Observing without acting.** Finish with a first small task, such as putting on layers.
- **Planning something too big,** such as "walk out by dark." Pick the next hour.
- **Walking farther "to get a signal" or to find a road at dusk.** The sources say to stay put at nightfall, when injured, or when exhausted.
- **Staying put but not being findable.** Staying put does not guarantee a quick rescue. NPS says help can take hours or days. It makes you findable; keep warm, dry, and visible, and signal.
- **No trip plan.** Without one, nobody knows to look for you.
