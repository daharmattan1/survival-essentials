## The Principle

Survival time without each resource is roughly exponential: you have about **3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in harsh conditions, 3 days without water, and 3 weeks without food**. This order is your *action priority* — effort applied to the wrong problem first is wasted effort you cannot replace. Most people invert this priority and chase food while dehydration or hypothermia is the clock running fastest.

## Why It Holds

The human body is a machine with constraints. Loss of core function (airway, circulation, temperature) kills in minutes. Dehydration concentrates blood, thickens fluids, shuts down sweat cooling, and induces shock. Starvation is a weeks-long decline in energy and cognition. The **fastest-acting threat gets the finite calories, attention, and decision-making power you have right now**. Digging a root system for a meal when you are hypothermic or severely dehydrated is work that hastens death, not delays it.

The 3-hour window for shelter is conditional on **harsh conditions** (cold, wind, wet). In mild weather, you have more time. In active cold or storm, exposure becomes lethal in hours.

## How To Apply

When you stop and assess (via STOP: Stop, Think, Observe, Plan), use this checklist in order:

1. **Air first.** If breathing is compromised (throat constriction, severe altitude, smoke, aspiration), stop everything else. Clear airway, seek elevation or fresh air.
2. **Shelter second.** In harsh conditions (cold, wind, wet, exposure), find or build shelter *before* anything else. Hypothermia and hyperthermia kill faster than any other field threat.
3. **Water third.** A person in dehydration shock cannot think, move, or make fires. Water. Three days is the ceiling; in heat, it is hours.
4. **Fire/warmth fourth.** Once shelter is started, fire/heat management extends your time and enables water treatment.
5. **Signaling fifth.** Search and rescue depends on you being locatable. A whistle, mirror, or smoke fire does not compete with the first four.
6. **Food last.** It is the least urgent. You will feel hungry immediately but will not die for weeks.

**Reorder only when the threat changes.** If shelter is built and you're not hypothermic, water moves up. If you're rescued or reached safety, food becomes relevant again.

## Common Failure

The most common failure is **the provision bias** — people spend enormous effort on food (traps, foraging, fishing) while dehydration or exposure is actively killing them. This happens because hunger is *immediate sensation* (you feel it an hour in) while dehydration or hypothermia is *delayed perception* (you stop thinking clearly before you realize the threat). Panic also drives people to "do something," and food-gathering feels productive even though it is the wrong priority.

A second failure is **ignoring harsh-condition shelter**. In mild weather, you can sleep on the ground. In cold or wet conditions, a tarp and a fire-lay between you and the ground is the difference between survival and death. People often delay shelter to gather more food or scout for rescue, then run out of daylight and energy.
