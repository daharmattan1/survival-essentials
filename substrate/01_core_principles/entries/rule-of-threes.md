## The Principle

The rule of threes is a **teaching heuristic**, a memory aid for setting priorities. It is not a measurement, and there is no exact physiology behind it. In its common form it says a person can last about **3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in harsh conditions, 3 days without water, and 3 weeks without food**. Use it for the *order* it implies: what stops the body fastest gets attention first.

Sources give different numbers:

- The FAA's survival course lists the rule with **air 3 minutes, shelter 3 hours, rest 30 hours, water 3 days, food 30 days** (FAA CAMI, AC 120-47).
- The Army manual FM 21-76 does **not** state the rule. It says only that you can live "several weeks" without food, and warns: "You must not count on being able to go for days without food."
- Colorado State University Extension says the body can survive for weeks without food but only a few days without water.

Real times depend on heat, cold, wet, age, health, and effort. The FAA itself says life expectancy without water, in extreme conditions, is three days. Treat every number here as a rough order of magnitude, never a countdown you can plan around.

## Why It Holds

The ordering holds because of simple logic, not a formula: a blocked airway or severe bleeding works in minutes, losing body heat works in hours, going without water works in days, and going without food works in weeks. That sequence is this guide's judgment; the sources above support the water and food ends of it. The rule has limits. It leaves out injury and being found, and it hides how much the numbers move with conditions. FM 21-76 notes that in some places the need for shelter takes precedence over the need for food and possibly even water, because prolonged exposure to cold causes exhaustion and weakness.

## How To Apply

Use this order, which adds what the rule leaves out. It matches the priority order in `01_core_principles.md`; airway, choking and smoke first aid is in `06_first_aid.md` (Not breathing, and choking).

1. **Immediate danger.** Get away from fire, rising water, traffic, a gas smell, carbon monoxide, or anything about to fall. Then stop and think.
2. **Injury, severe bleeding, or trouble breathing.** FM 21-76: "If injured, first aid has top priority no matter what climate you are in." Call 911 if you can.
   - **Severe bleeding:** press directly on the wound with a clean cloth or clothing and keep pressing. If extremely severe bleeding continues, a tourniquet can be used until medical help arrives (MedlinePlus). Stop the Bleed teaches three actions: direct pressure, packing the wound, and a tourniquet. Take a course before you need it.
   - **Choking or a blocked airway:** call 911 and give first aid. For an adult who cannot speak, MedlinePlus gives 5 back blows between the shoulder blades, then 5 abdominal thrusts, repeated, and CPR if the person becomes unconscious. For a pregnant person or someone too large for abdominal thrusts, use chest thrusts instead; see `06_first_aid.md`. A hands-on first aid course teaches this properly.
   - **Smoke:** get low and get out. Crawl low, under the smoke, to your exit; once you are out, call 911 (Ready.gov). Before opening a door, feel the door; if it is hot or smoke is coming around it, keep it shut and use your second exit.
   - **Severe altitude illness:** **descend.** The CDC Yellow Book calls descent "urgent and mandatory" in most cases of high-altitude pulmonary edema, and says to initiate descent for anyone suspected of high-altitude cerebral edema. It also says never to ascend to sleep at a higher altitude when you have symptoms. Do not climb in search of better air.
3. **Temperature and shelter.** Get out of the wind, rain, and sun. See `04_shelter.md` and `03_fire.md`.
4. **Being found.** Call 911 if you have service. Signal, and stay where searchers will look. NPS: "Changing location will make it difficult for authorities to find you."
5. **Water.** Keep drinking. Do not ration water you have; ration effort.
6. **Fire, as a tool.** It helps with warmth, signaling, and treating water. It also creates carbon monoxide and wildfire risk.
7. **Food.** Last. Not eating works in weeks, though strength and judgment fall long before that.

**Reorder when the situation changes.** FM 21-76: "Change your survival pattern to meet your immediate physical needs as the environment changes." If you are warm and sheltered, water moves up. If you are rescued, nothing on this list matters.

## Common Failure

- **Treating the numbers as exact.** "I have three days" is a guess, not a promise. Heat, injury, and exposure can cut it sharply.
- **Skipping the injury.** People start a fire, a shelter, or a food hunt before dealing with a bleeding wound or wet clothes. Injury comes first.
- **Doing the wrong thing for "breathing first".** Climbing with altitude sickness or staying in smoke makes things worse. Descend; get low and get out.
- **Moving just to do something.** FM 21-76: "Don't move just for the sake of taking action."
- **Putting food ahead of water, warmth, or being found** because it feels productive. This is this guide's judgment, not a studied effect.
