## Goal & When To Use

**Fire goes outside the shelter, never inside.** Do not burn a fire, stove, lantern, charcoal, grill or heater inside a tent, vehicle, garage or house. Cutting a vent hole or opening a flap, door or window does not make it safe (see the Fire Safety Boundary below).

A fire lay is the **structure** that holds fuel and controls airflow. The lay you choose determines whether a flame bridges to full burn or dies mid-tinder. Use a **teepee lay** for speed (it burns well even with wet wood); a **log-cabin lay** for a large, hot fire and a quick bed of coals; a **lean-to lay** when the wind will push the flame into the wood; and a **star lay** to conserve fuel when someone is awake to tend it. Choose before ignition — a lay built mid-burn collapses and wastes tinder.

## Steps

### 1. Prepare the Site and Platform

- Choose a flat, dry spot out of the wind and away from snow-laden trees [ATP 5-5]. Stay **at least 15 feet from tents**, vegetation and low-hanging branches, with vertical clearance of at least 3 times the flame height [Smokey Bear].
- Clear a circle **about 10 feet across** of litter and anything that burns [Smokey Bear]. In a true emergency the bare minimum is **3 feet (1 meter) in diameter** of exposed soil [ATP 5-6].
- Remove leaves, moss, pine needles and duff down to bare soil so the fire cannot spread along the ground [ATP 5-6].
- Remove or avoid porous or wet rock; it can explode when heated [ATP 5-6; FM 21-76 ch. 7].
- Level the site if it is sloped so fuel does not slide off [ATP 5-6]. Avoid steep slopes, rotten stumps and logs, and dry grass or leaves [NPS Grand Teton].
- In deep snow, build on a base of green logs: two layers, the top layer crosswise, large enough to hold the whole fire [ATP 5-6]. Lay tinder on a dry base so the fine fibers do not soak up moisture [ATP 5-13]; on wet ground, raise the lay on flat wood or dry nonporous rock.
- If you want a reflector, build it before the fire gets hot: a wall of nonporous rock or stacked green logs behind the fire directs the heat [ATP 5-6, 5-7].
- Put water and a shovel next to the fire before you light it [NPS Grand Teton].
- **Light from the upwind side** to protect the fragile first flame [ATP 5-32; FM 21-76 ch. 7].

### 2. Teepee Lay (Speed & Single-Handed Ignition)

**When to use:** quick ignition, boiling water, emergency signals, wet wood. Best for beginners.

- Gather tinder into a **fist-sized bundle**, fluffed so air passes through.
- Lay thin kindling (pencil-lead to pencil thickness) in a **teepee cone** around the tinder, leaning inward.
- Leave a **gap on the side you will light from** (the upwind side), large enough for your hand to reach the tinder core.
- Add heavier kindling (up to thumb thickness) around the cone, keeping the cone shape.
- Lean fuel logs (at least forearm thick) against the outside of the cone once the kindling is burning well. As the teepee burns, the outside sticks fall inward and feed the fire [ATP 5-24].

**Failure mode:** if the cone collapses when you add fuel, you've stacked kindling too loosely. Rebuild with stiffer twigs and tighter stacking angles.

### 3. Log-Cabin Lay (Sustained Burn & Larger Fuel)

**When to use:** you want a lot of light and heat, a quick large bed of coals for cooking, or a base for a signal fire, and larger fuel is plentiful [ATP 5-28].

- Place two logs **parallel**, a few inches apart.
- Lay two logs perpendicular across them, creating a square frame.
- Repeat, alternating direction, building a cribbed-log wall a few layers high. Each layer goes on perpendicular to the one below, smaller toward the top. Logs sit in the groove of the layer below (not tied).
- At the center-bottom, leave a **hollow chamber** for tinder and kindling.
- Build an inverted cone of kindling in the hollow, leaving gaps for airflow upward.
- Place tinder in the center, leaving an **air gap** on the upwind side to light through.
- Light the tinder from the upwind side; flame rises through kindling into the log layers, which burn from inside-out.

**Advantage:** the log frame supports fuel weight; airflow comes up through the center, not across the top. Oxygen entering through the stack gives a lot of light and heat and a quick, large bed of coals [ATP 5-28].

**Failure mode:** if logs are stacked too tightly or tinder is wet, flames choke on lack of oxygen. Leave gaps between logs; dry damp tinder against your body before use.

### 4. Lean-To Lay (Wind Protection)

**When to use:** the wind is steady and you want it to push the flame into the wood, not away from it.

- Push a **green stick into the ground at about a 30-degree angle**, with the end of the stick **pointing in the direction the wind is blowing** [ATP 5-25; FM 21-76 ch. 7].
- Place the tinder deep under the stick.
- Lean kindling against the stick, leaving gaps for air.
- **Light the tinder from the upwind side.** As the kindling catches, add more kindling and then fuel [ATP 5-25].

**Failure mode:** if the kindling is packed tight, the flame has no air and smothers. Add kindling by the handful with gaps between it [ATP 5-14].

### 5. Star Lay (Fuel Economy & Night Watch)

**When to use:** fuel is scarce or you want a small fire, and someone is awake to tend it. The star fire **must be constantly tended**; it works best with hardwood fuel [ATP 5-30].

- Arrange logs like **wheel spokes**, radiating from a central point, with the inner ends meeting in the fire.
- Build a small teepee cone of kindling and tinder at the hub, where the logs meet.
- As the inner ends burn down, **slide each log inward** like a clock mechanism, keeping the burn zone at the center.
- Little setup is needed, and you can burn larger pieces you cannot break down for other lays [ATP 5-30].

**Advantage:** you control the burn rate by how fast you advance the logs.

**Failure mode:** if logs are too thick and poorly dried, the center burns slowly and you spend all night pushing. Use well-seasoned wood; dead wood that snaps when broken is dry [ATP 5-21].

## Failure Modes & Fixes

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Flame dies within seconds | Tinder is wet; kindling has no airflow | Dry tinder against your body (between layers of clothing). Rebuild: loosen kindling spacing; keep a gap on the upwind side. |
| Smoke but no flame in the tinder | Wind is quenching the ignition site, or kindling is smothering the tinder | Shield the tinder with your body; light from the upwind side; use a lean-to lay; move to a wind-protected location. Add kindling in handfuls with gaps. |
| Kindling catches but doesn't ignite fuel | Gap between kindling and fuel logs is too large; fuel is damp | Close the gap between kindling and fuel; use dry, dead fuel; add smaller fuel first and graduate to larger logs. |
| Coals form but flames die when you step back | Airflow is blocked (too-dense tinder or wet kindling choking the draft) | Add small sticks between tinder and kindling to create ventilation tunnels; do not pack materials. |
| Logs roll off the lay when heavy fuel settles | Lay is unstable (especially lean-to or teepee) | For teepee: re-tighten the cone angle. For lean-to: seat the angled stick firmly in the ground. Do not wedge the lay with wet or porous rock. |
| Fire spreads unexpectedly in wind | Embers escape and ignite nearby debris | Clear a larger circle (about 10 feet across, down to bare soil) before lighting. Never leave the fire unattended, even for a moment: a small breeze can spread it. If wind is rising and you cannot keep the fire contained, put it out. |

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION: Fire Safety Boundary

**Fire goes OUTSIDE the shelter. Never inside.**
- **NEVER burn a fire, camp stove, lantern, charcoal, grill or heater inside a tent, vehicle, garage or house.** Carbon monoxide (CO) cannot be seen or smelled, and it can kill people who are asleep or who have been drinking before they notice symptoms [CDC CO; CPSC 5008].
- **A vent hole, an open tent flap, an open door or an open window does NOT make an indoor fire safe.** In Georgia in March 1999, six people died in two zipped tents, one with a propane stove burning and one with a charcoal grill. The CDC report states that opening tent flaps, doors, or windows is insufficient to prevent CO build-up from these devices [CDC MMWR 48(32)]. Do not cut a vent hole and burn a fire inside a shelter.
- CPSC: never use charcoal to cook or provide heat inside enclosed areas such as tents, campers, vans, cars, trucks, homes, garages or mobile homes [CPSC Charcoal]. CDC: never use a portable gas camp stove indoors, and never run a generator inside a home or garage even with doors and windows open [CDC CO]. CPSC: do not use fuel-burning portable heaters or lanterns while sleeping in tents, campers or other vehicles [CPSC 5008].
- Warm yourself without burning fuel inside: a better shelter, ground insulation, dry layers, and a reflector wall behind a fire built **outside**, at least 15 feet from the tent [Smokey Bear; ATP 5-7].
- **Know the symptoms** of CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, vomiting, sleepiness, confusion. They are easily mistaken for a cold or flu [CPSC 5008]. If anyone has them, get everyone into fresh air immediately and call emergency services.

**Clearance (before lighting):**
- Stay **at least 15 feet from tents**, vegetation and low-hanging branches, clear a circle **about 10 feet across**, and keep vertical clearance of **at least 3 times the flame height** [Smokey Bear]. The emergency minimum is 3 feet of bare soil [ATP 5-6].
- Note the wind. Light from the upwind side [ATP 5-32], and place the fire so embers blow away from tents and stored fuel.
- Keep the fire small, with water and a shovel beside it [Okanogan-Wenatchee NF; NPS Grand Teton].

**Unattended fire (the rule):**
- **Never leave a campfire unattended, even for a moment.** Even a small breeze can quickly cause a fire to spread [NPS Grand Teton]. Fully extinguish it before you leave or go to sleep [Okanogan-Wenatchee NF].
- Extinguish with **drown, stir, drown, feel**: pour water on the fire until the hissing stops, stir the water into the embers and dirt, add water again until everything is cool, then hover the back of your hand over the ashes to feel that the embers are cool [Smokey Bear]. Check for embers under surrounding rocks [NPS Grand Teton]. Before leaving, make sure the ground under the fire is cool to the touch [ATP 5-31].

**Wildfire risk (dry seasons):**
- Check for burn bans and campfire restrictions before lighting any fire outdoors [Okanogan-Wenatchee NF]. Where one is in force, use a legal alternative (an outdoor stove if the order allows it, or layers). A survival emergency is your call, but do not assume the law will exempt you.
