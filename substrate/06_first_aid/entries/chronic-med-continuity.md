# Chronic Medication Continuity

**This entry describes commonly-taught field response for interruptions in life-sustaining chronic medications when professional care is unavailable. It is NOT medical advice and does not replace physician guidance. Seek professional medical evaluation as soon as possible if any medication is missed or a dose is uncertain.**

---

## Recognize

### Insulin interruption (Type 1 diabetes, insulin-dependent Type 2)

**Signs of insulin lack (hyperglycemia → diabetic ketoacidosis / DKA):**
- Elevated blood glucose (>180 mg/dL if meter available)
- Increased thirst and frequent urination
- Fatigue, weakness
- Headache
- Fruity-smelling breath (acetone)
- Nausea, abdominal pain
- Rapid, shallow breathing (Kussmaul respiration)
- Confusion, loss of consciousness (severe DKA)

**Signs of too much insulin (hypoglycemia):**
- Low blood glucose (<70 mg/dL if meter available; or absence of meter)
- Shakiness, sweating, palpitations
- Anxiety, irritability
- Intense hunger
- Confusion, slurred speech, altered mental status
- Seizures or loss of consciousness (severe hypoglycemia)

---

### Anticoagulant interruption (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran)

**Signs of insufficient anticoagulation (clot risk):**
- Swelling, warmth, redness in a limb (DVT risk)
- Chest pain or shortness of breath (PE risk)
- Acute weakness or speech difficulty (stroke risk)
- Absence of obvious bleeding does not mean the drug is working

**Signs of too much anticoagulation (bleeding):**
- Easy bruising
- Bleeding from gums, nosebleeds
- Blood in urine or stool
- Vomiting blood (hematemesis)
- Severe abdominal pain with dark stools
- Intracranial bleeding (worst-case): severe headache, confusion, loss of consciousness, focal weakness

---

### Seizure medication interruption (phenytoin, lamotrigine, valproate, levetiracetam, etc.)

**Signs of inadequate anticonvulsant levels:**
- Increased seizure frequency or breakthrough seizures
- Aura (if the person typically has one): visual changes, tingling, déjà vu
- Altered mental status or irritability
- Status epilepticus (repeated seizures without recovery between) — **MEDICAL EMERGENCY**

---

### Cardiac medications interruption

**Beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, digoxin:**
- Chest pain or pressure
- Shortness of breath
- Palpitations, irregular heartbeat
- Dizziness, fainting
- Elevated blood pressure (if available to measure)

---

### Corticosteroid interruption (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone for adrenal insufficiency or chronic inflammation)

**Signs of steroid withdrawal / adrenal crisis:**
- Severe fatigue, weakness
- Dizziness, orthostatic hypotension (low BP on standing)
- Nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain
- Fever
- Mental status changes, confusion
- Rapid weak pulse, shock (in severe cases)

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

- **Severe hypoglycemia:** confusion, seizures, loss of consciousness (diabetic emergency)
- **Suspected DKA (insulin lack):** fruity breath, Kussmaul respiration, severe nausea/abdominal pain, confusion
- **Active bleeding (anticoagulant excess):** hematemesis, blood in stool, severe bruising, neurologic symptoms
- **Deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism signs** (anticoagulant lack): limb swelling/warmth, chest pain, shortness of breath
- **Status epilepticus:** repeated seizures without full recovery between (anticonvulsant lack)
- **Cardiac chest pain or palpitations** with hemodynamic instability (cardiac med lack)
- **Adrenal crisis:** severe hypotension, altered mental status, vascular collapse (steroid lack)

**These are medical emergencies requiring professional intervention (insulin, IV fluids for DKA, ICU monitoring for bleeding, advanced cardiac support).**

---

## Respond

### INSULIN INTERRUPTION (Type 1, or insulin-dependent Type 2)

**If medication is interrupted or supply is limited:**

1. **Check blood glucose if meter available.** This guides all subsequent decisions.
   - If <70 mg/dL (low): treat hypoglycemia immediately (see Step 5).
   - If 70–180 mg/dL (normal-to-slightly-elevated): hold insulin, monitor closely, continue diet.
   - If >180 mg/dL (high): consider modest insulin dose only if supply allows continuation.

2. **Maintain carbohydrate intake consistently.** Skipping meals without insulin causes dangerous swings. Eat small, regular meals (even if rationed).

3. **Rationing insulin (if supply is limited):**
   - **Do NOT skip doses entirely.** Insulin lack causes DKA within 24–48 hours.
   - **Reduce mealtime insulin slightly** (e.g., 80% of normal dose) while cutting carbs slightly, or use a split (morning > evening) to stretch supply.
   - This is empiric and carries risk. Ideal: resume normal dosing when supply allows.

4. **Monitor for DKA signs:** fruity breath, Kussmaul breathing, nausea, abdominal pain, confusion. If present, this exceeds field management → **evacuate for IV fluids and professional care.**

5. **Treat hypoglycemia immediately (if available):**
   - **Glucose tablets, candy, honey, sugar solution:** 15 grams of fast carbs (e.g., 3–4 glucose tablets, 1 tablespoon honey, 150 mL juice).
   - **Re-check glucose in 15 minutes.** If still <70 mg/dL, repeat.
   - **After glucose rises, eat protein and complex carbs** (peanut butter, nuts, bread) to prevent rebound low.
   - **Severe hypoglycemia (unconscious or seizure):** if available, **glucagon injection** (0.5–1 mg IM/IV). If no glucagon, place honey/glucose gel under the tongue and recovery position. Evacuate after stabilization.

6. **Evacuation:** if medication cannot be restarted or DKA/severe hypoglycemia develops, evacuate urgently.

---

### ANTICOAGULANT INTERRUPTION (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran)

**If medication is skipped or supply is limited:**

1. **Resume as soon as possible.** Most anticoagulants have a therapeutic window; missing 1–2 doses risks clot formation, but extended gaps (>7 days) gradually reduce anticoagulation.

2. **Do NOT skip and then double-dose** (increases bleeding risk). Resume normal dosing.

3. **Monitor for clot signs:** unilateral leg swelling/warmth (DVT), chest pain/shortness of breath (PE), transient neurologic symptoms (stroke).

4. **Monitor for bleeding:** unusual bruising, bleeding gums, blood in urine/stool, intracranial symptoms (worst-case).

5. **Dietary consistency (if warfarin):** vitamin K intake affects warfarin effect. Maintain consistent diet; major changes worsen anticoagulation.

6. **Limitations of field management:** INR (clotting time) cannot be checked without lab access. Field management is supportive:
   - **If bleeding:** stop activity, apply pressure, elevate, monitor vital signs.
   - **If clot suspected:** rest the limb, monitor for progression, watch for systemic signs.
   - **Evacuate for:** major bleeding, neurologic symptoms, PE signs, or DVT with rapid swelling.

---

### SEIZURE MEDICATION INTERRUPTION (phenytoin, lamotrigine, valproate, levetiracetam, etc.)

**If medication is missed or supply is limited:**

1. **Resume as soon as possible.** Breakthrough seizures can occur within hours to days of missed doses (drug-dependent).

2. **Do NOT abruptly stop and restart.** If supply is very low:
   - **Maintain the current regimen** (e.g., full dose every other day) rather than skipping randomly.
   - This keeps blood levels more stable than on/off dosing.

3. **Monitor for aura / prodromal symptoms:** many people sense an oncoming seizure. If aura occurs, move to a safe place (away from water, sharp objects, heights).

4. **Seizure protocol (if one occurs):**
   - **Protect from injury:** move away from hazards, place on side (recovery position) to keep airway open.
   - **Time the seizure** (duration matters for medical decisions).
   - **Do NOT restrain or put objects in the mouth** (can break teeth, cause aspiration).
   - **Allow recovery:** most seizures last 30 seconds to 2 minutes; afterward the person is confused but usually regains consciousness.

5. **Status epilepticus (repeated seizures without recovery):**
   - **Medical emergency.** If seizures continue for >5 minutes or recur without full recovery, **evacuate for emergency anticonvulsant therapy (IV lorazepam or phenytoin).**

6. **Limitations:** field restarts of anticonvulsants carry risk of overdose (phenytoin toxicity: tremor, ataxia, confusion). Resume at the known home dose if supply allows; do not experiment with new dosing.

---

### CARDIAC MEDICATION INTERRUPTION (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, calcium channel blockers, digoxin)

**If medication is missed or supply is limited:**

1. **Resume as soon as possible.** Most cardiac meds can be missed 1–2 doses without acute crisis, but longer gaps risk arrhythmia or myocardial ischemia.

2. **Monitor heart rate and rhythm (if possible):** elevated HR (>100 bpm at rest) or palpitations suggest inadequate beta-blockade or ACE inhibitor effect.

3. **Monitor for chest pain, dyspnea, or syncope.** These are signs of inadequate cardiac control.

4. **Limit exertion** while medication is interrupted (exercise increases cardiac demand and risk).

5. **Rationing (if supply is limited):**
   - **Do NOT skip doses randomly.** Cardiac meds work best with consistent dosing.
   - **Reduce to 80% of normal dose** (if possible) rather than skipping, to extend supply.
   - **Resume full dose** once supply is restored.

6. **Digoxin toxicity (if overdose suspected):** nausea, visual disturbances (yellow tinge), arrhythmia, weakness. Stop digoxin and do not resume until evaluated.

7. **Evacuation:** if chest pain, severe dyspnea, syncope, or hemodynamic instability develops, evacuate for professional monitoring and medication adjustment.

---

### CORTICOSTEROID INTERRUPTION (prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone for adrenal insufficiency)

**If medication is missed (especially for diagnosed adrenal insufficiency):**

1. **Resume as soon as possible.** Adrenal insufficiency requires continuous steroid coverage; interruption can cause crisis within 24–48 hours.

2. **If supply is limited (rationing):**
   - **Do NOT reduce the dose arbitrarily.** Steroids for adrenal insufficiency are replacement, not anti-inflammatory; the dose is physiologic, not optional.
   - **Contact professional care for guidance.** If no contact is possible and supply is critically low, continue the current regimen; do not skip doses to extend supply.

3. **Monitor for adrenal crisis:** severe fatigue, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, hypotension, altered mental status. This is a medical emergency.

4. **Adrenal crisis protocol:**
   - **Lie down** (lower head to heart level to maintain brain perfusion).
   - **Fluid and salt intake:** drink water with salt (or electrolyte solution if available).
   - **Rest completely** (exertion worsens crisis).
   - **Evacuate urgently for IV hydrocortisone.** Adrenal crisis requires hospital-level steroid replacement and IV fluids.

5. **Chronic steroids (for inflammation, autoimmune conditions):** longer withdrawal is safer than acute adrenal insufficiency, but abrupt stoppage can cause rebound inflammation. Resume at the known home dose.

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT skip insulin doses entirely.** DKA develops within 24–48 hours and is life-threatening.
- **Do NOT double-dose anticoagulants to "catch up."** Risk of major bleeding.
- **Do NOT stop seizure meds abruptly.** Status epilepticus can result.
- **Do NOT reduce cardiac meds without professional guidance.** Arrhythmia and ischemia risk.
- **Do NOT ration adrenal-insufficiency steroids.** These are physiologic replacement, not optional.
- **Do NOT attempt to IV-administer oral medications.** Crushing/dissolving tablets and injecting them causes sterile abscess or vascular injury.
- **Do NOT assume "a little without medication is OK."** The risks accumulate; maintain as much continuity as possible.

---

## Med Continuity

**Medication interactions and field adjustments:**
- **Diarrhea + oral meds:** absorption is reduced. Oral antibiotics, anticonvulsants, and anticoagulants may not reach therapeutic levels if severe GI upset is present.
- **Fever + anticonvulsants:** high fever can temporarily lower drug levels (increased metabolism). Monitor for breakthrough seizures.
- **Alcohol (if available) + any medication:** alcohol worsens side effects and impairs metabolism. Avoid.
- **Herbal or over-the-counter supplements:** many interact with chronic meds (e.g., St. John's Wort reduces warfarin effect). Stick to known medications.
