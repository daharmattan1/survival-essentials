# Carbon Monoxide Poisoning

**This entry describes commonly-taught field response for carbon monoxide poisoning. It is general information, NOT medical advice. If a CO alarm sounds, or anyone may have CO poisoning, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911 (or the local emergency number) from outside. Do not go back in until responders say it is OK.**

---

## Recognize

Carbon monoxide (CO) is a gas made whenever fuel burns. It is colorless, practically odorless and tasteless, so you cannot see, smell or taste it [1][5]. More than 400 Americans die each year from CO poisoning that does not come from fires [1].

### Symptoms

- Headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain and confusion are the most common [1][2].
- Early symptoms look like the flu [3], but without a fever [3][8].
- Heavy exposure causes confusion, loss of coordination, fainting and unconsciousness. People who are not rescued will likely die [1][3].
- Babies and small children may only be fussy, feed poorly or act oddly [6].
- Infants, older adults, and people with heart disease, anemia or breathing problems get seriously ill more easily [1][6].

### Clues that point to CO and not a cold

- **More than one person is sick at once,** with similar complaints and no fever [8].
- **Pets get sick first.** Pets often show symptoms before people do [6].
- **Symptoms ease when you leave the building** and come back when you return [5].
- **Something is burning or running nearby:** a generator, grill, camp stove, heater, furnace, gas stove or oven, or a car in or near the garage [3][5].

### Sleeping and drunk people

People who are asleep or have been drinking alcohol can die from CO before they ever have symptoms [1][2]. Never wait for symptoms if an alarm sounds.

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

- **A CO alarm is sounding.** Never ignore it. Get outside immediately, then call 911 [3][4].
- **Anyone has the symptoms above while fuel is burning nearby.** Treat it as CO until proven otherwise [3].
- **Anyone is drowsy, confused, faint, vomiting or unconscious.** Call 911 and say it may be CO poisoning [2][3].
- **Several people or pets are sick at once** [6][8].
- **"You could lose consciousness and die if you stay in the home"** is how CPSC puts it. Do not stay to investigate [3].
- **No phone?** Go to a neighbor's home and call from there [3].

---

## Respond

1. **Get everyone out into fresh air right away.** Do not look for the source [3]. Do not stop to collect things first (general practice). Take anyone who can walk. Move to open air, away from the building, garage or vehicle (general practice).
2. **Call 911 or the fire department from outside** or from a neighbor's home [3][4].
3. **Count heads once you are outside.** If someone is missing or unconscious inside, tell the 911 dispatcher exactly where (general practice). CPSC says do not reenter until emergency responders give permission [3]. This entry's rule goes one step further: do not go back in for people, pets or belongings, because a rescuer who goes in can become the next victim.
4. **Poison center: 1-800-222-1222** (America's Poison Centers [9]) can advise while you wait for 911. It never replaces 911 when anyone is sick. The CDC lists 911 and the poison center as the calls to make [2], and MedlinePlus lists the same number [6].
5. **Everyone who was exposed should see a doctor,** even if they feel better. Say you suspect CO [3]. Do not let a drowsy, dizzy or confused person drive (general practice).
6. **Do not go back in until the fire department or emergency responders say you may** [3]. Do not turn the fuel-burning equipment back on until it has been checked (general practice).
7. **While you wait,** keep everyone together in the open air and keep watching for confusion or drowsiness. Leave rescue of anyone still inside to the responders (this entry's rule).

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT go back inside** until responders give permission [3]. This entry's rule applies that to going in after people, pets or belongings too.
- **Do NOT wait for symptoms** when an alarm sounds [1][3].
- **Do NOT open windows to "air it out" and stay.** A vent or cracked window is not a fix. Leave [3].
- **Do NOT use a generator, grill, camp stove, heater or running car** in a tent, vehicle, garage, snow shelter or home as a way to stay warm. The only exception is a vented fireplace or stove in working order [1][2][3].
- **Do NOT assume you are fine because you do not smell anything.** CO has no smell [1][5].
- **Do NOT call it the flu** if others are sick too, or if the symptoms ease when you leave the building [5][8].
- **Do NOT drive yourself** if you feel dizzy or drowsy (general practice).
- **Do NOT leave a sleeping or intoxicated person inside** after an alarm. They can die without waking [1][2].

---

## Prevention (short)

Prevention has its own detail in the urban and fire guides. Start here:

- **Rule for this repo:** nothing burns inside a tent, vehicle, garage, snow shelter or home, except a vented fireplace or stove in working order. (The rule is written out in full in [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md#fire-indoors-in-tents-and-in-vehicles-the-carbon-monoxide-rule).) CDC and CPSC name charcoal grills, lanterns, camp stoves, gas ranges used for heat, and cars left running in garages [2][3].
- **Generators run outdoors only,** at least 20 feet from windows, doors and vents [1][2][4]. Never in a home, basement or garage, even with doors and windows open. Opening a door or window is not a fix [3].
- **Put battery or battery-backup CO alarms** on every level and outside each sleeping area [3][4][7]. Replace them following the maker's instructions or every 5 years [1]. Check the batteries when you change the clocks each spring and fall [1].
- **Power-out plan:** see [`power-outage-bug-in.md`](../../09_urban_survival/entries/power-outage-bug-in.md) and the guides: [09_urban_survival.md](../../../09_urban_survival.md#carbon-monoxide-and-fire-heat-light-and-cook-without-hurting-anyone) and [03_fire.md](../../../03_fire.md#fire-indoors-in-tents-and-in-vehicles-the-carbon-monoxide-rule).
