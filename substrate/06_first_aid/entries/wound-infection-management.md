# Wound Infection Management

**This entry describes commonly-taught field response for wound infection when professional care is unavailable. It is NOT medical advice. Seek professional care (antibiotics, wound assessment, tetanus update) whenever possible.**

---

## Recognize

### Early signs (hours to 24 hours post-wound)
- Increasing warmth, redness, swelling around the wound (beyond the immediate injury zone)
- Pain that worsens rather than improves over time
- Fluid (yellow/greenish pus, serous ooze) from the wound
- Red streaking radiating from the wound site (lymphangitis — **RED FLAG**)
- Mild fever (≤38.5°C / 101°F)

### Progressive signs (24–72 hours)
- Swollen lymph nodes (nodes in armpit, groin, neck) on the drainage path
- Increased pus production
- Wound has a foul or unusual odor
- Fever rising or persistent
- Localized abscess (pus pocket under the skin)

### Severe signs / tissue infection (≥72 hours)
- Spreading red streaks (cellulitis extending away from wound)
- Swelling of limb or face
- Fever ≥38.5°C / 101°F
- Pus is frank, copious, or mixed with blood

### Sepsis signs (medical emergency — **EVACUATE IMMEDIATELY**)
- Fever AND chills, OR hypothermia (<36°C / 96.8°F) — low core temp is worse than high
- Tachycardia (rapid pulse >100 bpm at rest, or >20 beats above baseline)
- Tachypnea (rapid breathing >20 breaths/min)
- Altered mental status (confusion, lethargy, irritability)
- Hypotension (dizziness on standing, weak pulse, slow capillary refill)
- Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
- Skin mottling or purple discoloration (especially fingers, toes, lips)

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

**Evacuate / call EMS immediately if ANY of these are present:**

- **Red streaking** radiating from the wound (lymphangitis)
- **Fever ≥38.5°C (101°F)** that is rising or persistent >12 hours
- **Swollen lymph nodes** on the drainage path + wound infection signs
- **Spreading cellulitis** (redness expanding beyond the wound)
- **Sepsis signs:** fever/chills/hypothermia + tachycardia + altered mental status + hypotension + mottled skin
- **Pus pocket** (localized abscess) that is enlarging
- **Foul-smelling discharge** with increasing systemic signs (fever, malaise)
- **Wound involves a joint, tendon, or deep tissue** (risk of necrotizing infection)
- **Puncture wound** with deep penetration or contamination (high anaerobic infection risk)

**Sepsis is life-threatening. Do not attempt field management of systemic infection.**

---

## Respond

### Step 1: Clean and irrigate (all wound infections)
- **Wash hands** with soap and water (or alcohol if water unavailable).
- **Remove dressings** carefully; do not pull away dried scabs.
- **Irrigate with clean water** (boiled and cooled, or the cleanest available): pour gently over the wound to flush debris and bacteria. Continue for ≥2 minutes.
- **Gently scrub** the wound perimeter with a clean cloth to remove crusted debris (do NOT deepen the wound).
- **Re-irrigate** after scrubbing.
- **Drain if accessible:** if a small pus pocket is visible and accessible, apply warm compresses (clean cloth soaked in warm, boiled water) for 10–15 minutes, then gently express pus (do NOT squeeze hard or dig). This is field drainage; deep abscesses require professional incision.

### Step 2: Topical antiseptic (if available)
- **Apply iodine (Betadine), hydrogen peroxide, or saline** to the wound bed after irrigation.
- **Iodine:** leave on for 30–60 seconds, then rinse with clean water if the wound is small (iodine can irritate for extended contact). Use sparingly on face/groin.
- **Hydrogen peroxide:** foam indicates active cleaning; use once and rinse (repeated H2O2 damages healthy tissue).
- **Saline or boiled water:** if no antiseptic, saline or clean water is the floor — irrigation alone reduces infection risk significantly.

### Step 3: Dressing
- **Pack the wound** loosely with clean gauze or clean cloth (slightly moist, not soaked). Do NOT seal a contaminated wound air-tight — anaerobes thrive without oxygen.
- **Cover with a secondary dressing** (cloth or gauze) to keep bacteria out.
- **Change dressing daily** or when wet/soiled. Repeat irrigation at each change.

### Step 4: Systemic signs (fever, spreading redness)
- **Monitor core temperature** every 6–8 hours if possible.
- **Rest and hydration:** infection accelerates fluid loss and energy demand.
- **Anti-inflammatory:** if available, ibuprofen (400–600 mg every 4–6 hours) may help manage fever and swelling; does not treat infection.
- **Elevate the limb** (if wound is on arm/leg) to reduce swelling.
- **Antibiotic use (field):**
  - If you have antibiotics (amoxicillin, doxycycline, cephalexin, clindamycin — from a previous prescription or emergency kit):
    - **Oral antibiotics for mild localized infection:** start immediately if fever is ≥38°C or spreading redness is present, even if no formal diagnosis.
    - **Dosing (amoxicillin example):** 500 mg three times daily; (doxycycline) 100 mg twice daily. If unsure, use lowest reasonable dose.
    - **Duration:** continue for ≥7 days, even if symptoms improve (stopping early risks relapse).
    - **Do NOT rely on antibiotics alone.** Continue irrigation and dressing changes.
  - If no antibiotics and fever/spreading signs appear: **this exceeds field care — evacuate**.

### Step 5: Watch for progression to sepsis
- **If fever climbs or new systemic signs appear** (chills, rapid heartbeat, confusion, mottled skin), treat as sepsis emergency: **STOP field care and evacuate immediately.**
- **Sepsis worsens hourly.** Delay costs lives.

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT seal a contaminated wound with tape or bandage.** Anaerobic bacteria (which cause necrotizing fasciitis) thrive without oxygen. Always leave a contaminated/infected wound open to air or loosely packed.
- **Do NOT ignore red streaking.** Lymphangitis (red lines) is a sign the infection is invading lymph vessels. This is NOT a minor infection and requires systemic antibiotics or evacuation.
- **Do NOT squeeze or aggressively drain an abscess.** Pus pockets can rupture deeper into tissue, spreading infection. Gentle warm compress + minimal expression only; large abscesses need professional drainage.
- **Do NOT skip irrigation.** Topical antiseptic without irrigation leaves bacteria and debris behind.
- **Do NOT delay evacuation for fever ≥38.5°C.** A rising fever suggests systemic infection; field care cannot manage it.
- **Do NOT continue oral antibiotics if vomiting or diarrhea is severe.** This suggests gastrointestinal involvement or systemic illness — escalate to evacuation.
- **Do NOT apply tourniquets or compress a limb with cellulitis.** This can trap bacteria and worsen tissue damage.
- **Do NOT re-use old dressings.** Clean cloth/gauze only.

---

## Med Continuity

Not applicable to wound infection (no chronic medication interruption). If the patient is on anticoagulants (warfarin, apixaban) or immunosuppressants, infection progresses faster — lower the threshold for evacuation.
