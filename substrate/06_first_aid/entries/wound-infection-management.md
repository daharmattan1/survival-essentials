# Wound Infection Management

**This entry describes commonly-taught field response for wound care and wound infection when professional care is unavailable. It is NOT medical advice. Seek professional care (wound assessment, tetanus update, and any antibiotic decision) whenever possible. Call 911 for any sign of sepsis.**

---

## Recognize

### Signs of infection [1]
- Warmth or redness around the wound
- Painful or throbbing sensation
- Swelling
- Fever
- A red streak extending from the wound
- Pus-like drainage

This entry gives no temperature or pulse cut-offs, because the sources opened for it give none. Treat any fever with a wound as a reason to contact a clinician (this guide's judgment).

### Signs of sepsis, a life-threatening reaction to infection [3]
- Cold, damp or sweaty skin
- Confusion or disorientation
- Severe pain or discomfort
- Fever, chills, or feeling very cold
- Racing heart or a weak pulse
- Shortness of breath

Almost any infection can lead to sepsis, and it can quickly cause tissue damage, organ failure and death [3].

---

## ⚠ RED FLAGS / EVACUATE

**Call 911 or get emergency care now if:**
- **Any sign of sepsis** (above), especially confusion, trouble breathing or a racing heartbeat with a wound or infection. CDC calls sepsis a life-threatening medical emergency; the 911 step is this guide's judgment [3].
- **Bleeding is severe or cannot be stopped** (the page's example: after 10 minutes of pressure). See `bleeding-control-and-shock.md` [1].
- **The person cannot feel the injured area, or it does not work properly,** or there are other serious injuries [1].

**Contact a clinician right away (get to care) if:**
- A red streak spreads from the wound, or there are other signs of infection (warmth, redness, throbbing pain, swelling, fever, pus) [1].
- The wound is large or deep, more than a quarter inch deep, on the face, or reaches bone [1].
- It is a human or animal bite [1].
- It was caused by a fishhook or rusty object, or by stepping on a nail or similar object [1].
- An object or debris is stuck in the wound [1].
- Tetanus protection may be out of date (see Respond, Step 4) [1][2].

## Respond

### Step 1: Clean a minor wound [1]
1. Wash your hands with soap and water.
2. Apply direct pressure to stop any bleeding.
3. **Cut:** wash the cut thoroughly with mild soap and water.
4. **Puncture:** rinse under running water for 5 minutes, then wash with soap. Look for objects in the wound but do not poke around. If you find one, leave it in place and get care.
5. Do not assume a wound is clean because you cannot see dirt. Always wash it.

### Step 2: Dress it [1]
1. Apply antibacterial ointment if you have it.
2. Cover with a clean bandage that will not stick to the wound.
3. Change the dressing when it is wet or dirty (general practice). Wash your hands first.

### Step 3: A major wound
**Do not try to clean a major wound,** especially while it is still bleeding. Control bleeding first and get medical help [1]. Do not remove a long or deeply stuck object, and do not push or pick at debris in a wound. Cover any exposed body part with clean material until help arrives [1].

### Step 4: Tetanus [2]
CDC's rule for a person who has completed the tetanus vaccine series:
- **Clean, minor wound:** a booster is needed if the last dose was 10 or more years ago.
- **Dirty or major wound** (puncture, wound with dirt, soil, feces or saliva, bite, burn, crush injury, frostbite): a booster is needed if the last dose was 5 or more years ago.
- **Unknown history, never vaccinated, or an incomplete series:** vaccinate, for any wound.
- Antibiotics do not prevent tetanus.

A clinician or pharmacist gives the booster, so plan for it when you reach care.

### Step 5: Watching an infected wound
- **Contact a clinician** for any infection sign listed above [1].
- **Pus or a pus pocket:** do not squeeze, cut or drain it yourself (this guide's rule). Get it seen.
- **Rest and drink fluids** while you arrange care (general practice).
- **Pain and fever medicine:** an over-the-counter pain reliever may ease pain or fever. Follow the label dose only. It does not treat infection.
- **Antibiotics are a clinician's decision.** Do not self-start leftover or kit antibiotics, and do not guess a dose.
  - If a clinician has already prescribed an antibiotic for this wound, take it exactly as prescribed and finish the course unless they tell you otherwise.
  - Keep cleaning and dressing the wound either way. Antibiotics never replace wound care.

### Step 6: If sepsis signs appear
Stop field care and call 911 or get emergency care now [3]. Sepsis is a medical emergency [3].

---

## ⚠ DO NOT

- **Do NOT ignore a red streak spreading from a wound.** Get medical care now [1].
- **Do NOT try to clean a major wound** or poke at debris or a deeply stuck object [1].
- **Do NOT remove an object stuck in a wound.** Get care [1].
- **Do NOT breathe on an open wound** [1].
- **Do NOT squeeze, cut or drain a pus pocket yourself** (this guide's rule).
- **Do NOT assume a wound is clean** because you cannot see dirt. Always wash it [1].
- **Do NOT skip tetanus** if the last dose is 10 or more years ago (clean wound) or 5 or more years ago (dirty or major wound) [2].
- **Do NOT rely on antibiotics to prevent tetanus** [2].
- **Do NOT self-start an antibiotic** or guess a dose. If a clinician prescribed one, **do NOT wait out severe vomiting or diarrhea** while taking it: contact a clinician, because the person may be getting sicker or may not be keeping the medicine down (general practice).
- **Do NOT re-use old dressings** (general practice).

---

## Med Continuity

This entry gives no medicine-specific guidance and no dosing. If the person takes a blood thinner or a medicine that weakens the immune system, tell the clinician about the wound early. Medicine questions go to a clinician, a pharmacist or 911. See `chronic-med-continuity.md`.
