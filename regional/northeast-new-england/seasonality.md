# Northeast / New England: Seasonality Calendar

> A month-by-month orienting overview of foraging, hunting, growing and hazard windows for **this biome only**
> (zones about 4b to 6b; Concord, NH is the reference point). It tells you *what category is roughly in season
> now* so you know which category index to open.
>
> Biome profile: [`_index.md`](_index.md). Food decision hub: [`food_hub.md`](food_hub.md).

---

> ## ⚠ This is an ORIENTING OVERVIEW, NOT an identification source
>
> This calendar lists *what category to look for*, never *what you may eat*. **Every consumption decision runs
> through the category index's positive-ID gate** (`edible-plants/`, `mushrooms/`, `game-animals/`, `crops/`),
> the look-alike matrix that leads it and, for fungi, the 9-point [mushroom doctrine](mushrooms/_index.md) (an
> expert who has examined the specimen in hand comes first). Seasonal availability is **not** identification:
> ramps and false hellebore come up in the same weeks, chanterelle and jack-o'-lantern fruit in the same
> weeks, and deadly galerina grows on the same wood in the same season as the honey mushroom. The calendar will
> never tell them apart; the entry's gate does. **Positive ID or do not consume.**
>
> **Where each window comes from.** Every window below is stated in the linked entry, and each entry's sources
> are listed in that entry. Most wild-plant and mushroom entries say **no source opened gives a date for
> Concord, NH or for New York**, and several say peak weeks for New England were not found. Treat each window as
> the pattern the sources support, not a calendar for your woods. No month here comes from memory: where an
> entry gives no window, this page says so.
>
> **Hunting seasons change every year** and differ by state, zone and unit. The dates below are the ones the
> state agencies published for the year the entry names (mostly 2026). **Check the state agency.** Hunting and
> trapping are different legal systems; the entries cover hunting only. No cited page gives a survival
> exemption. Not legal advice.
>
> **Frost.** Concord's average last spring freeze is May 8 and first fall freeze October 3 (NWS, 1991 to 2020;
> see [`_index.md`](_index.md)). UNH calls Memorial Day a conservative estimate of the last frost for central
> NH. Your elevation and town move these dates.

## Month by month (Concord-area baseline)

Every line below links the entry that states it.

**January**: Lean season for wild food: the wild-plant and mushroom entries give no January window. Stored
food: potatoes keep about 5 to 8 months and cabbage 4 to 6 months in a cold cellar, per the sources in the
[potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md) and [cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)
entries. Connecticut hare season has a second window, January 1 to 31 ([snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md)).
Ticks: the blacklegged tick can be active at any temperature above freezing ([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)).
Cold water and wet, windy days: [hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md).

**February**: Maple sap can start in southern Maine from mid-February and in Pennsylvania and southern New York
from mid to late February (the Vermont maple research center describes its own season as January to April)
([maple](crops/entries/maple-sap-and-syrup.md)). Hare seasons run on (Massachusetts to Feb 27); the 2025-26 New York
grouse season ran to Feb 28 ([snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md),
[ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md)). Stored food only for wild-plant purposes.

**March**: Sap season: early to mid-March in northern areas and at higher elevations, mid-March to late April in
northern Maine; it ends when the buds break ([maple](crops/entries/maple-sap-and-syrup.md)). Hare seasons end
March 14 (Vermont, except units D and E, to March 31), March 15 (New York Northern Zone, 2025-26) or March 31
(Maine, New Hampshire) ([snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md)). Ticks on any thaw
([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)). Not yet planting time for most gardeners here: the crop entries count
planting from **your** last-frost date (see April and May).

**April**: Sap season continues in northern Maine to late April ([maple](crops/entries/maple-sap-and-syrup.md)).
Fiddleheads emerge from late April in southern Maine ([fiddleheads](edible-plants/entries/fiddlehead-ostrich-fern.md)):
the first time, someone who knows ostrich fern confirms it in person; boil 15 minutes or steam 10 to 12 minutes, throw out the water, bracken is not food. Cool-season crops go in
**before** the last frost: cabbage and kale set out about 4 weeks before it (UNH), potato seed 1 to 2 weeks before
it (UNH) or 2 to 4 weeks (UVM), in soil of 40 to 50 F depending on the source. Count from **your** last-frost date:
UNH calls Memorial Day a conservative estimate for central NH, and Concord's average last freeze is May 8
([potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md), [cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)). American dog ticks are active April through
September in Vermont ([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)). Water hemlock is most toxic in spring, and false
hellebore comes up very early ([toxic plants](hazards/entries/toxic-plants-new-england.md)). Northeast waters stay cold
well into spring ([hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md)).

**May**: Ramp leaves are up early to mid-May in Maine, and **false hellebore and lily of the valley share the
same woods**: read the [matrix](edible-plants/_index.md) first ([ramps](edible-plants/entries/ramps-allium-tricoccum.md)).
Lily of the valley flowers in May and June (same entry). Fiddleheads mid to late May in far northern Maine
([fiddleheads](edible-plants/entries/fiddlehead-ostrich-fern.md)). UMaine's planting chart puts potatoes in central
Maine from May 1 to June 1 and cabbage plants from May 1 to August 1 ([potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md),
[cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)). Blacklegged tick nymphs peak in May and June
([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)). Connecticut requires a life jacket on everyone aboard a paddlecraft
October 1 through May 31 ([hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md)).

**June**: Fiddleheads run to early June ([fiddleheads](edible-plants/entries/fiddlehead-ostrich-fern.md)). King
bolete is reported from June ([king bolete](mushrooms/entries/king-bolete-boletus.md)). Poison hemlock flowers June to
August ([toxic plants](hazards/entries/toxic-plants-new-england.md)). Tick nymphs still peak. Hypothermia is not only a
spring and fall problem: New Hampshire Fish and Game reported hikers with hypothermia on Mt. Lafayette in June 2026,
in cold, wet, rainy conditions ([hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md)).

**July**: Chanterelle season begins (July to September, Bessette), **and jack-o'-lantern fruits July to October in
the same weeks**: cut every stem, orange inside means discard ([chanterelle](mushrooms/entries/chanterelle-new-england.md)).
Ramp flowers open late June to July after the leaves have withered ([ramps](edible-plants/entries/ramps-allium-tricoccum.md)).
UNH says to sow fall vegetables from late July through August; UVM says to sow kale in midsummer, counting back from
your average first fall frost ([cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)). Treat all surface water
([`../../substrate/02_water/_index.md`](../../substrate/02_water/_index.md)).

**August**: Wild blueberry: Maine's commercial harvest is in August and highbush berries mature in August; managed
barrens are farmland, so get permission ([wild blueberry](edible-plants/entries/lowbush-blueberry-vaccinium.md)).
Chanterelle and king bolete continue. Fall cabbage and kale sowing ends with the month (UNH) and UMaine's
cabbage-plant window ends August 1 ([cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)).

**September**: Honey mushroom season: fall, and **deadly galerina fruits on the same wood in the same season**
([honey mushroom](mushrooms/entries/honey-mushroom-armillaria.md)). Chanterelle to September, king bolete to October.
Potatoes: dig about mid-September when the soil and air cool and most tops have yellowed (UNH)
([potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md)). First fall freeze comes mid-to-late September in northern New
Hampshire and from early September in parts of Vermont ([cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)).
Hunting, 2026 dates from the entries: Maine expanded archery deer from Sept 12 (designated areas); New Hampshire,
Connecticut and Vermont (designated zones, antlerless only) archery from Sept 15; northern New York early bow from
Sept 27; ruffed grouse in Maine and Vermont from Sept 26 (New York 2025-26 from Sept 20 in the north); snowshoe hare
in Maine and Vermont from Sept 26
([deer](game-animals/entries/white-tailed-deer-new-england.md), [ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md),
[snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md)).

**October**: Concord's average first fall freeze is **October 3** (NWS); UNH says early to mid-October in central and
southern NH ([`_index.md`](_index.md), [potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md)). King bolete, honey mushroom and
jack-o'-lantern are all still fruiting ([king bolete](mushrooms/entries/king-bolete-boletus.md),
[honey mushroom](mushrooms/entries/honey-mushroom-armillaria.md), [chanterelle](mushrooms/entries/chanterelle-new-england.md)).
Store potatoes dark and cool, at about 40 F, not below 38 F and not above about 46 F (the sources' ranges run 37 to 46 F), and cabbage cold and humid
([potato](crops/entries/potato-short-season.md), [cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)).
Adult ticks: activity rises in October and November ([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)). Hunting, 2026 dates
from the entries: grouse and hare open in New Hampshire Oct 1, in Massachusetts and Connecticut (grouse) Oct 17;
deer archery in Vermont Oct 1, Maine Oct 3 to 30, Massachusetts Oct 5; New Hampshire muzzleloader Oct 31; New York
regular firearms (northern zone) Oct 24 ([deer](game-animals/entries/white-tailed-deer-new-england.md),
[ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md), [snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md)).
Connecticut's life-jacket rule for paddlecraft restarts October 1
([hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md)).

**November**: Honey mushroom: *A. gallica* fruits late, in cold weather in late fall and early winter
([honey mushroom](mushrooms/entries/honey-mushroom-armillaria.md)). Kale keeps growing in frost, and the manifest
window runs to hard frost ([cabbage and kale](crops/entries/cabbage-and-kale-cold-hardy.md)). Hunting, 2026 dates
from the entries: firearms deer in New Hampshire Nov 11 to Dec 6, Vermont Nov 14 to 29, Maine Nov 2 to 28,
Massachusetts shotgun Nov 30 to Dec 12, Connecticut Nov 18 to Dec 8; Massachusetts grouse ends Nov 28 and
Connecticut grouse Nov 30; Connecticut hare opens Nov 21; hunting is not allowed on Sundays in Massachusetts
([deer](game-animals/entries/white-tailed-deer-new-england.md), [ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md),
[snowshoe hare](game-animals/entries/snowshoe-hare.md)). Adult ticks still active
([ticks](hazards/entries/ticks-new-england.md)).

**December**: Honey mushroom into early winter (same entry). Deer: New Hampshire and Vermont archery to Dec 15,
Maine muzzleloader to Dec 5 statewide (Dec 7 to 12 in some districts), Massachusetts primitive firearms Dec 14 to 31, Connecticut muzzleloader Dec 9 to 31
and private-land archery to Dec 31 ([deer](game-animals/entries/white-tailed-deer-new-england.md)). Ruffed grouse in
Maine, Vermont and New Hampshire to Dec 31 ([ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md)). No wild-plant
window; live on stored food and conserve energy. Cold water and wet, windy days
([hypothermia](hazards/entries/cold-water-and-shoulder-season-hypothermia.md)).

## What this calendar does not cover

- **Ruffed grouse and hare in New York** are given for 2025-26 only; check the New York DEC for the current year.
- **Rhode Island** has no state agency source in any entry; check the state agency.
- **Spruce grouse** is a protected look-alike of the ruffed grouse: no open season in Maine or New Hampshire, state
  endangered in Vermont and New York. Do not shoot a grouse you cannot tell from one
  ([ruffed grouse](game-animals/entries/ruffed-grouse.md)).
- **Plant collecting on public land** is limited: Vermont state forests and parks need a special written permit to
  uproot or cut a plant (berries, fruits, seeds, nuts and mushrooms for personal use excepted), and New Hampshire
  DRED properties bar removing plants. Check the land manager.
- **Squirrel, cottontail, turkey, bear, moose and raccoon seasons** have no entry here; use the state agency and
  the [food guide](../../05_food.md).
- **Wild plants and mushrooms with no entry** are not cleared by their absence. No calendar here gives a window
  for them.
