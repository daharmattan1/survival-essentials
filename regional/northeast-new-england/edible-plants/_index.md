## ☠ Look-alike matrix (read BEFORE the edible exemplars)

Every plant below that has a dangerous look-alike in this biome is paired here. The discriminators are
**necessary but not sufficient**. Positive ID needs *several* matching traits, and each entry's
`## ⚠ POSITIVE-ID SAFETY GATE` is the real test. Rows run from the most dangerous to the least. The ramp and
false hellebore pair can poison the heart: case papers report slow heart rate and low blood pressure, and
deaths have been described for *Veratrum*. If you suspect someone ate any of these, call Poison Help at
**1-800-222-1222**. Do not wait for symptoms (call 911 for a seizure, collapse, trouble breathing or if they cannot be woken; do not
induce vomiting).

|---------------|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------|--------------|
| **Ramps / wild leek** (*Allium tricoccum*) — [entry](entries/ramps-allium-tricoccum.md) | **American false hellebore** (*Veratrum viride*) and **lily of the valley** (*Convallaria majalis*) | An onion or garlic smell from the **single leaf in your hand** is necessary but **never sufficient**: the smell of a patch, or on your hands, proves nothing, and no smell means stop. Ramp leaves rise from the ground or bulb, one to three per plant, each flat and elliptic, and the flowers sit on a bare stalk after the leaves die back. **A leaf stalk proves nothing either way:** Go Botany says ramp var. *burdickii* leaves are "not or scarcely petiolate," and the sources disagree on whether false hellebore leaves have a stalk. Bracken: fuzzy, no brown papery covering, no U-shaped groove. **Fuzz instead of scales means it is not ostrich fern or lady fern.** The groove and scales alone do not clear lady fern (Alaska state profile), so the first time a person who knows ostrich fern confirms it in person. UMaine: not enough is known about other ferns to recommend eating them. **Even a correct ostrich fern must be boiled 15 minutes or steamed 10 to 12 minutes, and the water thrown out** | Bracken: cancer in laboratory rats (UMaine); NC State rates bracken a high-severity poison. Raw or lightly cooked fiddleheads: diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps and headaches, usually 30 minutes to 12 hours after eating (UMaine, CDC, Health Canada); the toxin is not identified |

Edible confusables: black huckleberry (*Gaylussacia baccata*), sourtop blueberry (*V. myrtilloides*). Poisonous dark-berried plants to rule out: **American black nightshade** (*Solanum americanum*) and **bluebead lily** (*Clintonia borealis*) | A low shrub with small toothed leaves set singly on the twig, bell-shaped white or pink flowers, and round waxy powder-blue (or black) berries on open acidic ground. Black huckleberry: fine resinous yellow dots on the leaf undersides. Sourtop: hairy leaves and stems, leaf edges with few or no teeth. **Nightshade: green erect stems, star-shaped white flowers with a yellow center, shiny black berries in clusters. Bluebead lily: dark blue bead-like berries on a long stalk above two to four curved leaves.** Berry color is not a test | Black nightshade (NC State: high severity): nausea, vomiting, salivation, drowsiness, abdominal pain, diarrhea, weakness, respiratory depression, convulsions, hallucinations; may be fatal. Bluebead lily (NC State: low severity): berries poisonous. Managed barrens: pesticide use; no residue or re-entry figures in the sources opened |

> **Rows for fiddleheads and blueberries differ from the ramp row.** The fiddlehead hazards are a long-term
> one (bracken) and an unexplained one (illness after raw or lightly cooked ferns). The blueberry sources
> name no toxic look-alike for the berry, and the two poisonous plants in its row are named as poisonous,
> not as blueberry look-alikes. Missing data on look-alikes is not a promise that none exists.

> **No onion smell = do not eat. An onion smell does not make it a ramp.** False hellebore and lily of the
> valley share the ramp's woods and season. And **berry color and "animals eat it" are not tests**: see the
> wild-plants rules in the [food guide](../../../05_food.md). Plants without an entry in this folder are not
> cleared; an absent entry is missing data, not a green light.

## Synthesis & load order

No plant here is cleared by a glance. The most dangerous is the **ramp** (false hellebore and lily of the
valley grow in the same spring woods); the **fiddlehead** is the one plant here that needs a mandatory,
timed cooking step and has a cancer-linked fern (bracken) in the same habitat; the **wild blueberry** is
the lowest-hazard entry, but the managed barrens it grows on are farmland and need the owner's permission.
Calorie reality: all three are spring or summer seasonal foods, not staples. Public land is regulated:
Maine state parks and historic sites allow no gathering, Maine public lands list berry and fiddlehead
picking as needing no permit, Vermont bans commercial harvest of fiddleheads and ramps on state and
conservation land, and its forests-and-parks rule lets you uproot or cut a plant only with a written permit,
except wild berries, fruits, seeds, nuts and mushrooms for personal use (ramps and fiddleheads are not on that
list). New Hampshire's rule on all DRED properties bars removing plants. Check the land
manager's rule before you pick. Load order:

```
1. LOOK-ALIKE MATRIX (above)   — "what could kill me" first; internalize the ramp smell rule and the fiddlehead cooking rule
2. This manifest              — find the entry + read its facets
3. The plant entry            — ⚠ POSITIVE-ID SAFETY GATE (multiple concurrent traits) + ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION (fiddleheads)
4. Route through ../food_hub.md — season-indexed food decisions for the whole biome
5. CITE the entry's sources (T1/T2) and triangulate ≥2 refs at safety-read
Positive ID or do not consume. Universal Edibility Test is a LAST resort only (and useless for fungi).
```
