## Zone Fit & Why It Earns Space

Winter squash stores for 3-6 months with minimal intervention (cool/dry room, no special processing). One plant yields 3-10+ squashes, each providing 100-300 calories of dense carbs. Unlike summer squash (zucchini), winter squash is harvested after full maturity — skin hardens, seeds mature, and sugars concentrate. A small plot (100 sq ft) stores a household through winter. Butternut is the most reliable and widely adaptable; Delicata and Hubbard are also excellent for zone 7a.

## Planting Windows (Zone 7a)

**Plant:** Late May–early June when soil temperature ≥65°F and all frost danger is past (last frost mid-April; wait 4-6 weeks). 

**Harvest:** September–early October (vine dies back or frost threatens). Squashes should be fully mature (hard rind, deep color; skin resists fingernail). If early frost threatens, harvest all squashes regardless of size; immature ones store less well but are still edible.

## Growing

- **Spacing:** Vining types need significant space (6-8 feet between plants in rows 8 feet apart, or hill-planted 8 feet apart). Bush types compact (~4 feet); compact more efficiently in small gardens.
- **Soil:** Well-draining, fertile, full sun (6+ hours). Squash loves compost-amended soil and consistent moisture during vine growth.
- **Water:** 1-1.5 inches per week during growth phase (May-August). Reduce in late season to concentrate sugars and toughen skin for storage.
- **Mulch:** Straw or compost around plants reduces weeds and keeps fruit off bare soil (reduces rot).
- **Pests (7a region):** Squash vine borer (most damaging — adult wasp lays eggs at base of vine; larvae tunnel inside, killing the plant). Prevent by mounding soil over vine nodes (they'll root, bypassing damaged sections) or wrapping base with aluminum foil. Pickleworm and powdery mildew (late summer, less serious). Scout regularly; hand-pick borers if you see wilting.
- **Diseases:** Powdery mildew (white coating on leaves, mostly cosmetic); downy mildew (rare, yellow patches); bacterial wilt (less common). Improve air circulation; avoid overhead watering.

## Harvest & Storage

**Maturity:** Rinds harden (fingernail can't pierce). Colors deepen (Butternut = tan, Acorn = dark green, Delicata = tan stripes). Leave squashes on vine as long as possible before first frost. When frost threatens, harvest all squashes by cutting stem with 1-2 inches of stem attached.

**Curing (critical for storage):** Cure in a warm (65-75°F), well-ventilated space for 10-14 days. This hardens the rind further and heals small cuts. Without curing, storage life drops dramatically.

**Storage:** After curing, store at 50-60°F in a cool, dark, well-ventilated space (basement, garage, unheated room). Squashes should not touch each other (wrap in newspaper or straw to prevent rot spread if one decays). Check monthly; discard any soft or rotting squashes. 

**Shelf life:** 3-6 months depending on variety (Butternut ≈5-6 months; Delicata ≈3-4 months; Hubbard ≈6+ months) and storage conditions. Damaged squashes should be used first.

## Seed Saving

Winter squashes are **insect-pollinated.** Different species (moschata, pepo, maxima) do not cross, but varieties within a species can cross. 

**For self-sufficiency:**
1. Choose open-pollinated varieties (most heirloom squashes are; hybrid varieties on seed packets are labeled F1).
2. For pure seed lines, isolate varieties:
   - **Separation distance:** 150+ feet between different varieties of the same species (achievable only in large gardens or separate seasons).
   - **Hand-pollination:** Select mature female flowers (those with a small fruit behind the flower); hand-pollinate with male flowers early morning. Cover pollinated flower with mesh to prevent insect re-pollination.
3. Harvest one mature squash (not the largest; choose a typical one). Leave it on the vine until very hard. Let it sit indoors for 2-4 weeks (seeds finish maturing).
4. Cut open; scoop seeds and surrounding tissue into a bowl. Ferment in water for 2-3 days (this removes the gel coating). Rinse thoroughly; spread seeds on a plate to air-dry in a warm, well-ventilated space for 2-3 weeks until bone-dry.
5. Store in cool/dry conditions (50-60°F, <50% humidity, sealed container). Viability: 3-5 years.

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

Winter squash must be cooked to be edible. Raw squash flesh is extremely hard and indigestible.

**Preparation rules:**
1. **Cut (carefully):** Rind is very hard. Knife can slip. Place squash on a sturdy cutting board; use a sharp chef's knife with a longer blade. Cut lengthwise, then cut halves into sections. Alternatively, pierce skin multiple times with a fork and roast whole at 375°F for 45 minutes; cool slightly, then cut.
2. **Remove seeds and strings:** Scoop out seeds and fibrous tissue with a sturdy spoon. (Save seeds for roasting or planting.)
3. **Cook:** Roast cut-side down at 375-400°F for 40-60 minutes (until flesh is very soft and skin can be easily scraped off), or boil in water for 30-40 minutes until fork-tender. Smaller pieces cook faster. Flesh is done when it easily separates from the rind.
4. **Serve:** Scoop cooked flesh from rind. Can be eaten plain, mashed, puréed, or used in soups, breads, etc. Cooked squash freezes well for later use.
