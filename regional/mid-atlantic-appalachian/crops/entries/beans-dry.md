## Zone Fit & Why It Earns Space

Dry beans are **protein + carbs in storable form** — 1 lb dried beans yields ~2 cups cooked beans (≈600 calories, 15g protein). They store for 5-10 years in cool/dry conditions with zero processing. For survival gardens, beans close the protein gap that grains and tubers leave open. Single planting feeds multiple households. Legumes also fix nitrogen, improving soil for next year's crop.

## Planting Windows (Zone 7a)

**Plant:** Late May, when soil temperature ≥60°F and all frost danger has passed. Last frost in Baltimore ≈ mid-April; wait 3-4 weeks longer to avoid cold damage. 

**Harvest:** August–September (pods should be fully mature, papery brown pods with hard seeds inside before first frost, ≈ late October). If early frost threatens while pods are still green, pull entire plant and hang in a warm, dry place for 2-3 weeks to finish drying.

## Growing

- **Spacing:** Bush varieties 4-6 inches apart in rows 18 inches apart. Pole varieties can be trellised (8 inches apart, rows 24 inches); they sprawl but yield more per area.
- **Soil:** Well-draining, full sun (6+ hours). Beans are less fussy than other crops; tolerate mediocre soil but benefit from compost.
- **Water:** 1 inch per week during flowering/pod development. Reduce once pods mature.
- **Support:** Pole beans need trellis or stakes. Bush beans generally stay upright but may lodge in heavy rain.
- **Pests (7a region):** Bean beetles (yellow/orange beetles with black spots; very destructive — hand-pick daily, including eggs). Aphids (less serious). Row covers on seedlings reduce early damage.
- **Diseases:** Rust (orange pustules on leaves; reduce leaf wetness); anthracnose (brown spots on pods). Avoid overhead watering; improve air circulation.

## Harvest & Storage

**Maturity:** Pods brown, dry, brittle. Seeds rattle inside. Harvest when pods are papery but before they split. 

**Drying:** Pull pods off plants (or entire plant if frost threatens) and spread in a warm, dry room or garage. Pods should dry for 2-3 weeks until seeds are rock-hard. Shell by hand (wearing gloves to reduce friction blisters) or by placing dried pods in a cloth bag and striking gently with a mallet to release seeds.

**Storage:** Cool (50-60°F), dry (<50% humidity), airtight container or sealed jar. Check periodically for weevils (small holes indicate infestation; discard). Viability: 5-10 years; best germination first 3-4 years.

## Seed Saving

Dry beans are **self-pollinating open-pollinated crops** — seeds come true to type. 

**For self-sufficiency:**
1. Choose open-pollinated varieties (almost all dry bean varieties are; check seed packet).
2. Leave best pods on plant to mature fully (brown, dry). Harvest by hand (do not thresh mechanically).
3. Spread pods in a warm, dry space for 2-3 weeks.
4. Shell carefully; discard any damaged or shriveled seeds. Store only the largest, healthiest seeds.
5. Cool/dry storage (50-60°F, <50% humidity, sealed container). Examine before next planting for weevils.

Isolation distance: 50+ feet between different bean varieties prevents cross-pollination (they are self-fertile but insect-pollinated flowers can cross). If space is limited, plant varieties in separate years.

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

**Raw or undercooked dry beans contain lectins (phytohaemagglutinin), a toxic protein that causes severe nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.** 

Lectins are destroyed by heat. Slow cookers or warm-water soaking do NOT reliably inactivate them. **Full cooking — boiling or pressure-cooking until beans are completely soft — is non-negotiable.**

**Preparation rules:**
1. **Sort dried beans:** Remove stones, shriveled beans, and discolored beans (may indicate mold or damage).
2. **Rinse:** Quick rinse to remove dust.
3. **Soak (optional but recommended):** Soak overnight in cool water, or use "quick soak" (boil 2 minutes, let sit 1 hour). This reduces cooking time and gas production. Drain soaking water.
4. **FULL COOK:** Boil beans in fresh water for **at minimum 1-2 hours**, until completely soft and easily crushed between fingers. A rolling boil is essential — slow simmering under 180°F does not reliably destroy lectins. High-altitude cooks (above 5,000 ft) should boil longer due to lower water temps.
   - **Pressure cooker:** High pressure (10-15 PSI) for 25-30 minutes achieves full cook rapidly and reliably.
   - **Stovetop:** Rolling boil, uncovered, for 1-2+ hours (time depends on bean variety and age; red kidney beans require longer than black beans).
5. **Discard cooking water** if beans were not pre-soaked.
6. **Test:** Beans should be soft enough to mash easily. Any hardness remaining = risk.

**Common mistakes that leave beans unsafe:**
- Slow cooker on "warm" setting (temps too low; lectins persist)
- Underestimating cooking time in high-altitude areas
- Assuming soaking alone makes beans safe (it doesn't)
- Trying to speed-cook in minimal water (beans don't cook uniformly)
