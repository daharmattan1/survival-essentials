## Zone Fit & Why It Earns Space

Kale and collards are **the season-stretchers** in a survival garden — frost-hardy greens that produce leaves in spring, take a summer break, revive in cool fall weather, and continue into winter (under mulch or cold frame). They are edible raw or cooked, dense in calcium/iron/vitamins, and extremely reliable. A single plant produces leaves continuously for 6+ months. They tolerate cold snaps that kill tender greens; sugars concentrate after frost, making them sweeter and more nutritious.

## Planting Windows (Zone 7a)

**Spring crop:** Sow seed or transplant seedlings 2-3 weeks *before* last frost (early April), or immediately after (mid-April). Both windows work — early plantings establish before heat, later plantings avoid a false spring. Harvest May-June as outer leaves reach full size.

**Fall crop:** Sow seed or transplant mid-July through early August (after summer heat peak, while soil is still warm for germination). Harvest September-November and into winter. Fall-planted kale/collards can survive temperatures down to 20°F (often lower, depending on variety and hardiness).

**Winter survival:** In zone 7a, with mulch or cold-frame protection, kale/collards survive winter and resume growth in spring (but are usually heavily browsed or declining by March). Without protection, they survive exposed but production slows/stops in January-February.

## Growing

- **Spacing:** 18-24 inches apart in rows 24-30 inches apart. Larger spacing allows more light and airflow; reduces crowding.
- **Soil:** Well-draining, fertile (compost-amended), full sun (6+ hours preferred). Kale/collards tolerate partial shade but slower growth.
- **Water:** 1-1.5 inches per week during growth. Once established, more drought-tolerant than most brassicas.
- **Mulch:** Straw around plants after they're established keeps soil cool and moist; prevents root damage from freeze-thaw.
- **Pests (7a region):** Cabbage worms (green caterpillars that bore into leaves), flea beetles (small holes in young leaves), cabbage looper. Row covers protect seedlings. Hand-pick large caterpillars. Spinosad (organic, T1-safe) or neem oil reduce pest pressure.
- **Diseases:** Clubroot (rare; preventable with rotation and lime); powdery mildew (late season, mostly cosmetic); bacterial leaf spot (brown spots with yellow halos; remove affected leaves). Good air circulation reduces disease.

## Harvest & Storage

**Timing (spring):** Harvest outer leaves once they reach full size (6-12 inches, depending on variety). Pinch or cut lower leaves first; new leaves emerge from the center crown. Continue harvesting outer leaves every 1-2 weeks through June.

**Timing (fall/winter):** Begin outer-leaf harvesting in September. Continue as weather allows through November. After hard freeze (first night ≈ 28°F or below), plants may wilt but often recover. Harvest continues under mulch into January (depending on severity and variety).

**Storage (fresh):** Kale/collard leaves keep 1-2 weeks in refrigerator (plastic bag, unwashed). For longer storage, blanch leaves 2 minutes, freeze in portions (vacuum seal or freezer bags; viability 8-12 months).

**Shelf-life (frozen):** 8-12 months in freezer.

## Seed Saving

Brassicas are **biennial** (flower and seed in year 2) and **cross-pollinate** (different Brassica varieties will cross). 

**For self-sufficiency (practical approach):**
1. Choose open-pollinated varieties (most heirloom kale/collards are; check seed packets).
2. Plan for 2 years: first year grows leafy plants; second year you let plants flower/seed.
3. **Fall planting + winter survival:** Plant mid-summer (for fall/winter harvest); leave a few plants unharvested. Mulch heavily in late fall; plants survive winter and bolt/flower in spring of year 2.
4. **Isolation:** Brassicas cross readily. For pure seed, isolate varieties 150+ feet apart or grow in separate seasons. If nearby neighbors grow cabbage/broccoli/etc., isolation is difficult; cross-pollination likely (acceptable if you only care about viable seed, not purity).
5. **Flower → seed pod:** In spring (year 2), plants bolt. Small yellow flowers appear; allow them to fully develop. Pods form (siliques), elongate, turn brown. 
6. **Harvest:** When pods brown and dry on plant, cut entire flowering stalk. Hang upside-down in a paper bag for 1-2 weeks. Pods dry; tap gently to release seeds.
7. **Storage:** Store seeds in cool/dry conditions (50-60°F, <50% humidity, sealed container). Viability: 3-4 years.

**Easier alternative:** Buy certified seed most years; save seed only if you have space/patience for the 2-year cycle.
