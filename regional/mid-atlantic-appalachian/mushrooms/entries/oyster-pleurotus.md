## ⚠ POSITIVE-ID SAFETY GATE

> ⚠ **Oyster vs. angel wings and jack-o'-lantern: one look-alike has killed people, the other makes people very sick**
>
> **Positive-ID checklist (ALL must hold):**
> - **True gills** that are white or grayish and run down the stem, not orange [1][2]
> - **Cap whitish, gray, tan or brown, shell- or fan-shaped**, never orange [1][2]
> - **Thick, fleshy fruiting body.** Oyster is a robust mushroom with thick flesh [3]
> - **Stem absent, or short, thick, white and off-center** [1][2]
> - **On a log, stump or trunk, usually hardwood**, in clusters [1][2]. Conifer is possible [2], so a conifer host is a reason to check for angel wings [3]
> - **Spore print white to grayish lilac** [1][2]. This only rules out other colors.
>
> **Do NOT use if ANY of these are present (disqualifying traits):**
> - **Orange cap, orange flesh, or a tight dense cluster on a stump, tree base or buried root** (jack-o'-lantern) [6][7]
> - **Small, thin, pure white, nearly stemless, on conifer wood, especially hemlock** (angel wings) [3]
> - Any doubt about which of the above it is
>
> **Confusable with:** Jack-o'-lantern (*Omphalotus illudens*) → bright orange, dense clusters on stumps and buried roots of hardwoods, orange flesh → severe vomiting and cramps [5][6][7]. Angel wings (*Pleurocybella porrigens*) → thin white fans on conifer wood → a 2004 outbreak in Japan sickened 59 people and 17 died of acute encephalopathy, nearly all with compromised kidneys [4].
>
> **Both have true gills, so "gill edge" and "ridge vs. gill" tests do not work.** Oyster mushrooms have true gills [1]. Use color, flesh thickness, host and cluster habit as above, and have an expert confirm.
>
> **Verdict:** Positive ID on every item, confirmed by a mushroom expert, or do not eat it. **This species must never be eaten on field identification alone.**

## ☠ Deadly Look-Alikes

### Angel wings (*Pleurocybella porrigens*)

| Check | Oyster (*Pleurotus*) | Angel wings |
|---|---|---|
| Size and flesh | Cap 3 to 15 cm, thicker flesh; "much more robust" [2][3] | Cap 2.5 to 10 cm, smooth, white, flesh very thin [3] |
| Color | Whitish, gray, tan or brown [1][2] | White [3] |
| Gills | Narrow, close to nearly distant, white or gray, running down the stem [1][2] | White, crowded, narrow, running down the stem [3] |
| Stem | Absent or short, thick, off-center, hairy base [1] | Absent or a narrow, stubby white base [3] |
| Host | Mainly hardwoods, sometimes conifers [2] | Dead conifer logs and stumps, especially eastern hemlock [3] |
| Season | See below | August through October [3] |
| Spore print | White to grayish lilac [1][2] | White [3] |

Spore print does not separate these. The thin flesh, pure white color and conifer host do, and the oyster host rule is not exclusive [2]. Pale oysters exist [2], so color alone is also not enough. If a white fan on conifer wood is thin, small and stemless, throw it out.

**What it does:** NAMA's note reports that in autumn 2004 Japan had 59 sick in 9 prefectures and 17 deaths from acute encephalopathy, with symptoms beginning 13 to 18 days after eating. Nearly all deaths involved people with compromised kidneys, average age 70 [4]. NAMA's syndrome page lists convulsive encephalopathy in people with chronic renal failure [5]. The cause is not settled [4]. This reference gives no basis to eat angel wings, and anyone with kidney disease should avoid them.

### Jack-o'-lantern (*Omphalotus illudens*)

| Check | Oyster | Jack-o'-lantern |
|---|---|---|
| Gills | True gills [1][2] | True gills, close or crowded, running down the stem [6] |
| Color | Whitish, gray, tan or brown [1][2] | Bright orange to pumpkin orange [6] |
| Flesh | Not described as orange in sources | Pale orange, unchanging [6] |
| Growth | Shelves and fans on trunks, logs and stumps, in clusters [1] | Large dense clusters on stumps or buried roots of hardwoods, especially oaks [6][7] |
| Spore print | White to grayish lilac [1] | White to creamy or pale yellow [6] |

Gill edge does not separate them, because both have true gills. Spore print does not either. What separates them is orange color, orange flesh, and a dense stump or root cluster. Oyster gills can turn yellowish with age [1][2], so yellowing gills on a pale or brown cap is not the same as an orange mushroom. When unsure, throw it out.

Do not use the glow. Jack-o'-lantern gills are luminescent when fresh [6], but the glow fades after picking [8].

Missouri says jack-o'-lantern is not life-threatening to healthy adults but makes you very sick for a few days [7]. NAMA says most victims recovered within 12 hours [5]. Missouri also says there are no poisonous look-alikes of oyster in that state [1]. That page does not cover angel wings and is not about this region.

## Identification

**Cap:** Whitish, grayish or tan (MDC) [1]. Pale to dark brown fading to buff (*P. ostreatus*) [2]. Shell-shaped to semicircular or elongated, margin smooth or wavy [1]. *P. pulmonarius* is often paler [2].

**Gills:** Narrow, nearly distant (MDC) or close (Kuo), whitish or with a gray tinge, descending the stalk, becoming yellowish with age [1][2].

**Stem:** Often absent. When present, short, thick, white, off-center, hairy base [1]. Kuo gives 1 to 7 by 1 to 3 cm, often rudimentary and lateral [2].

**Hosts:** Dead logs and living trees, mainly hardwoods but sometimes conifers; white rot [2]. *P. populinus* is on quaking aspen [2].

**Spore print:** White to grayish lilac (MDC) [1]; white to faintly yellowish or lilac (Kuo) [2]. Not a discriminator against the look-alikes above.

**Smell, color, insect damage:** NAMA says these are not reliable tests of edibility [9].

## Habitat, Substrate & Fruiting (this biome)

- ***P. ostreatus*:** late fall (October) through early spring (April) in Kuo's description [2]. Missouri says year-round, especially after rain in mild weather [1].
- ***P. pulmonarius*:** late April to September (Kuo) [2]. Missouri says only in warm months [1].
- **Conifers:** possible hosts [2]. This matters because angel wings grow on conifer wood [3].
- **Range:** the sources are national or Missouri guides. No source opened gives Mid-Atlantic peak weeks, so none are stated.

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

1. Confirm every item in the gate on every cluster. Have an expert confirm the collection first.
2. Throw out any old, decayed or insect-damaged mushrooms.
3. **Cook thoroughly. Do not eat raw.** NAMA notes many edible species need appropriate preparation [9]. MDC says to be sure of the ID and eat only a small amount the first time [1].
4. Keep one species per container. Never mix collections.
5. A small first portion cannot clear a doubtful mushroom. Jack-o'-lantern illness starts typically 1 to 3 hours after eating [5]. The angel wings illness in Japan began 13 to 18 days later [4].
6. **If in doubt, do not eat it.** "When in doubt, throw it out" [11].

## Toxicology of the Confusables

### Jack-o'-lantern (*Omphalotus illudens*)

- **Toxins:** illudin M and illudin S. Once thought to be muscarine, but NAMA says the symptoms do not match [5].
- **Onset:** typically 1 to 3 hours [5].
- **Symptoms:** nausea (90% of cases), vomiting (73%), abdominal pain, headache, exhaustion, weakness, dizziness; sweating, salivation and diarrhea less often [5].
- **Duration:** most recovered within 12 hours per NAMA [5]; a few days per Missouri [7]; most victims end up in the emergency room per Cornell [8].
- **Cooking:** no source opened says cooking removes it.
- **Care:** with vomiting and diarrhea, replacing lost fluids and electrolytes is important [5].

### Angel wings (*Pleurocybella porrigens*)

- **Illness:** acute encephalopathy (brain illness with convulsions) in the 2004 Japan outbreak; 17 deaths [4][5].
- **Onset:** 13 to 18 days after eating, so the link to the meal is easy to miss [4].
- **Who:** nearly all deaths were people with compromised kidneys, average age 70 [4].
- **Suspected toxin:** a 2011 study proposed an unstable amino acid, pleurocybellaziridine. Primate studies and brain pathology are lacking [4].

### If someone may have eaten a look-alike

This is commonly taught first aid for when help is slow to arrive, not medical advice.

1. **Call Poison Help, 1-800-222-1222, now.** Do not wait for symptoms, and tell them if the person has kidney disease [10][11].
2. **Call 911** if the person collapses, has a seizure, has trouble breathing, or cannot be woken [10].
3. **Do not induce vomiting** [10].
4. **Save a sample.** Photograph it beside a coin for scale and keep pieces in a paper bag, not plastic [11].
5. Get medical care even if the person feels fine. Angel wings illness can start two weeks later [4].

## Legal note

Picking is regulated on public land and differs by manager. Shenandoah National Park allows small amounts of edible mushrooms for personal use [12]. Pennsylvania state forests prohibit removing plants "including fungus, lichen and moss" without written DCNR permission [13]. Check the land manager's rule. No survival exemption was found.
