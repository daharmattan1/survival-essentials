## ⚠ POSITIVE-ID SAFETY GATE

> ⚠ **Puffball vs. Amanita "egg" button — delayed liver/kidney failure**
>
> **Positive-ID checklist for PUFFBALL (ALL must hold):**
> - Fruiting body is **spherical, pear-shaped, or club-shaped**, growing from soil (not on wood)
> - When you cut the puffball vertically **top-to-bottom, the interior is solid and uniformly white throughout** — no differentiation of color or structure
> - No cap, gills, or stem outline visible when sliced (interior is homogeneous)
> - Outer skin is smooth or warty, pale to brown
> - Spore mass inside is white, then becoming yellowish-brown as it matures
> - Fruiting June–October
>
> **Positive-ID checklist for WOOD EAR (ALL must hold):**
> - Fruiting body is **gelatinous, ear-shaped or cup-shaped**, growing directly on dead hardwood (logs, stumps, branches)
> - **Rubbery texture** — soft when wet, firm when dry but still flexible
> - **Dark brown to black**, thin and translucent in places
> - No gills, pores, or stem
>
> **Do NOT use if ANY of these are present (disqualifying traits for PUFFBALL):**
> - Interior shows any outline, cap, gills, or stem structure when sliced top-to-bottom
> - Interior is not uniformly white/pale
> - Fruiting body has a definite stem (vs. a root-like attachment)
> - Growing on wood (that is a puffball's terrestrial variant; check slice test)
>
> **Confusable with:** (1) Amanita "egg" button (*A. bisporigera* destroying angel; *A. phalloides* death cap also documented Maryland northward) → **CUT EVERY PUFFBALL TOP-TO-BOTTOM: any outline of a cap, gills, or stem inside = an Amanita button → DISCARD.** (2) *Scleroderma* spp. (earthball / pigskin puffball) → **ruled out by the SAME cut test: interior is dark purple-black (not white), with a thick warty rind — DISCARD.** Wood ear has **no deadly confusable known in this region.**
>
> **Verdict:** **MANDATORY PROCEDURE — CUT EVERY PUFFBALL TOP-TO-BOTTOM.** Interior must be solid, uniformly white/pale; any hint of cap, gills, or stem structure = *Amanita* → DISCARD IMMEDIATELY. This species must never be eaten on field identification alone; the slice test is non-negotiable.

## ☠ Deadly Look-Alikes

### Amanita "Egg" Button (*Amanita bisporigera*, *A. phalloides*, and other *Amanita* spp.)

**The discriminators (verbatim from the deadly-look-alike matrix):**
- A true puffball is **solid, uniformly white inside** when sliced
- **CUT EVERY PUFFBALL TOP-TO-BOTTOM**: any **outline of a cap, gills, or stem inside = an Amanita button → DISCARD**

**The fatal error:** Young *Amanita* mushrooms are enclosed in a universal veil, giving them a round, egg-like appearance. To an untrained eye, an Amanita button looks like a small white puffball. The only reliable test is the **vertical slice** — if you see any internal structure (even a faint outline) of a cap, gills (thin lines radiating from the center), or a stem stub, **you have an *Amanita*.**

**What it does:** *Amanita* spp. (especially *A. bisporigera*, the destroying angel, and *A. phalloides*, the death cap — both documented in the mid-Atlantic, *phalloides* Maryland northward) contain **amatoxins** (α-amanitin, β-amanitin, γ-amanitin). These are **protein synthesis inhibitors** that damage the liver and kidneys irreversibly.

### *Scleroderma* spp. (Earthball / Pigskin Puffball) — Common Poisonous Look-Alike

**The discriminator:** Apply the **same vertical cut test** used for Amanita. A true puffball interior is solid white throughout. *Scleroderma* interior is **dark purple-black** (firm, marbled, or entirely dark from young), and the outer rind is **thick and warty** (vs. the thin, fragile skin of *Lycoperdon*). If the interior is anything other than uniformly white, **DISCARD.**

**What it does:** *Scleroderma* species cause **GI poisoning** — nausea, vomiting, cramping, diarrhea — onset typically 30 minutes to 2 hours. Not typically lethal in healthy adults, but can be severe. The same cut test that catches Amanita also catches earthball; one procedure rules out both.

**Timeline & symptoms:**
- **Latency (6–24 hours, often 8–12 hours):** asymptomatic or mild nausea
- **Phase 1 ("shock"):** sudden vomiting, severe diarrhea, cramping, dehydration, often mistaken for food poisoning
- **Phase 2 ("recovery", 24–72 hours):** symptoms subside; patient feels better (false recovery) — but organ damage is occurring
- **Phase 3 (72+ hours):** jaundice, liver/kidney failure, oliguria, coma, death (~50% mortality without prompt treatment; ~10% with modern intensive care, per NAMA)

**No antidote; only supportive care:** treatment requires hospitalization, IV fluids, liver/kidney function monitoring, dialysis if renal failure occurs. **IV silibinin** (the European antidote, used in some US centers under compassionate use) may reduce mortality when administered early; oral milk thistle capsules are NOT effective and should not be substituted. Many victims die even with aggressive ICU care.

**Lesson:** the 6–24 hour latency is the killer — the victim does not immediately connect the meal to the poisoning, and by the time liver damage is diagnosed, the toxin has often done irreversible harm. **Every puffball must be cut and inspected.** No shortcuts.

### Wood Ear — No Deadly Look-Alike Known

Wood ear (*Auricularia* spp.) has **no known deadly look-alike in the mid-Atlantic region**. Other gelatinous fungi on wood exist (jelly fungi, witch's butter), but none are known to cause serious poisoning if eaten. Inedible or mildly GI-upsetting, yes; lethal, no.

## Identification

### PUFFBALL (Lycoperdon spp. and related genera)

**General form:**
- **Spherical, pear-shaped, or club-shaped**, growing from soil
- No stem (or a root-like attachment only)
- Typically 2–10 cm in diameter (variable by species)

**Outer skin:**
- **Smooth or warty** (spiny in *Lycoperdon perlatum*, smooth in *L. pyriforme*)
- Color: pale tan to brown, sometimes with a checkered or pearl-like texture
- Skin may peel or slough off as the mushroom matures

**Interior (THE DIAGNOSTIC TEST):**
- **Solid and uniformly white or pale when freshly cut**
- No visible cap, gills, stem, or chambers
- As the mushroom matures (ages), the interior gradually yellows and becomes the spore mass (this is normal for a mature puffball, but fresh specimens are white)

**Spore release:**
- When fully mature, the puffball develops a small pore or crack, and the spore mass (now brown/yellow) puffs out in a cloud when squeezed or disturbed

**Stem-like attachment:**
- Some species (e.g., *L. pyriforme*) have a root-like base; this is not a true stem and should not resemble an Amanita stem inside the fruiting body

**Critical field test:**
1. **Before you harvest, cut the puffball vertically top-to-bottom with a clean knife.**
2. **Examine the interior under good light.**
3. If you see **any outline, color differentiation, or structure** (cap shape, gill rays, a stem stub inside), **DISCARD IMMEDIATELY** — it is an *Amanita* button.
4. Only if the interior is **solid, uniform, and white/pale** is it a true puffball.

### WOOD EAR (Auricularia polytricha)

**General form:**
- **Ear-shaped or cup-shaped**, or irregularly lobed
- Grows directly on dead hardwood (logs, stumps, fallen branches)
- No stem; attached to wood by a gelatinous base

**Texture & appearance:**
- **Gelatinous and rubbery** — soft and flexible when wet or fresh, firm but still flexible when dried
- **Dark brown to black** in color
- Thin-bodied; translucent in places when held to light
- Interior surface may have radial ridges or folds, not true gills

**Size:**
- 2–15 cm across; highly variable

**Undersurface:**
- May have a fine, hair-like or veiny appearance; these are not gills

**Spore print:**
- Brown; rarely taken in field (gelatinous fungi do not drop spores easily)

**Seasonal fruiting:**
- Year-round potential; peak in summer and fall after rain
- Can revive from dried state after rain

## Habitat, Substrate & Fruiting (Mid-Atlantic / Appalachian, Zone 7a)

### Puffball

**Where it grows:**
- **Soil-based:** grasslands, meadows, pastures, forest edges, disturbed ground
- Sometimes on well-rotted wood or mulch piles

**Timing:**
- **Summer–fall (June–October)** in the region
- Fruit after rain or high humidity
- Can be prolific in wet years

**Species notes:**
- *Lycoperdon perlatum* (gem-studded puffball) is common and choice
- *L. pyriforme* (stump puffball) grows near wood and decaying stumps
- Various other species; most are edible when interior is white

### Wood Ear

**Where it grows:**
- **Dead hardwood:** oak, beech, birch, elm, ash, and others
- Logs, stumps, fallen branches; often in moist, shaded areas
- Never on soil; always on wood

**Timing:**
- **Year-round**, with peak activity summer–fall
- Fruits during warm, moist weather; can revive from dried state after rain

**Climate niche:**
- Prefers moist, mild regions; abundant in the mid-Atlantic

## ⚠ MANDATORY PREPARATION

### Puffball

**Preparation protocol:**
1. **CUT AND INSPECT (non-negotiable):** Before harvest, slice each puffball vertically top-to-bottom. If the interior is solid white, proceed; if you see any internal structure, **DISCARD IMMEDIATELY** (Amanita).
2. **Harvest only white-interior specimens:** young, fresh puffballs with pure white interior are best.
3. **Clean gently:** brush or rinse; do not soak.
4. **Peel the outer skin** (some species have a tough skin that should be removed).
5. **Cook thoroughly:** sauté, roast, or simmer in broths. Do not eat raw.
6. **First-portion rule:** consume a small portion (1–2 small puffballs) with no other new foods or alcohol. Wait 6–8 hours. Only if no GI upset, continue.
7. **Never rely on smell or appearance alone.** The slice test is the ONLY reliable discriminator.
8. **Never mix collections:** only puffballs in your container.

### Wood Ear

**Preparation protocol:**
1. **Clean:** rinse gently under water to remove debris.
2. **Rehydrate (if dried):** soak in warm water for 10–15 minutes; wood ear expands significantly.
3. **Cook thoroughly:** simmer in broths, soups, or stir-fries. Wood ear is chewy and gelatinous; cooking time is 15–30 minutes.
4. **First-portion rule:** consume a small portion (2–3 small pieces) with no other new foods or alcohol. Wait 6–8 hours. Only if no GI upset, continue.
5. **Avoid overcooking:** wood ear becomes mushy if cooked too long; 20–30 minutes is typical.
6. **Never mix collections:** only wood ear in your container.

## Toxicology of the Confusables

### *Amanita bisporigera* (Destroying Angel) — Amatoxins

**The toxins:** α-amanitin, β-amanitin, γ-amanitin, and related cyclic peptides. These are **thermostable** and **cannot be cooked out**. They bind to eukaryotic RNA polymerase II, blocking mRNA synthesis and protein production in hepatocytes and renal tubule cells.

**Timeline & symptoms:**
- **Latency (6–24 hours, often 8–12 hours):** asymptomatic or mild nausea
- **Phase 1 (12–24 hours):** sudden onset vomiting, severe diarrhea, abdominal cramping, dehydration; patient may feel like typical food poisoning
- **Phase 2 ("recovery", 24–72 hours):** symptoms subside; patient feels better and may be discharged from urgent care — **this is the false recovery; organ damage is ongoing**
- **Phase 3 (72+ hours):** jaundice, hepatic encephalopathy, oliguria, hypoglycemia, renal failure, coagulopathy; death in 50–90% of untreated cases
- **Minimum lethal dose:** as low as ~7 mg for a small adult (a single destroying angel cap can contain 5–15 mg)

**Treatment:** supportive care only (IV fluids, liver/kidney monitoring, dialysis, possibly silibinin in some centers). Prognosis is poor if the amatoxin is not rapidly cleared or if liver necrosis is already advanced by hospital admission.

**Lesson:** the false recovery window (24–72 hours) is when many deaths occur — the toxin has already caused irreversible liver damage, but the patient has no warning. **This is why the slice test is non-negotiable.** No puffball-like mushroom should ever be eaten without the internal inspection.
