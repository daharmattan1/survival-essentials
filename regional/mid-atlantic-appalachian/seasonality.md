# Mid-Atlantic / Appalachian — Seasonality Calendar

> A month-by-month orienting overview of foraging, hunting, and growing windows for **this biome only**
> (USDA 7a, Baltimore lowland baseline). It tells you *what is roughly available now* so you know
> which category index to open.
>
> Biome profile: [`_index.md`](_index.md). Food decision hub: [`food_hub.md`](food_hub.md).

---

> ## ⚠ This is an ORIENTING OVERVIEW — NOT an identification source
>
> This calendar lists *what category to look for*, never *what is safe to eat*. **Every consumption
> decision runs through the category index's positive-ID gate** (`edible-plants/`, `mushrooms/`,
> `game-animals/`, `crops/`) and the deadly-look-alike matrix that leads it. Seasonal availability is
> **not** identification. A morel and a false morel fruit in the same weeks — the calendar will never
> tell them apart; the entry's gate does. **Positive ID or do not consume.**
>
> **Elevation shifts everything later/narrower.** These are lowland (Baltimore) dates; add ~1–3 weeks
> of delay in spring and subtract similarly in fall as you climb into the Appalachians.

## Quick season table

| Category | Spring (Mar–May) | Summer (Jun–Aug) | Fall (Sep–Nov) | Winter (Dec–Feb) |
|----------|------------------|------------------|----------------|------------------|
| **Wild greens** | Peak (tender new growth) | Tougher; some persist | Cool-season regrowth | Sparse |
| **Berries / fruit** | Flowering | **Peak** (brambles, summer fruit) | Late fruit, persimmon | None fresh |
| **Nuts / mast** | — | Developing | **Peak — acorns, hickory** (calorie king) | Gleaning leftovers |
| **Mushrooms** | Morels (⚠ false-morel pair) | Oyster, chicken-of-the-woods | **Peak fall flush** | Rare (cold-hardy only) |
| **Game / hunting** | (spring turkey, where regulated) | Lowest priority (heat, spoilage) | **Deer season window** (regulated) | Limited; small game |
| **Crops (7a)** | Plant after ~mid-Apr frost | Tend / early harvest | Main harvest + fall crops | Storage crops only |

## Month-by-month (lowland 7a baseline)

**March** — Last hard frosts. Earliest wild greens emerge. Start cool-hardy seeds indoors/under cover.
Morel season approaches at lowland (⚠ deadly false-morel look-alike — `mushrooms/_index.md` doctrine first).

**April** — **Last spring frost ≈ mid-month.** Peak spring greens; morels in the lowland. After frost
date, direct-sow/transplant cool-season crops (kale/collards, potatoes). Ticks become active —
[`hazards/_index.md`](hazards/_index.md).

**May** — Greens still tender; spring mushrooms taper in the lowland (continue higher up). Plant
warm-season crops (beans, squash, corn) once soil is warm. Passive fishing productive.

**June** — First brambles ripen. Oyster and chicken-of-the-woods possible on hardwood. Garden in full
growth. **Heat/dehydration risk rises** — [`../../substrate/06_first_aid/_index.md`](../../substrate/06_first_aid/_index.md).
Peak tick season.

**July** — **Peak berries.** Summer mushrooms continue on dead/dying hardwood. Tend and begin early
harvest. Hottest, most humid — water demand high; treat all of it.

**August** — Late summer fruit; lamb's quarters and other summer greens. Start fall garden (second
kale/collard sowing). Begin scouting deer sign for fall.

**September** — **Mast begins: early acorns and hickory nuts drop** (the single biggest wild-calorie
window — acorns require mandatory tannin leaching, `edible-plants/_index.md`). Fall mushroom flush
starts. Main garden harvest. Persimmons after first frost soften.

**October** — **First fall frost ≈ late month.** Peak acorn/hickory mast; peak fall mushrooms. Deer
season windows open in much of the region (regulated — `game-animals/_index.md` legal note, not legal
advice). Harvest and cure storage crops (winter squash, potatoes, onions) before frost.

**November** — Late mast gleaning; cold-hardy mushrooms only. Deer season continues in places. Root
cellar / preserve the harvest. Foraging returns drop sharply.

**December–February** — **Lean season.** Lean on stored/preserved food and crops harvested earlier;
cattail roots and inner bark are cold-season fallbacks. Limited small game. Conserve energy — the
Rule of Threes (`../../substrate/01_core_principles/_index.md`) says calories spent must be earned.
