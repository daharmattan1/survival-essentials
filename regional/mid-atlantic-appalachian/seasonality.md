# Mid-Atlantic / Appalachian — Seasonality Calendar

> A month-by-month orienting overview of foraging, hunting, and growing windows for **this biome only**
> (USDA 7a, Baltimore lowland baseline). It tells you *what is roughly available now* so you know
> which category index to open.
>
> Biome profile: [`_index.md`](_index.md). Food decision hub: [`food_hub.md`](food_hub.md).

---

> ## ⚠ This is an ORIENTING OVERVIEW — NOT an identification source
>
> This calendar lists *what category to look for*, never *what you may eat*. **Every consumption
> decision runs through the category index's positive-ID gate** (`edible-plants/`, `mushrooms/`,
> `game-animals/`, `crops/`), the deadly-look-alike matrix that leads it and, for fungi, the 9-point
> [mushroom doctrine](mushrooms/_index.md) (an expert who has examined the specimen in hand comes first).
> Seasonal availability is **not** identification. A morel and a false morel fruit in the same weeks,
> chanterelle and jack-o'-lantern fruit July to October, and the deadly *Amanita* fruits in summer
> and fall. The calendar will never tell them apart; the entry's gate does. **Positive ID or do not consume.**
>
> **Where each window comes from.** Every window below is stated in the linked entry. Most mushroom and
> wild-plant entries say no Virginia, Maryland, West Virginia or Pennsylvania agency dates were found, so
> treat them as the pattern the sources support, not a calendar for your woods. Crop windows come from
> Maryland and Virginia extension.
>
> **Elevation.** These are lowland (Baltimore) baselines. Higher in the Appalachians expect fruit to come
> later and the frost window to narrow (the pawpaw and black walnut entries say this as general practice;
> no source opened gives the size of the shift).

## Month-by-month (lowland 7a baseline)

Dates for crops are Maryland and Virginia extension figures quoted in the entries. Last spring frost in zone 7a
averages **April 15 to 25**; first fall frost **October 15 to 25** ([potato](crops/entries/potato.md)).

**March** — Cool-season crops can go in **before the last frost**: potatoes (Maryland from March 15, Virginia from March 10; [potato](crops/entries/potato.md)); kale and collards (Virginia from March 1; [kale and collards](crops/entries/kale-collards.md)). Wild onion leaves, violet and chickweed are up in early spring: wild onion has a death-camas twin, so read the [matrix](edible-plants/_index.md) first. Morel season can begin (Kuo: March through June; Missouri: late March through April). Mushroom doctrine and [false-morel row](mushrooms/_index.md) first.

**April** — Last spring frost averages April 15 to 25. Kale (Maryland April 1 to 20; Virginia to April 20) and potatoes (to May 1 in Maryland) are still going in. Dandelion, cattail shoots and morels are the spring items; each has its own gate. Spring turkey (bearded birds) opens in April in Virginia (April 11 in 2026), Maryland (April 18) and West Virginia (April 20); Pennsylvania opens May 2 ([turkey](game-animals/entries/wild-turkey.md)). Ticks are active on any warm day, and water hemlock is most toxic in spring: [`hazards/_index.md`](hazards/_index.md).

**May** — Warm-season crops go in **after** the last frost: beans, flint corn, winter squash (Maryland May 15 to June 15; [winter squash](crops/entries/winter-squash.md)). Sweet potato slips can go out from May 1 once soil is at least 65 F ([sweet potato](crops/entries/sweet-potato.md)). Spring turkey runs to late May. *P. pulmonarius* oyster starts in late April ([oyster](mushrooms/entries/oyster-pleurotus.md)).

**June** — Black raspberry fruit starts in June (NC State); brambles run through August ([brambles](edible-plants/entries/wild-blackberry-raspberry-rubus.md)). Black trumpet is reported from June ([entry](mushrooms/entries/black-trumpet-craterellus.md)). Water hemlock is in flower June to August. Tick nymphs are most active in late spring and early summer. Heat and dehydration: [`../../substrate/06_first_aid/_index.md`](../../substrate/06_first_aid/_index.md).

**July** — Chanterelle (July is typical) shares its weeks with jack-o'-lantern, July to October: cut the stem, orange inside means discard ([chanterelle](mushrooms/entries/chanterelle-cantharellus.md), [matrix](mushrooms/_index.md)). Chicken-of-the-woods, black trumpet, puffball and *P. pulmonarius* are possible. Garlic is dug around July 1 in central Maryland, early to mid July in Pennsylvania ([garlic](crops/entries/garlic.md)). Fall kale and collards go in from July 10 in Maryland. Treat all surface water ([`../../substrate/02_water/_index.md`](../../substrate/02_water/_index.md)).

**August** — Pawpaw fruit begins (Maryland Extension and NC State: August to October; [pawpaw](edible-plants/entries/pawpaw-asimina.md)): ripe flesh only. Lion's mane is reported from August ([entry](mushrooms/entries/lions-mane-hericium.md)). Fall kale and collards: Maryland to August 10, Virginia August 1 to September 10.

**September** — Acorns begin to fall (September to November): mandatory leaching, and buckeye or horse chestnut nuts look like acorns and can be fatal ([acorn entry](edible-plants/entries/acorn-oak.md)). Hen-of-the-woods starts (September to November). *Galerina* is most reported September to November: [amatoxin entry](hazards/entries/amatoxin-mushrooms-amanita-galerina.md). Squirrel season opens in September (Pennsylvania and West Virginia, September 12 in 2026); archery deer opens late September or early October. Winter squash and other crops are harvested in September or October.

**October** — **First fall frost averages October 15 to 25**: dig sweet potatoes before it, and harvest winter squash (cure all except acorn and delicata; discard any bitter squash) and potatoes (harvest after the plants die back and before a hard frost). Black walnut nuts fall (October; hull and dry them; throw out moldy ones). Garlic goes in (Virginia October 1 to 30; Maryland and Pennsylvania mid to late October). Fall mushrooms continue. Rabbit season opens (Pennsylvania October 17 in 2026); fall turkey and raccoon seasons open in places. State seasons change every year and none of the entries offers a survival exemption: [`game-animals/_index.md`](game-animals/_index.md). Not legal advice.

**November** — Acorn season ends (to November). Hen-of-the-woods and lion's mane run to November. Deer firearms season starts late in the month in places (West Virginia buck firearms November 23 to December 6 in 2026). Raccoon and opossum seasons run in places (Virginia hunting October 15 to March 10; Virginia trapping November 15 to February 28). Oyster (*P. ostreatus*) starts in late fall.

**December–February** — **Lean season.** Live on stored and preserved food from the crop entries (winter squash up to 3 to 6 months by type; garlic about 3 to 6 months for hardneck). The wild-plant entries give no winter window. Small game seasons run to February (squirrel, rabbit) or late February (raccoon, opossum; Virginia hunting runs to March 10). *P. ostreatus* oyster fruits late fall to early spring. Conserve energy: the Rule of Threes (`../../substrate/01_core_principles/_index.md`) says calories spent must be earned.
